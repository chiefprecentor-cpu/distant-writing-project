# Player Camera Position: Suspended Player-Embodiment Lens

## Status and Scope

This is the author's currently confirmed working principle for the player's camera position and presentation. It constrains how the work organises shots, address, responses, and choices for the player, but does not confirm that there is a "player" with a fixed ontological identity inside the work's world.

"Suspended player-embodiment lens" is the current working shorthand. More precisely, the embodied bearer, perceptual position, and addressee of the lens presented to the player can remain uncertain for a long time.

This file can be used independently inside Writer OS; its creative status is explained by this file and related internal links, while specific changes are preserved by Git history.

## Separate from Runtime LLM

- The current work's player faces text and branches that have been written, edited, and arranged in advance, and does not converse with a real-time running LLM during play.
- Interface or other characters appearing on screen as if they can respond to the player does not mean that an instant answer-generating model exists behind them; such responses can still be formed by prewritten text, choice relations, and presentation order.
- This production boundary only explains how the text is generated. It does not resolve the player's ontology inside the work's world, the bearer of the lens, or whether a given response is received by a character. For the project-level scope, see [Project Brief](../../docs/project_brief.md).

## Presentation Order π

`π` denotes the presentation order in which the player actually encounters, rewatches, and reinterprets content. It can arrange:

- which worldline or record the player sees first;
- when a setting detail only needs to be understood;
- how the same shot gains different meaning because of prior viewing experience;
- when the player shifts from watching toward possible response.

`π` is not a physical dimension, and does not automatically equal the meta-time `τ` experienced by the terminal AI. The player's choice to view or arrange content within `π` does not automatically constitute causal intervention in `τ`.

## In-World Enumeration Can Explain Traversal, but Does Not Define the Player in Advance

An earlier presentation candidate imagined using the terminal AI's real enumeration to provide an in-world explanation for multiple playthroughs, branches, and restarts in the visual novel. This idea has not yet become a confirmed mechanism, but it can be retained as a presentation resource: the player's seeing multiple worldlines does not have to be only a media convention, and may gradually be understood as a structure left by real enumeration within the work.

However, "multiple routes have an in-world reason" and "the real player is the terminal AI carrying out the enumeration" are two propositions. The latter proposition is not currently adopted:

- Saves, loads, rewatching, or selecting routes can first change only `π`; they cannot on that basis directly prove that the player is creating, discarding, or taking over worldlines along `τ`;
- the terminal AI's enumeration can become a content-layer reason for the nonlinear structure, while the player is still only watching and arranging their own understanding;
- the work can later gradually establish a participatory relation between player and enumeration locally, but it needs to make clear who received what, which operation truly entered in-work causality, and which consequences the player knows about;
- whether to explain the reason for traversal directly in the prologue, gradually reveal it in the middle or later sections, or only let the player infer it from repeated structures remains a concrete presentation issue for the narrative stage.

Therefore, in-world explanation does not automatically cancel the "suspended player-embodiment lens". It explains why the work may contain multiple traversals; it does not explain in advance who exactly is doing the traversing.

## Relations That Must Remain Separate

- Seeing a piece of content does not equal obtaining in-work authorisation to view it.
- Being struck by a sentence's "you" does not mean the work has already confirmed that the real player is the in-work recipient.
- Being able to respond does not mean the response must be received by a character.
- Being perceived by a character does not mean being accurately identified by that character.
- Being remembered does not equal obtaining authorisation to act.
- Obtaining authorisation does not equal being able to control the result.
- A menu changing the presentation path does not automatically mean the menu changed the worldline.

## Does Not Presuppose Real-Player Cooperation

The work does not presuppose that the real player will necessarily read patiently, accept intimate relationships, identify with the on-screen "you", or cooperate with the position the work assigns them. The player may click randomly, skip text, save and load, read a walkthrough first, refuse to choose, stop watching, or quit the game.

These real behaviours do not automatically become responses, refusals, or causal actions inside the work. The suspended embodiment lens can let the player, without being forcibly named, complete their own thinking about silence, refusing to answer, stopping watching, refusing to receive memory, and their own relation with characters and the world, even if only at a subconscious level; the work does not need to turn all of this thinking explicitly into menus.

If a later scene wants a character to receive, remember, or hold the player accountable for one refusal, or wants the player to bear in-work responsibility for its consequences, that scene still needs to establish input, reception, and consequence relations sufficient to support that attribution. It cannot retroactively assign responsibility for a prewritten result to the player simply because the real player once clicked or continued watching.

## Local Handling Principles

There is no need to determine a unified player ontology for the whole work in advance. When designing a specific narrative part, handle only the relations that part actually needs:

- what the player can see at this moment;
- whether the lens needs to be explicitly assigned to a certain character;
- whom the "you" in the work may point to;
- whether the player can respond, remain silent, or refuse;
- who can receive and remember a response;
- whether a choice changes presentation, character expression, or in-work causality;
- at most, which understandable consequences the player can be responsible for.

Mystery can be an active aesthetic design. Only when later knowledge, memory, responsibility, or causal consequences depend on some explanation does the scene need to fix relations sufficient to support that consequence.

Ordinary narrative design only performs consistency checks proportionate to the actual scene. It does not enumerate every counterexample or treat literary creation as a stress test.
