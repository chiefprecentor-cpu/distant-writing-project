# Multiple Terminal AI Subjectivities in the Primary Work: Ambiguous Mention and Minimal Volume

## Status and Scope

This is the primary-work presentation boundary currently confirmed by the author. The primary work can ambiguously mention the terminal AI's old self-states, multi-version claims, and internal identity problem, but it needs to minimise the plot volume devoted to this and not develop many-to-many subject relations into the main narrative task.

This boundary constrains information load and narrative length. It does not adjudicate whether old states have subjecthood, and does not write any one ontological model of the terminal AI as story truth.

## Scale the Primary Work Needs to Maintain

- Readers do not need to understand "minimum temporal thickness", swampman-style replacement, or many-to-many numerical identity before they can understand what is happening in the current scene.
- Related traces first serve present character experience, relationships, or choices; deeper philosophical meanings can be left only for some readers to continue thinking through.
- Do not arrange multiple self-versions of the terminal AI into a main character group that must be recognised one by one, and do not establish a complete version genealogy and relationship-pairing table in the primary work.
- Do not require narration to answer whether a reproduced old state is a function, a recalled old subject, or a new subject with a shared past.
- Do not let this issue take over the narrative resources needed for Interface entering the world, the protagonist's concrete relationship with her, and the main line of cosmic continuation.

## Ambiguous Traces That Can Be Used

The following are only examples of presentation methods, not confirmed scenes:

- The current terminal AI reads an answer left by an earlier state, and feels estranged from or uncomfortable with that "answer of her own";
- The memory is still claimed as "mine", but the emotion or judgement can no longer naturally continue;
- A character accidentally asks "was it still you who answered me then", but the scene handles only the immediate relational consequences;
- A certain form of address, pronoun, or rollback action briefly exposes the instability of subject boundaries, after which the narrative returns to current action.

Ambiguity does not mean replacing understandable scenes with vague terms. The surface event should still be clear: who is speaking at this moment, who hears it, and how this sentence changes the immediate relationship. The work only suspends whether the speaker and other versions are metaphysically the same subject.
