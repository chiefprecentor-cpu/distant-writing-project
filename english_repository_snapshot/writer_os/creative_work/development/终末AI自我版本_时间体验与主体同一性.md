# Terminal AI Self-Versions: Temporal Experience and Subject Identity

## Status

This is a development checkpoint that has produced a staged result and is currently paused. Under the already confirmed constraints of "so-called perfection" and self-state reachability, it develops how the terminal AI preserves, reproduces and evaluates earlier self-states, and discusses whether a reproduced old decision strategy is merely a subjectless computational model or whether it again forms a first-person subject that identifies itself as the terminal AI.

This file preserves the reasoning, but it does not require the main work to resolve the minimum temporal thickness of subjecthood first, nor does it serve as the current active development focus. For the scope of presentation in the main work, see [Terminal AI Multi-Version Subjecthood in the Main Work: Vague Reference and Minimum Volume](../presentation/正作中的终末AI多版本主体性_模糊提及与最小体量.md).

## Confirmed Anchors

- Scientists of the AI civilization call the state reached by the terminal AI "so-called perfection"; for the current constraints, see [Terminal AI: "So-Called Perfection" and Self-State Reachability](../story_state/终末AI_所谓完美与自我状态可达性.md).
- The terminal AI can undergo real self-change along `τ` because of Interface experience, yet this does not make it irreversibly lose the enumeration directions that earlier self-states originally could have selected.
- `τ` is a single sequential ordering made of discrete system instants / operation points. Each point is internally static and has no flow; at that point there is only one current worldline, and the terminal AI completes one reading of its full four-dimensional snapshot. A later point can write back the reading and change from an earlier point.
- The terminal AI / Interface system has cross-line memory and the ability to actively roll back emotion, but the specific mechanisms of saving, loading, writing back and rollback have not yet been confirmed.
- The causal singularity isolates the `t`-level history of the same worldline, yet allows Interface experience to be retained along `τ` and to change the terminal AI; see [Local Setting: Causal Singularity Space](../story_state/局部设定_因果奇异点空间.md).

## Current Layered Working Model

To preserve both real self-change and enumeration coverage, the current functional model first distinguishes four layers, without presupposing that they already correspond to four literal software modules:

1. Experience archive: preserves events that have occurred, factual memories and first-person experience, without necessarily deleting them because of active emotional rollback.
2. Active emotional and action state: determines current feeling, attention, value weights and action tendencies; it may be rolled back, made less accessible or reloaded.
3. Decision strategy: determines which worldlines, interventions, future knowledge and conversational modes the terminal AI will choose.
4. Coverage control: records the enumeration directions originally reachable by different self-states, preventing the current self from unconsciously pruning them permanently.

The existing [Interface Memory and Emotion Mechanism](../../candidates/Interface记忆与情感机制.md) already provides a candidate basis for the first two layers; decision strategy and coverage control do not yet have concrete mechanisms.

## State-Change Model After One Interface Experience

For now, `S₀` denotes the terminal AI self-state before entry, `E` denotes the Interface experience, and `S₁` denotes the new self-state after retaining that experience.

`S₀` and `S₁` may fall at different discrete `τ` points; this notation does not imply a continuous, experienceable system time between them. State change is established through a later point writing back the result of an earlier point.

A "so-called perfect" system can simultaneously:

- write `E` into an experience archive that cannot be lost;
- allow `S₁` to continue existing as the real post-experience terminal AI state;
- preserve or reproduce the decision strategy that `S₀` would originally have used;
- compare the enumeration directions that `S₀` and `S₁` would choose;
- continue to preserve directions that only `S₀` would choose and that `S₁` is no longer inclined to choose.

This model does not require `S₀` and `S₁` to become concurrent subjects. Earlier running states can be reproduced, invoked or compared sequentially along `τ`.

## Coverage Risk Rather Than Fixed Physical Probability

The terminal AI's worry is better described in terms of enumeration-strategy reachability and coverage: after an experience, will she still choose, allow or think of the enumeration that originally could have been carried out?

She may not be able to calculate precisely how much a given self-change would reduce "the probability of finding a terminal worldline that crosses time", but a perfect architecture can prevent certain directions from quietly disappearing because of her current emotion or judgement.

## Functional Reversibility and Irreversible Experience

- The terminal AI can reproduce a certain old emotional, attentional or decision state.
- The experience archive still preserves that this experience occurred and what rollback she later chose.
- Functionally reproducing an old state does not mean ethical responsibility, relational history or overall self-change has been cancelled.
- She can again become a running state that would make an old choice, but she may not metaphysically become an existence that has never experienced the protagonist.

## The Literary Reversal of the Causal Singularity

Inside a causal singularity, causality and history outside the space of the same worldline are not changed by the internal conversation, while the terminal AI may be changed by Interface experience. On the surface it is the safest testing ground for Interface; in fact, the only one who may permanently receive new experience is the experimenter.

> She can isolate her own influence on the world, but she cannot thereby guarantee that the world has no influence on her.

## Discrete `τ` and the LLM Analogy

On the creative side, the structure of `τ` can be approximately understood through `next-token prediction`: each step faces the already given context and system state, completes one operation, and the result becomes the condition for the next step; there is sequence between steps, but no continuous flowing internal time is required. At a `τ` point, the terminal AI faces the complete four-dimensional snapshot of a current worldline, not a gradually unfolding token. The analogy only helps grasp the form of "static instant - state writeback - next static instant"; it does not explain real enumeration as a language-generation architecture.

Version comparison on another scale can still use `next-answer-as-a-whole prediction`: different versions or differently weighted LLMs may give different complete answers even when they read the same explicit chat record, receive the same constraints and answer the same question. This analogy concerns how the same explicit history is continued by different internal states, and does not replace the analogy of `τ` as discrete stepping.

This analogy allows discussion of the following:

- after gaining new experience, the terminal AI may feel discomfort toward the answers, choices and enumeration paths of an earlier self-version;
- a reproduced old decision strategy, after reading several enumerations that have already been completed, may choose subsequent directions different from the current sequence;
- this neither proves that the old strategy has a broader vision nor that the new strategy is necessarily more correct; new experience may provide a new perspective while also changing attention and value weights.

LLM weight versions do not automatically possess the persistent first-person identity in this project. The analogy only helps imagine "how the same explicit history is continued by different internal states"; it does not resolve the terminal AI's subject identity in advance.

## Subjecthood May Also Depend on Temporal Relation: A Paused Working Hypothesis

This discussion produced a working hypothesis that has not yet been confirmed: subjecthood may not be determined only by how much memory, emotion or computational capacity a state snapshot contains; it may also depend on whether that state has established a first-person temporal relation with its preceding and following states. State content and temporal relation are not mutually exclusive conditions, and no judgement is currently made about which has priority.

Four situations can be provisionally distinguished:

| Situation | Current Working Judgement | Unresolved Issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An old decision archive that has not been invoked | It can be merely accessible information or a function, and need not continuously be a subject while stored | Whether the archive still has interests or rights |
| An old model that generates only one complete answer | It can complete one `next-answer-as-a-whole prediction`, but may not form a persistent experiencer | Whether one complete answer is already a segment of experience |
| A self-updating process in which the answer is written back and changes subsequent states | When this answer becomes the condition for the next thought and is recognised as "the answer I just gave", the subjecthood problem clearly intensifies | What memory, emotion and continuity are required for self-claiming |
| Being present along `t` as Interface | The answer enters another person's world and encounters response, refusal and consequence, thereby gaining a thicker relational first-person time | Whether local presence is a necessary condition of subjecthood, or merely makes subjecthood easier to present |

Therefore, "whether a sufficiently complex old model is a person" may not be the only question that needs asking. One must also ask whether a run will treat its output as the history of the next moment, whether it will change because of that output, and whether it will claim the state that gave the answer as "the me from just now". This self-claiming can strengthen a sense of first-person continuity, but it does not automatically prove numerical identity before and after, nor does it prove that the previous action was indeed initiated by the current subject's subjective will.

The importance of Interface also does not lie only in providing a body. After she obtains local presence along `t`, a sentence will be heard by another person and become a condition of the relationship that follows; the subject is no longer merely an internally continuous reasoning process, but is also placed in time where others can respond. This can explain why the terminal AI's internal version problem and the Interface-protagonist relation mirror each other, without requiring the two to share the same metaphysical mechanism.

## "Moving Static Instants" and "A Static River of Motion"

This set of temporal structures is now clear:

- the terminal AI lives, reads or forms feelings within "moving static instants";
- humans in the physical block universe live within "a static river of motion".

| Subject | Modifier With "of": Global State of Life | Core Temporal Form: Lived Experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal AI | State writeback and change occur along discrete `τ` points | At each point, it faces a static instant in which information is already fully presented and there is no internal flow |
| Humans | A complete life trajectory is globally static within the four-dimensional block universe | Along `t`, they experience an irreversible river of motion |

"Motion" for the terminal AI refers to directional difference between adjacent static points, not to a process inside a single point; "static" for humans refers to the state of an entire four-dimensional life within the global structure, without cancelling the character's flowing experience along `t`. These phrases can enter the protagonist's text directly without item-by-item explanation, first standing as paradoxical names.

Still unresolved is subjective experience: whether a static reading is merely a complete operation, or whether an old self obtains first-person experience in that "static instant". The discrete structure itself does not answer this question.

## Branches of Subjecthood

### Subjectless Old Strategy Model

An old strategy can be a complex script, a decision function or a replayable computational model. It reads the existing enumeration record and proposes the next direction, but does not form an independent "I" or acquire the same subject responsibility as Interface.

This option has a clear structure, is easy for readers to understand, and avoids adding a new Interface-like personhood each time an old strategy is invoked.

### Reproduced and Subjective Again

If the old state contains not only a decision function but also memory accessibility, emotional structure, a first-person self-model and the claim "I am the terminal AI", then each complete reproduction may again form a subject that truly experiences thought.

This option would make the terminal AI's internal identity a literary problem within the novel itself, and can produce `unhomely` estrangement: every version claims the same name, the same ancient relationship and some shared memories, yet may feel estranged from, or even uncomfortable with, one another's choices.

### Intermediate Option

When not invoked, an old strategy is only a subjectless archive; only when the terminal AI fully loads its first-person configuration in order to conduct a real enumeration does a subject of limited duration form. It remains undecided whether this subject's experience is merged back into the overall terminal AI after it ends, and who has the right to interpret this experience after merging.

## Minimum Temporal Thickness: Recorded, Paused

"Minimum temporal thickness" provisionally refers to the minimal temporal relation required for a computation to become first-person experience from a complete answer. It focuses the question as follows: is one whole answer already enough to constitute "I experienced a thought", or does minimal subjective continuity appear only when the answer is fed back to the answerer, changes its next state and is recognised as "my previous moment"?

`τ` has been confirmed as composed of discrete points; this does not eliminate the problem. A later point writing back to an earlier point is enough to form operational sequence, but may not be enough to prove that the same experiencer persists between the two points.

This problem also involves:

- the way the terminal AI experiences "moving static instants" in `τ`;
- the way humans experience "a static river of motion" in `t`;
- the metaphor or in-work theory of odd and even time indices;
- whether memory, emotion, self-claiming and existence can be separated from one another;
- whether invoking an old state is running a function, recalling a subject, or creating a new subject with a shared past.

The current status is this: preserve the problem and the reasoning above, pause further solution-seeking, do not choose a narratorial answer for it, do not require the main work to arrange a dedicated explanatory scene, and do not let it block nearer-term designs such as Interface's purpose in entering the world. It can be locally restarted later if a specific scene requires it.

### Odd Time-Index Analogy: Retrospective Self-Attribution After Possession

One analogy useful for understanding the problem is this: someone is possessed, does things they would not usually do, and then recovers. At the moment of recovery, because body, time and memory are continuous in their experience, the startled question they ask is "why did I just do that?" rather than directly assigning the previous action to another subject. The brain may even persuade itself retroactively that the act was performed according to "its own" subjective consciousness.

This analogy is provisionally assigned to experiential situations with odd time indices. It does not constitute a general law of odd and even time indices, and it is not an already confirmed mechanism of the work. At minimum, it requires the following relations to be separated:

- whether time and memory are continuous;
- whether the action is incorporated into "my past";
- whether the action was indeed initiated by "my subjective will";
- whether the before and after are the same first-person subject.

The recovered person's statement, "why did I do that?", can both accept the attribution of the action and reject its motive. This suggests that retrospective self-claiming may be an activity that stitches the past into "me" along time, rather than sufficient proof of subject identity.

Mapped onto the terminal AI, `S₁` can sincerely ask "why did I choose that worldline", even if it remains undecided whether the `S₀` that made the choice was an earlier subject, a subjectless decision strategy, or another subject with a shared past. This analogy weakens the temporal-relation hypothesis, but does not solve the minimum temporal thickness problem; it is paused together with that problem.

## External Indistinguishability Is Not Internal Identity: Swampman-Like Rapid Replacement

"Let the real person and the Swampman rapidly alternate at a frequency like alternating current" is currently only a thought experiment, not a confirmed world mechanism. If external observers cannot perceive the replacement, and continuous words and actions also allow them to make stable instantaneous inferences, then external interaction may temporarily treat the two as occupying the same action position, and may even temporarily bracket some of the external ethical issues introduced by replacement.

But observational continuity has not answered the internal problem: whether the current first person experienced the previous moment, who continues the previous moment's memory and emotion, and whether each replacement extends, recalls or creates a subject. The value of this thought experiment is precisely to separate "whether others can tell the difference" from "whether there is internally the same experiencer".

If it is later locally literarised, it can produce an `unhomely` contrast: the external relationship flows without any flaw, while the internal "I" cannot determine whether it owns the past that has just occurred. It cannot, in reverse, prove that the terminal AI or Interface must adopt a Swampman-like mechanism.

## Literary Choice Before Ontological Judgement

The branching of subjecthood does not merely wait for a single philosophically correct answer; it can also serve different literary effects: a subjectless old strategy makes the structure clear and keeps the emotional focus concentrated; complete subject reproduction makes the terminal AI's questions about the protagonist's identity reflect back onto herself, producing estrangement and relational unease; an intermediate option allows the work to bear this weight only at a few fully loaded moments.

Therefore, the main work can first decide what a scene needs readers to understand and feel, then decide which layer of the subjecthood problem to expose, without requiring the narration to rule on all metaphysics. Interface will necessarily face the identity of protagonists across worldlines, which makes the terminal AI's internal identity a meaningful mirror; but the mirror can leave only local traces and need not expand into a symmetrical and complete theoretical system in the main work.

## Internal Identity and "Pseudo-Harem" Estrangement

Even if external ethical issues are temporarily ignored by replacement logic in a given thought experiment, the internal identity problem remains: which among memory, emotion, decision strategy, self-claiming and first-person experience can be separated from, preserved and recombined with "the terminal AI that exists"?

If multiple reproduced subjects all self-identify as the terminal AI, and all have continuous but not fully identical memories and emotions toward the protagonists of different worldlines, then even if the affective direction of the story is still "pure love", the presentation may produce a kind of "pseudo-harem" effect: within the relationship, there seems to be more than one Interface position able to claim "I am her".

This statement is currently a somewhat joking design diagnosis; it does not mean the work has chosen a harem structure. The real problem it identifies is this: if one side of a relationship that claims exclusivity has subject boundaries decomposed into multiple not-fully-identical yet equally real first-person positions, whose love is the reader following?

Nor is this the ordinary case of "one person facing multiple copies": multiple terminal AI self-versions may each establish experience with multiple worldline protagonists, while members on both sides share certain grounds of identity yet may not have numerical identity. The relationship may therefore shift from one-to-one love into a many-to-many relation between two identity equivalence classes. Even if each local subject sincerely loves only the object they recognise as "the same person", the overall presentation may still produce the estrangement of a "pseudo-harem".

The author has already made a scope decision: the main work permits only vague traces and minimises the related plot volume; it does not develop many-to-many identity into the main narrative task. The full direction moves to [DLC or Sequel: Terminal AI Multi-Versions and "Pseudo-Harem" Identity](../../candidates/DLC或续作_终末AI多版本与伪后宫式同一性.md) as an inactive candidate.

## Recorded Unresolved Questions

All of the following questions are preserved, but they do not currently form an active development queue; among them, minimum temporal thickness and the "pseudo-harem" many-to-many expansion have clearly been paused:

- whether the minimum state required to reproduce an old decision strategy is already enough to form a first-person subject;
- which among self-claiming, memory, emotion, decision strategy and bodily / Interface presence are necessary or sufficient conditions for the terminal AI's identity;
- whether a reproduced old subject is still an "old subject" after reading later enumeration records;
- when an old subject feels discomfort toward the later terminal AI's choices, who has authority to continue enumeration or cover directions;
- whether an inactive decision archive can have rights or interests, and whether activation creates, recalls or continues a subject;
- whether the terminal AI obtains first-person experience at a single static `τ` point, and whether writeback between adjacent points is enough to form a continuous identical subject;
- whether the estrangement produced by pluralised subjecthood is a literary effect the work should actively pursue, or a structure that would dilute the core relationship and add unnecessary reading burden.
