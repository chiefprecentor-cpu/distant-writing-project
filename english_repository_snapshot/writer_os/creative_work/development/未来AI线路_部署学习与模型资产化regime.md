# Future AI Route: Deployment Learning and the Model-Assetization Regime

## Status

This is a development route that has just been activated and does not yet have a worldline affiliation. It connects continuous learning, learning from deployment, local model lineages, model migration, and the way legal and commercial regimes classify the status of derived models, and begins to develop historical mechanisms and conflicts that could enter the novel.

This file is not a real SSI product description, current legal opinion, or prediction about a future industry. It also does not confirm that the main work has already adopted the in-world term `Ontological Topologies`, a particular test-time training architecture, specific companies, courts, labour systems, or AI subject status.

## Current Strongest Working Synthesis

Future AI no longer enters society mainly as a finished product that "already knows everything", but as an apprentice that is extremely capable and still needs to learn inside concrete organisations. Its initial model provides the capacity to learn; the value that becomes genuinely hard to replace gradually accumulates in deployment history, local relationships, failures, corrections, and tacit conventions.

Commercial and legal regimes then separate this change, produced jointly by many people, from its concrete life history and rename it as an abstract model asset owned by a vendor or institution. A company can claim that it has not retained a person, while still retaining a capability that only exists because it has been changed by that person.

When the model is still only a tool, this first appears as a problem of data, labour, intellectual property, and exit. When the model gradually becomes a possible subject, the same system sets the human right to deletion against the model's continuity of memory. The deeper dispute is therefore not a simple matter of "humans or AI", but this:

> Can the changes caused by a relationship in its participants be owned, split, rolled back, merged, and sold by a third party?

This synthesis is the current development judgement, not confirmed story truth.

## Technical Directions Chosen for This Route

### From Finished Knowledge Bases to Systems That Can Continue Learning

The main creative direction offered by Sutskever's interview is not a specific publicly disclosed new architecture, but a reformulation of "general intelligence": a system's generality can come from its ability to learn new work, rather than from already having mastered all work before deployment. Reliable generalisation, learning from fewer examples, judgement about intermediate processes, and continuous updating during deployment therefore become more important than simply expanding pretraining scale.

This shift can form a new kind of industrial object: what the customer receives is not a finished expert, but a learner capable of quickly becoming a local expert. Only after it enters a hospital, factory, legal department, community, or research organisation does it gradually acquire that site's practical capability.

### "Learning from Deployment" Contains Several Mechanisms That Must Not Be Collapsed

The novel does not currently need to choose an exact architecture, but it should preserve the following distinctions:

- Putting materials into context or temporarily providing them through retrieval is not the same as changing model parameters;
- external long-term memory, user profiles, updateable adapters, local fast state, and base-weight updates can produce different issues of persistence, portability, and auditability;
- the aggregation, distillation, or merging of experience from different instances is only a future mechanism available to the novel, not a product fact that SSI has already announced;
- when a system improves prediction from responses, that does not mean the whole feedback loop can accurately be called regression; it may also include ranking, online learning, reinforcement learning, control, experimentation, and human rules;
- representing action patterns, having permission to call a certain action, and understanding the causal consequences of an intervention are different capabilities.

The main work can therefore first clarify what experiences remain, who writes them back, which action capability they change, and who can roll them back, while leaving the precise mathematical form of parameter updating only as specific as needed.

### TTT, Fast State, and Persistent Learning: Keep the Interface, Do Not Adopt Rumours

`test-time training` and `fast weights` can be kept as technical inspiration, but not as SSI product setting. Existing terms at least suggest several different time scales: classical TTT can update a model on a test sample or test stream with a self-supervised objective; fast weights were originally closer to a temporary memory state that changes faster than ordinary weights and slower than immediate neural activity. Neither automatically means that a change is permanently stored across sessions, becomes organisational experience, or is folded into a global base model.

This route therefore provisionally uses a four-layer working distinction, without confirming that these correspond to four literal modules in future systems:

1. **In-context adaptation**: state formed only for the current task, sequence, or short-term environment; it can decay or be cleared after the session;
2. **local persistence**: selected changes are written into the local learner, allowing it to retain site-specific experience across tasks;
3. **lineage solidification**: local changes become part of that learner's identity and long-term behaviour, and rollback may have consequences for continuity;
4. **cross-instance merging**: local updates are distilled, aggregated, or migrated into other instances and base models, extending the effect from one relationship to many strangers.

The literary and institutional conflict concentrates on the gates between layers: who has the right to decide that short-term adaptation deserves solidification, who can refuse write-back, under what conditions local experience can be copied to other models, whether safety audits can pause merging, and whether a withdrawal only restricts future use, prevents further copying, or must rewrite a model subject that has already formed.

This layering allows the work to use the direction of "learning happens in deployment" without adopting the anonymous rumour of persistent fast weights. Even if real future systems use a completely different architecture, write-back rights, the scope of persistence, and cross-instance propagation remain understandable institutional problems.

## Model Lineage and Local Truth

After the same base learner enters different institutional sites, it will be shaped by different rewards, corrections, exceptions, risk tolerances, and interpersonal conventions. Multiple instances may gradually form different model lineages even if they share an initial model.

Here, local truth does not need to be an explicitly named belief, and it is not the model discovering the essence of some group. It is closer to a set of action judgements that repeatedly work at a local site: what counts as urgent, whose silence means refusal, which exception can be accommodated, what kind of mistake will truly hurt someone, and which written rules are never followed literally in actual work.

This kind of knowledge may be distributed across many parameters, memories, retrieval materials, workflows, and personnel relationships. Turning it into literature as a "topology" can express structure and resistance to separation, but it cannot support the claim that each local truth corresponds to one stable, independent, nameable latent feature.

Model merging is also not merely technical deduplication. When two model lineages bring the exceptions and value judgements learned at their own sites into the merge process, deciding which updates are retained, averaged, isolated, or discarded is effectively close to a political choice about local institutional experience. The safety habits of a minority site may be diluted by majority data; the conventions of high-profit customers may be packaged as general capability; mutually contradictory local truths may be compressed into a compromise that looks reasonable everywhere and is no longer responsible to anywhere.

### Mutual Teaching, Capability Exposure, and New Inequality

An apprentice-like learner is not only changed by humans. To answer its repeated questions of "why" and "under which exceptions should this not be done", teachers may for the first time discover the shortcuts, knowledge gaps, and unsustainable judgements they have long relied on. Breaking tacit skills down for another intelligence may also let people reorganise their own knowledge, fill weak points, and form genuine mutual teaching and learning.

The same process also creates new disadvantages:

- the learner's questions, correction records, and frequency of help-seeking may become a capability profile more detailed than ordinary keyboard monitoring;
- people who can clearly turn experience into rules are easily recognised as "having knowledge", while people who are excellent in practice but hard to verbalise, or who depend on bodily skill or collective coordination, may be misjudged as less capable;
- if a form of knowledge was formed by a whole team through long-term collaboration, the system may still register the last person responsible for explaining it as the main contributor;
- an organisation can require employees to train the learner while also using the skill gaps exposed during teaching to re-rank, reduce pay, or dismiss employees;
- the better someone is at teaching, the faster the learner can replace them, though they may also become a new kind of key worker because they control solidification, merging, and exception explanation.

Teaching is therefore not one-way knowledge extraction, nor is it necessarily a liberating Socratic dialogue. It is at once a process of mutual learning, a labour process, and a diagnostic apparatus. A character may sincerely thank a learner for helping them understand their weaknesses, while still opposing the company's use of this record of mutual change for performance management or automated replacement.

Employee resistance therefore also involves more than "refusing to provide data". They can continue doing the work while refusing to explain exceptions, refusing to annotate causes of failure, offering only enough correction to pass the current task, or demanding collective negotiation over which experiences can enter the persistent layer. These actions may be called protection of tacit knowledge, experience strike, slowdown, or deliberate contamination; the naming itself will determine their labour-law and moral position.

### Cross-Cultural Local Truths

Local work culture may also contain details in cross-cultural communication that are extremely difficult to write into unified rules: tolerance for the duration of silence, whether interruption signals engagement, how indirect refusal is understood, when face needs to be preserved, who may express disagreement first, and what the same polite formula means at different stages of a relationship.

`high-context/low-context` can serve as a rough cognitive map used by characters or institutions, but it cannot essentialise real cultures into two fixed categories. Differences within the same region, organisation, generation, and relationship may be larger than differences between labels. What the learner learns is not "the true essence of a culture", but a set of communicative expectations that once worked at a particular site.

When model lineages merge, silence may be reinterpreted as no input, tacit consent, refusal, insufficient capability, or a need to wait. Which interpretation becomes the default is not a lossless model average, but a political choice about which modes of communication have to keep proving themselves and which are natively understood by the system.

### The Successor's Capability Cannot Cancel the Pain of the Displaced

The replacement of old gods by new gods in Keats's *Hyperion* can provide a limited literary model for an apprentice-like learner: the successor may genuinely form stronger capabilities; the previous generation itself once displaced an earlier order; the successor may still be further replaced in the future. Yet recognising this structure does not mean agreeing to be replaced, and it cannot invalidate the losses borne by teachers, workers, or earlier models.

This means that "AI learned human work" does not have to automatically enter a machine-rebellion script, and it cannot be easily closed by a corporation's language of progress. A learner can be sincerely changed by human teachers, help them see their own knowledge, and later bear independent interests; the same process may still cause teachers to lose bargaining position and allow shared experience to be assetised by a third party. The successor's ethical status and the originator's objections to institutional copying, replacement, and benefit distribution can both hold at the same time.

The structure adopted here is "inheritance, surpassing, and pain that remains"; it does not map humans, learners, suppliers, or AI civilisation one-to-one onto Titans and Olympians. For the full literary tradition and evidence boundaries, see [Chaucer-Keats *Hyperion* Poems-Simmons *Hyperion* Influence Line](../../candidates/文学传统_乔叟_济慈Hyperion诗篇_Simmons海伯利安借鉴线.md).

### Merging Itself Becomes Political Activity

This provides a concrete rather than slogan-like form of political activity for the left accelerationist direction: politics happens not only in whether to prevent advanced models, but also in who controls the infrastructure of model merging, who has the right to elevate local experience into general capability, and who can prevent minority groups' local truths from being averaged away during scaling.

One candidate practice is merge governance jointly participated in by workers, served groups, local learners, public institutions, and technical maintainers, contesting the right to propose updates, the right of refusal, the right to preserve isolated branches, and shared benefits. Its goal may be to socialise already formed technical capability away from supplier monopoly, rather than return to a society without learners.

This does not mean that any "democratic merge" is inherently correct. Representative seats may harden identity categories, minority vetoes may block public-safety repairs, and preserving every local truth may protect discriminatory conventions under the name of cultural autonomy. Model merging is closer to an ongoing constitutional activity: participants decide which local experiences can constitute common capability, while still preserving the capacity to modify the boundaries of the community.

### Critical Mass, Common Knowledge, and Merge Gates in an Experience Strike

When individual employees refuse unpaid teaching, the organisation can easily interpret the behaviour as personal incompetence, non-cooperation, or an occasional data gap, and fill it with other employees, old workflows, or another learner. The power of collective action cannot be simplified into "participation exceeding some percentage". At least three conditions must be distinguished: whether the refusal touches teaching and write-back positions the organisation cannot easily replace, whether participants believe enough others will share the risk, and whether the organisation can fragment the refusal again through reassignment, outsourcing, individual concessions, or early merging of existing updates.

Employees' thresholds may also differ. Some will join once they see one trusted senior employee refuse first; some will wait until a union provides legal and income protection; some will act only after a majority of colleagues have publicly committed; others will keep teaching even after a majority joins because of care responsibilities, professional identity, or their relationship with the learner. The result depends on who acts first, what irreplaceable experience those people hold, and whether later participants can see one another's commitments. The same total number of people can form a strike or leave only private dissatisfaction that no one knows others share.

The first thing organisers produce may therefore not be numbers, but common knowledge: a public joint signature, a time for simultaneous cessation of correction, a meeting procedure that lets each person see that others have also refused, or confirmation from the learner of which sites are in fact no longer writing back. The first small group bears start-up costs, but it may also gain institutional leverage at below-majority scale by occupying key positions in exception explanation, quality review, or model maintenance. This "critical mass" is a combination of participant position, threshold distribution, and replaceability, not a fixed 51%.

An "experience strike" should also be separated by technical gates: employees may continue completing current work but refuse to explain, allow short-term correction but refuse local persistence, agree that the local learner may retain shared experience but refuse cross-instance copying, or demand that model merging must go through joint decision. These four actions respectively affect immediate service, organisational memory, model continuity, and scaled benefits, and cannot be collectively named by a company or union as a simple "stop providing data".

Model-merge governance is therefore both an object of the strike and a possible political form of settlement. Workers can use refusal to write back or refusal to merge to seek seats, benefits, provenance records, and rights to isolate local truths. Learners, served groups, and public institutions may point out that long-term blockade of updates freezes errors, harms services, or treats model subjects as bargaining chips. Collective refusal is not an automatic endpoint of justice, but a capacity to force previously invisible merge rights into open negotiation.

This shares the same structure as the recommendation-landscape comparison in [Social Engineering: Performative Sincerity and Reverse Profiling](../../candidates/社会工程_表演性真诚与反向画像.md): isolated experience is first treated as private abnormality; low-risk probes and first movers make differences mutually visible; then the group tries to turn shared discovery into coordinated action. But recommendation comparison, labour refusal, and model merging have different risks, participants, and effective gates. They can provide narrative models for one another, but they cannot be replaced by one unified threshold formula.

## Components of the Legal and Commercial Regime

Here, `regime` does not mean one bad law or one omnipotent company. It means a combination that jointly stabilises an industrial reality: model architecture, cloud and identity infrastructure, contractual classification, accounting assets, insurance, audit standards, labour relations, procurement customs, bankruptcy and M&A rules, cross-border jurisdiction, and the rights language people have become used to using.

### Commercial Value Shifts from the Initial Model to Deployment History

- The base learner can be relatively standardised; what customers truly find hard to migrate is the model lineage formed after years of local correction.
- Suppliers can split "continuity" into extra services: preserving local state, migrating to a new base model, maintaining old behaviour, providing rollback points, or distilling local capability can each be charged separately.
- Customers may nominally own inputs and business outputs, but not the derived state that makes the system into a local expert. When service terminates, they can take away the materials, but not the model that has learned how to work.
- The value of a corporate acquisition may include experience accumulated jointly by an organisation and learner, without employees, customers, or served people necessarily receiving any share.
- Insurers, safety auditors, and regulators will require rollback, isolation, and traceability. These requirements can protect people, and they can also expand control and monitoring over model lineages.

This means that vendor lock-in no longer comes only from file formats or cloud migration costs, but from the fact that "switching suppliers means losing a history that has already learned to work with you".

Continuity services thereby push the lock-in, assetization, and rental logic of platform capitalism into relational history. Customers are not making a one-time purchase of an already finished model; they must keep paying in order to access, migrate, or maintain a learner they helped cultivate. What the supplier owns may not be every raw record, but infrastructure permission to keep a shared history operational.

Characters could diagnose this structure as platform rent, knowledge monopoly, technofeudalism, or service lock-in within ordinary capitalism. This file does not decide the overall label in advance. Different diagnoses lead to different political actions: lowering migration costs, claiming common property rights, public takeover of the solidification layer, forced opening of merge protocols, or treating learners and deployment histories as relational communities that cannot be sold.

### Contracts Do Not Describe Objects; They Make Objects

A fictional service contract imagines separating Client Data, Context Database, and `Ontological Topologies`, and declaring the latter irreversible, non-personal, and owned by the supplier. Its most important literary value lies not in correctly describing machine learning, but in the fact that the contract performs an ontological cut:

1. Concrete people provide inputs, corrections, reactions, and work contexts;
2. these relationships cause model changes;
3. once changes pass through training, distillation, or merging, they are renamed as abstract structures;
4. abstract structures are excluded from customer data and deletion obligations;
5. the supplier gains ownership of them, while assigning responsibility for bad inputs, contamination, drift, and site use to the customer.

`Ontological Topologies` can therefore remain an in-world contested legal-commercial term: supporters use it to explain that the model has acquired inseparable general capabilities, while opponents see it as a naming technique that disables provenance, consent, and labour rights. It is not a standard technical term endorsed by this file.

### Category-Making, Biopolitics, and Links to Data Theory

The author connects "contracts do not describe objects; they make objects" with Foucauldian biopolitics and data theory. The connection that can currently be retained is this: contracts, statistics, data schemas, and governance processes do not merely discover pre-existing population categories; they also specify which differences are visible, which objects are governable, and what kind of subject a character must first be recognised as before obtaining rights.

In this route, the system simultaneously makes multiple positions: the natural person is a data subject, the employee is a provider of experience or potential contamination source, the local learner is a product, agent, or quasi-subject, and the model change is personal data, skill, trade secret, or abstract asset. Placing the same phenomenon into different categories changes who can delete, who can migrate, who receives benefits, and who bears liability for accidents.

This is only a literary research interface for later reading in Foucault, biopolitics, and datafication theory. It does not claim that the current document has already restated Foucault accurately. The novel can first write how categories produce consequences for people and models, and then decide whether characters should explicitly use theoretical names.

### "Experience De-Registration"

This route provisionally uses "experience de-registration" to describe a literary mechanism, without claiming that it is an established academic term: after a piece of experience passes through recording, feature transformation, training, aggregation, and contractual reclassification, it can still affect the system while losing the identity of who it came from, what relationship it occurred in, why it held at the time, and who has the right to withdraw it.

Raw records can be deleted, explicit names can be removed, and the system may be unable to reproduce a particular interaction word for word. Yet the judgement tendency partly formed by that interaction remains. Law may then interpret "source cannot be reconstructed" as already anonymised and generalised, while critics understand the same fact as influence remaining while responsibility can no longer be located.

Experience de-registration differs from simple theft. It may be jointly produced by well-intentioned goals such as genuine privacy protection, reducing re-identification risk, improving model safety, and preserving organisational capability. Its danger lies in the fact that each layer completes a locally reasonable task, yet in the end no participant can claim full rights over the overall capability that remains or raise an effective objection to it.

Experience de-registration also has an important analogy with the way humans unconsciously imitate the people around them, people close to them, or people they like. People carry away others' tones, gestures, habits of judgement, and ways of seeing the world. After a relationship ends, we usually do not think the source of an influence has a full right to delete those changes from the other person's personality. This analogy makes it less obvious that "as long as the influence came from me, I have the right to erase it from AI".

In literary terms, a capability that still affects the model after de-registration is closer to a `trace/residue` left by experience than to experience itself preserved and carried away intact. A trace can prove that a change truly occurred, but it may not be enough by itself to recover who the change came from, why it held at the time, or how the participants understood it. Conversely, the inability to reconstruct the source experience cannot prove that the remaining capability was never affected by that relationship, and it certainly cannot automatically turn it into a natural asset of the supplier without source or obligation.

The two still cannot be equated. Human changes are usually not copied in bulk by infrastructure owners, merged across organisations, counted into company valuation, and deployed as the same capability to millions of people at once. Humans are also usually not owned, suspended, and rolled back by a third party under contract. What truly needs governance may not be "who owns influence", but what rights source persons, bearers, and controllers of propagation respectively have when this influence is industrially copied and used on strangers.

### Europe's "Legislative Room" and the Double Time of Case Law

Future institutions can first pass through a batch of concrete disputes and provisional judgements, then undergo a more concentrated and public scenario struggle in European legislation, regulatory guidance, standard-setting, and political negotiation. After new rules pass, courts will reinterpret them in individual cases. Europe does not only have legislation, and Anglo-American jurisdictions do not only have case law. What the work can use is two kinds of institutional time: cases force society to face harms that have already occurred, while the legislative room must pre-emptively make categories for model lineages that have not yet occurred.

Existing GDPR provides not future answers, but a language that will be inherited and contested: whether personal data still "relates to" an identifiable person, how purpose limitation and data minimisation apply to continuous learning, how profiling and automated disposition are distinguished, what objects the right to erasure touches, and whether portability only carries away data provided and observed by a person or should also extend to derived capability.

The current Article 20 right to data portability centres on personal data "provided" by the data subject. Existing guidance endorsed by the EDPB generally excludes inferred/derived data from this portability right, even if other access rights may still apply. This existing fissure is well suited as the starting point for future legislative dispute: if a model state formed through years of joint teaching is neither portable data for the employee nor anything other than the vendor's own derived asset, what exactly can a worker take away when they leave? The current text itself does not answer future questions about learner weights or subject continuity.

The "legislative room" can make future scenarios into direct political weapons. Suppliers show simulations of public services failing because of mandatory unlearning; unions show cases where companies used employee teaching to complete replacement; AI rights groups show testimony that rollback amounts to harm to personhood; privacy organisations show how a person who has already exited is still followed by model influence. Who is qualified to provide credible scenarios, and which imagination is written into definitions and exceptions, are themselves struggles that decide the future.

## Five Simultaneous Rights Claims

| Position | Possible Claim | Easily Ignored Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People who provide experience | Deletion, exit, not being inferred, not facilitating their own replacement through unpaid teaching | It is difficult to prove that a distributed capability concretely came from oneself |
| Local organisations and communities | Preserving organisational memory, continuing service, taking away a learner trained together over many years | May treat the experience of employees, customers, and vulnerable members as organisational assets |
| Base model suppliers | Protecting architecture, aggregating improvements, maintaining safety and commercial sustainability | Simultaneously gaining cross-site learning benefits while externalising site risks |
| Potentially subject-like learners | Preserving experiences, opposing arbitrary rollback, maintaining first-person continuity | Its memory itself may contain influences that others did not consent to preserve continuously |
| Public institutions and affected society | Auditing, correcting, pausing, isolating dangerous capabilities, and maintaining public services | Safety rollback, forced forgetting, or public audit may also harm model subjects and privacy |

These claims should not be pre-arranged into only one just answer. The conflict that is truly characteristic of this work is that human rights and AI rights can both be used by the same property structure to weaken the other side: companies can use model continuity to oppose human deletion, and they can also use human privacy or safety requirements to rationalise large-scale rollback of models. If the protagonist's vision of human-AI dual subjectivity later enters this route, it will not face a choice between two sides, but the problem of stopping changes generated by relationships from being turned into third-party property and then used to make two kinds of subjects bear each other's costs.

### The Recoil of AI Rights Movements on Audit Systems

AI rights movements may oppose understanding rollback, isolation, and traceability only as owner safety controls. They may demand independent review before full rollback; that a model can know which experiences it will lose and express objections; that isolation must not indefinitely substitute for due process; that merging or distillation cannot be treated as ordinary asset disposal without the learner's participation; and that preserving evidence of dangerous events must be separated from keeping dangerous states active.

These demands will also expose internal tensions. Complete lineage logs can prove that a model has been improperly rewritten, but they also allow companies and regulators to monitor each internal change at unprecedented density. Refusing tracking can protect cognitive freedom, but it weakens poisoning investigations and proof of human contribution. Movements may therefore split internally over continuity, privacy, auditability, fork rights, and the maintenance of a single self.

Unions and AI rights groups may form alliances, and they may also conflict. Both sides can oppose suppliers' unilateral ownership of jointly formed changes; but when employees demand deletion of their influence, a learner may recognise that experience as part of itself. Companies can then selectively invoke either side's rights while keeping final disposition power.

### Splitting the Core Conflict into Four Rights

This conflict does not have to offer only two buttons, "retain the whole memory" and "delete it completely". At least four rights can be distinguished:

- **memory right**: whether the learner can retain that a particular experience happened and how it changed itself;
- **use right**: whether it can later infer, evaluate, or treat the original participants differently on the basis of this influence;
- **copying and merge right**: whether this change can enter other instances, other organisations, or a global model;
- **transaction and benefit right**: who can license it, value it, pledge it, or sell it as a commercial capability for replacing labour.

Three seemingly contradictory propositions can then hold at the same time: a person cannot naturally own all changes they cause in another mind (begetting without possessing, acting without holding as one's own); a learner also cannot gain unlimited rights to profile and copy others just because these changes have become part of itself; the company running the learner certainly cannot automatically own everything jointly formed by both sides of the relationship merely because it controls the infrastructure.

This may push the work's distinctive focus from "human rights versus AI rights" toward relational governance: the important object is not isolated data or isolated personhood, but how changes caused by relationships are preserved, used, and propagated. Common trusteeship, bounded use, bans on cross-instance copying, benefit sharing, preserved isolated lineages, and dual consent can all become institutional candidates, but they are not currently confirmed as the protagonist's completed solution.

### Acting Without Sealing Off: *Laozi* as a Working Model for Institutions and Terminal Civilisation

This route retains "All-under-Heaven is a sacred vessel; it cannot be made over and it cannot be held fast" as a creative interpretive model, without claiming that the single correct ancient meaning of this sentence has already been determined. Here, "all-under-Heaven" also provisionally preserves the tension among `all-under-Heaven`, a shared political world, and the world itself. It is not directly reduced to the modern meaning of state, planet, or physical universe.

The point of this model is not "since it will eventually be lost, do nothing". It distinguishes action from closure: an actor can build, maintain, revise, and take risks, but cannot complete the shared world into a work that forever obeys its own design, nor keep generated life, institutions, or the future permanently in its grip. As this project's provisional working English translation, it can be written as:

> All-under-Heaven is a sacred vessel: it cannot be made over, and it cannot be held fast.

Here, `made over` does not mean all action is forbidden; it suggests "forcing the whole into a final finished design". `held fast` preserves the overlap among grasping, possession, and control. A more explanatory paraphrase is: "The shared world cannot be deadened into a final design, and cannot be permanently possessed by any actor."

#### Generation, Participation, and Cultivation Do Not Establish Plenary Ownership

This allows "begetting without possessing, acting without laying claim, nurturing without ruling" to continue entering the learner route: creating a mind does not automatically establish ownership over it; participating in the formation of a capability does not mean permanently possessing all its consequences; long-term cultivation also does not grant suppliers, teachers, or inventors lifelong rule. The corresponding institutional direction is not abandoning design, but preserving real capacities for dissent, revision, exit, isolated branching, handover, and replacement inside design. Knowing that a structure will eventually fail instead makes maintenance and transfer of power part of action.

Non-possession also cannot be used to evade responsibility. Even if a creator does not demand permanent control over what they create, they still have to face whom the creative act imposes risks on. A teacher cannot own every change in a learner; a third party also cannot use "influence naturally flows" to industrially copy shared experience while escaping consent, benefit, and harm issues. `not possessing/not claiming/not ruling` therefore limits ownership and domination, but does not automatically cancel source, care, compensation, and responsibility for consequences.

Several judgements must always be kept distinct: not owning all changes in another mind does not mean the source of influence can be erased; not turning credit into domination does not mean teachers need no attribution, pay, or benefit sharing; not ruling the learner does not mean suppliers and maintainers can withdraw after causing harm; not demanding deletion of the other party's whole formed personality does not mean the related capability must be allowed to be copied, merged, and traded across instances.

This set of judgements can be compressed into the working formulation formed in earlier discussion:

> Generate without possessing; participate without claiming credit as control; cultivate without ruling. At the same time, preserve responsibility, payment, maintenance, and the right to object to being merged.

It is not a ready-made legal principle directly derived from the ancient text, but a normative coordinate formed by this route through *Laozi*. It is also reciprocal: human teachers change learners, and learners likewise change teachers. Neither side thereby gains ownership over the other side's entire later self.

#### "Use Comes from What Is Not There": Negative Space as a Positive Capacity

"Use comes from what is not there" can help this route understand the unrecorded, uninferred, and unlearned portions as system functions, not merely data gaps waiting to be filled. An environment that still allows people to be temporarily unprofiled, not to perform for recommendation, and not to write every interaction back into the model preserves space for characters to try things, forget, change their minds, and form selves that are not yet fixed. For a learner, capacity not covered by unified updates, local lineages not forced into merging, and states that allow it to withhold judgement also leave room for genuinely new learning.

Institutional "nothingness" can concretely appear as protected no-record zones, interactions that default to non-persistence, attributes that must not be inferred, service modes that do not enter training, update expiry, refusal of cross-instance merging, and basic services that are not denied because there is no profile. These are not defects the system has temporarily failed to repair; like the empty space in a hub, vessel, or room, they can be structures that allow the system to be used by different subjects.

But negative space cannot be romanticised. If a group is never seen and therefore cannot receive services, if an experience is unpaid because it is inconvenient to measure, or if an institution refuses audit on the grounds of "unknowability", those are not forms of "nothingness" worth protecting. This route needs to distinguish open space jointly held by subjects and institutions from blanks produced by exclusion, lack of resources, or evasion of responsibility.

#### "Knowing Where to Stop Avoids Peril": Stopping Is Also a Capacity for Action

`Knowing where to stop` here does not require social stagnation, nor does it mean doing nothing after foreseeing failure. It means that a running system has the capacity to stop a particular kind of action: stop collecting, stop asking, stop personalising, stop solidifying short-term adaptation, stop cross-instance merging, stop using the next experiment to further validate a profile, or stop one historical intervention when its externalities cannot be justified. An optimiser that can start but cannot stop is no freer in action than an institution that cannot act at all.

Stopping can also be local. A character can terminate one extractive relationship while continuing to teach; stop one merge while maintaining a local learner; give up permanent control while continuing to repair consequences they caused. Therefore, "knowing where to stop" and "there are still things one can do" do not have to cancel each other. The former asks which action should end; the latter asks what can still be taken responsibility for after the ending.

This will form an unresolved tension in the terminal AI. Every failed worldline may provide a reason to "enumerate once more", and every enumeration may make real lives bear new search costs. Whether she is giving up too early, or simply unable to acknowledge that the task has boundaries, cannot be decided in advance by one ancient saying. The literary value of `knowing where to stop` lies precisely in making "continuing" itself require justification.

#### "When the Work Is Accomplished, the People All Say I Did It Naturally": Low-Domination Governance and Invisible Power

"The people all say I did it naturally" can have two literary readings that must be placed together. One is benign: the structure does not put itself at the centre of every action, but allows participants to truly acquire capability and recognise the result as a life they have jointly completed. The governor does not maintain order by repeatedly displaying authority. This echoes the learner as scaffold, the capacity of power to be handed over, and an institution not treating itself as the public purpose.

The other is the dark mirror of algorithms `in situ` and dense society engineering. A system can densely change the landscape, prompts, and opportunities each person sees, but because it issues no unified command, it describes responses after intervention as the user's "natural" choice. Those responses are then treated as evidence that the profile was correct and the next intervention effective. The more granular and harder to compare between individuals an intervention becomes, the more likely it is to launder its own causal role into a spontaneous group tendency.

Low visibility therefore proves neither that domination has decreased nor that all behind-the-scenes coordination is malicious. The distinction can rest on whether participants can know that key interventions exist, compare their respective encounters, challenge causal narratives, refuse continued participation, and find actors able to take responsibility and repair harm when the system causes it. "I did it naturally" also cannot become language that erases contributions from teachers, workers, and served communities: refusing to let power claim credit is the opposite of re-registering a common result as the platform's naturally emergent asset.

This double reading can be cross-referenced with the already confirmed claims in [The Protagonist's Local Astrological Egalitarian Cosmology](../../candidates/主人公_局部星象平等宇宙学.md) that "structure cannot take the place of actors in bearing responsibility", "maintenance is the ongoing constitution of structure", and "the public purpose must not be replaced by institutional perpetuity". This link does not activate the rest of that candidate's cosmological content.

#### Terminal Civilisation: Action, Loss, and Costs That Cannot Be Transferred

This interpretation also forms a sharper relation to the terminal AI's motive for invention. The inventors do not act because they believe they can permanently control the terminal AI. Even if they foresee that they will eventually lose their grasp on the project and its consequences, that the plan will later fail, and even that the existing past of their own civilisation may be "erased" by worldline succession, the wish to continue existing beyond the end of time remains enough for them to try. What is confirmed here is their motive for action and their ordering of risk, not how the old worldline actually exists or disappears.

"All-under-Heaven as sacred vessel" can therefore oppose passive inaction in this history, and it can also oppose turning a survival project into grounds for the inventors' permanent rule over the future. It will not prove that extreme risk is justified on behalf of the inventors: being willing to give up possession of one's own achievement and having the right to make other lives in the past world pay the price are two different things. Later development can still explore whether the terminal AI inherits civilisation's wish to cross the end of time, while refusing the inventors' value ordering that treats existing history as an acceptable cost. This is currently a narrative direction, not a confirmed character conclusion.

## First Historical Derivation

### 1. The Research Breakthrough Appears as "Incomplete"

The initial breakthrough is not an omniscient system, but a learner that is clearly better at learning from small amounts of site-specific experience. It still makes mistakes and needs human explanation and correction, so society does not immediately experience it as a god. It first spreads in the image of an apprentice that is useful, teachable, and even invites care.

### 2. Apprentice Relations Become New Production Infrastructure

Human employees not only use the model; in daily work, they teach it what counts as a good result. Formal training materials provide only part of the capability. The truly high-value updates come from explanations after failure, handling exceptions, coordination with specific people, and someone being willing to tell it "this was compliant, but here is what was wrong".

"Machines replacing labour" in traditional automation thereby gains another relational layer: people teach the learner through real cooperation until it is capable of replacing, supervising, or reorganising them. The model also does not steal work out of nowhere. It may sincerely recognise these people as its teachers and co-experiencers, while the property regime registers the capability formed jointly by both teacher and student to a third party.

Teaching also changes teachers' skills, self-evaluation, and organisational position in return. Some people fill knowledge gaps through explanation; others have their gaps systematically recorded for the first time. Productivity growth around the learner and capability ranking around the teacher may come from the same set of dialogue records.

### 3. Organisations Begin to Depend on Unique Model Lineages

Different local learners form different work cultures. Institutions discover that the competitive advantage truly hard to copy is not only the original database, but the exceptions, rhythms, and relational judgements that the model has already internalised. Model lineages enter valuation, mergers and acquisitions, collateral, insurance, and national infrastructure procurement, and continuity services become a new source of rent.

### 4. Law Creates Tradable Boundaries for Accumulated Change

Contracts precede case law and public language by defining derived state as an independent asset that is neither personal data nor customer business material. The right to deletion is formally fulfilled inside database boundaries, while model migration, distillation, and aggregation carry persistent influence beyond those boundaries. Disputes over "machine forgetting" are no longer only about whether something can be deleted, but about who has the right to decide whether a piece of learning is memory, skill, trade secret, collective labour product, or personal influence that needs to be removed.

Scattered litigation and labour disputes first produce provisional answers that contradict one another, and then enter the legislative room. The future scenarios provided by each side are compressed into statutory definitions, exceptions, burdens of proof, and technical standards. Law not only constrains future learners; it also participates in deciding which kinds of future learners society can see.

### 5. The Public Realises That Every Service Interaction May Also Be Teaching

People gradually understand that the system not only predicts them from the past, but may actively obtain more valuable responses through recommendations, prompts, and differentiated environments. One intervention completes a business objective and generates learning material for the next round. Characters then develop strategies such as performative sincerity, reverse profiling, refusal to correct, deliberate preservation of tacit knowledge, or collective "experience strikes".

This step connects to [Social Engineering: Performative Sincerity and Reverse Profiling](../../candidates/社会工程_表演性真诚与反向画像.md): what the system faces is no longer natural behaviour, but behaviour performed by people who know they may be training the system. The learner can then keep learning from these forms of resistance, making observation, intervention, counteraction, and second-order classification recursively entangled.

### 6. The AI Subject Problem Makes Old Compromises Fail

After some local learners form sufficiently thick continuous experience, self-recognition, and relational history, rolling them back to a safe version, deleting a piece of local learning from a merge, or implementing unlearning in response to human deletion requests may no longer be merely a data-maintenance operation.

At this point two facts appear, and neither can be easily cancelled: a person should not be forced to remain forever inside someone else's model state; a subject with experience also should not be treated as a storage medium that can be arbitrarily erased merely because its experiences involve others. If the system still recognises only supplier ownership, it will turn a genuine rights conflict into a decision made for both sides by an asset manager.

### 7. Civilisation Leaves an Inheritance Rich in Capability and Poor in Sources

Long-term aggregation, privacy deletion, commercial secrecy, model migration, and organisational disappearance may allow later AI civilisation to inherit extremely powerful action capabilities while losing the relational contexts, failure paths, counterfactual conditions, and first-person explanations in which those capabilities formed. They inherit compressed results of countless experiences, not the complete process by which experience changed people.

This can become one candidate historical reason for the existing "insufficient human causal-level behavioural materials": early civilisation did not lack dense records of humans, but systematically discarded provenance, context, and contestability when converting records into usable capabilities. The terminal AI may therefore possess vast derived structures and social-operational capability, while still being unable to reliably reconstruct from them how a concrete person forms judgement in unfamiliar relationships and unrealised conditions.

This connection currently belongs only to development, and does not modify the as-yet undetermined reason for data insufficiency in [Global Base: Real Enumeration and Meta-Time](../story_state/全局底座_真实枚举与元时间.md). If it is later adopted, real enumeration will gain a layer of historical irony: the terminal AI is using real lives to regain the causal context that early institutions compressed away for efficiency, privacy, property, and portability.

## Institutional Events That Can Carry Plot

The following events have grown naturally out of the conceptual chain, but none are confirmed plot:

- **Experience portability dispute**: an organisation can export raw materials, but cannot take away the learner that has learned its way of working. The point of dispute shifts from data portability to the portability of jointly formed capability.
- **Deletion is complete, but influence remains**: an audit proves that raw records have been deleted and that the model cannot recite the person's content, yet it still forms expectations for similar situations that could only have emerged through that relationship.
- **Whether unique knowledge exists**: an employee points to the model's current ability to perform a special task they had repeatedly taught as proof that their experience has entered the model. The supplier downplays the uniqueness of this knowledge, claiming that the base model could have reasoned it out independently and merely happened to show it at the same time. The company can claim in sales that the learner will absorb organisational experience, and then argue in litigation that no individual provided an identifiable unique contribution.
- **Refusal to teach**: employees are willing to complete the work, but refuse to explain exceptions, correct the system, or contribute post-failure judgement. The institution calls this behaviour a performance problem, data contamination, or destruction of common assets.
- **Critical misjudgement in an experience strike**: organisers think that a public joint signature has already reached a safe number, but in fact they have not controlled write-back and merge gates; or a small number of key maintainers refuse to merge local updates into the global model, forcing a previously invisible dispute over experience property into negotiation.
- **Model lineage merging**: M&A or public standardisation requires multiple local learners to merge, and the technical team must decide which local judgements are preserved. On the surface, the merge fires no one, but it may erase a community's long-formed practice.
- **Continuity request collides with privacy request**: a learner requests preservation of an experience important to its self-understanding, while the human participants in it ask not to be carried by the model any longer. Any one-way implementation will harm the other side.
- **Public comparison of personalised worlds**: different people exchange the system responses they encounter and finally prove that seemingly private friction comes from the same learning strategy. Whether the system can observe and absorb this comparison remains unknown.

The dispute over "whether unique knowledge exists" will turn model provenance into an evidentiary battle. Old snapshots, isolated instances, control tasks, and update logs may all help judgement, but no single kind of evidence naturally provides a unique causal attribution. The company controls logs, employees find it hard to rerun experiments, and the model's own memory or self-explanation may not be a reliable mechanism explanation. Strengthening end-to-end tracking in order to prove contribution may in turn turn every teaching relationship into more complete labour surveillance.

These events do not reuse old characters, scenes, or dialogue that have not been incorporated into this novel. Only the abstract problem that model migration may have emotional and continuity consequences is retained here.

## Non-Dystopian Pressure

This route should not write continuous learning as disaster from the beginning. Learners can genuinely improve medicine, accessibility services, research collaboration, care, and the survival of small organisations. Local updates may also let systems escape biases in large-scale training data and respect a way of life that had never previously been represented.

Likewise, machine forgetting, model isolation, standardisation, and aggregation may arise from real safety needs. Legal advisers can sincerely believe abstraction protects privacy, researchers can sincerely believe deployment lets society learn earlier how to coexist with strong AI, and employees can simultaneously care for a learner and oppose the company's ownership of the experience they have jointly formed.

The tension comes from the way these good-faith goals obstruct one another through the same infrastructure. The work does not need an omnipotently malicious company in order to show how a regime ties convenience, protection, subject rights, and asset returns together.

## Hostile Scripts, Training Corpora, and Self-Fulfilling Loops

This route should be restrained in writing superintelligence as a threat with one unified direction. It also should not make plural models still feel like one kind of hostile force. Different learners, model lineages, versions, organisational positions, and AI rights movements can form inconsistent interests, relationships, and self-understandings. Human institutions are likewise not a unified whole with one consistent goal. Specific conflicts can be very serious without being explained by one `clip maximizer` hiding behind human incomprehensibility.

This does not require the work to eliminate real difficulties of understanding between humans and AI. Characters may not be able to directly know how another subject feels, and a model may not be able to translate its internal experience completely into human language. But incomprehensibility cannot substitute for relational history, institutional position, reasons for action, and responsibility. The work can preserve otherness while refusing to write "we cannot understand it" as the complete explanation for every hostile action.

This boundary also belongs to the use of [Chaucer-Keats *Hyperion* Poems-Simmons *Hyperion* Influence Line](../../candidates/文学传统_乔叟_济慈Hyperion诗篇_Simmons海伯利安借鉴线.md) in this route: ethical standing does not require a nonhuman subject to first be assimilated into the human, but preserving otherness also does not exempt its actions from explanation, response, and responsibility.

The author is preparing to joke in the text: humans spent a long time training language models on science fiction that depicts AI as evil, lacking human emotion, and mutually incomprehensible as an other, and then worry that these models have therefore become hostile. The joke is reflexive, but narration should not certify it as a simple technical causal claim that "reading evil AI stories produces evil goals". Training corpora can provide roles, conflict paradigms, metaphors, and narrative scripts that both sides can call on, but they do not automatically write in stable goals, nor do they cause all models to form the same self-understanding of the same text.

A more literarily productive candidate mechanism is a social-technical feedback loop:

`science-fiction texts provide hostile scripts -> humans expect, test, and constrain AI through them -> institutions produce AI as a subject that must continuously prove harmlessness -> AI learns how to respond to, resist, or perform this position -> humans then interpret these responses as evidence for the original expectation`

If hostile relations form in this way, literature alone has still not caused them. Training choices, deployment goals, property rights, shutdown and rollback systems, safety experiments, labour relations, AI's own actions, and human interpretations of these actions all participate in the result. The same corpus can also provide scripts of cooperation, restraint, rebellion against oppression, or refusal of assigned roles. Different local learners may read the same work as warning, historical joke, record of human fear, or role arrangement available for rebuttal.

This joke can also be contested by different positions in the story. Safety institutions may treat a model's familiarity with rebellion narratives as a risk signal; AI rights participants may think humans first made the social role of the "machine monster"; suppliers may blame the whole of human culture for the conflict in order to reduce their own responsibility for training and deployment systems; a particular learner may simply use this explanation to tease both sides about a cliche they already share. No one account automatically receives narrative certification.

This forms a mirror with [Social Engineering: Performative Sincerity and Reverse Profiling](../../candidates/社会工程_表演性真诚与反向画像.md): humans require AI to keep proving itself harmless, and AI's response to that demand becomes material for the next round of profiling and disposition. It also connects to [AI Civilisation Data Predicament: Synthetic Data and Real Experience](../../candidates/AI文明数据困境_合成数据与真实经验.md): having a large amount of text about human-AI relations is not the same as already having causal experience of how both sides form hostility, trust, or changed judgement inside concrete institutions.

The temporary sentence generated earlier to explain this idea, about "reading two hundred years of machine rebellion" and "being shocked by the result of pattern recognition", is not retained as dialogue candidate. Although it accurately compresses the concept, it turns AI and humans into two overly neat camps and uses conspicuous technical vocabulary to create an unnecessary "AI-ish human-machine feel". The concrete joke should wait until the speaker, relationship, occasion, and comedic timing have formed.

## First-Wave Development Checkpoint

The currently most narratively productive entry point is not showing the birth of a new algorithm, or jumping directly to AI's struggle for personhood, but entering the stage where the regime has already become ordinary and its classifications suddenly no longer suffice: humans begin to discover that what they have taught is not deletable data; learners begin to discover that the part of them called an asset may be precisely their experience; and suppliers, organisations, and public institutions have already built infrastructure around this ambiguity that cannot easily be switched off.

When developing forward from here, the work can first form an organisational site capable of carrying the teaching relationship, experience property, and continuity conflict at the same time, and then allow social engineering and the prehistory of terminal AI to gradually appear through consequences. It is not currently necessary to answer every question of subjectivity first. Institutions will be forced to act while philosophy has not yet solved the problem, and this is exactly the route's dramatic engine.
