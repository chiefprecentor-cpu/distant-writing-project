## Plot-Block Boundary

This set of materials develops the following together:

> A public system repeatedly produces encounters, and Xuyuan misreads them as fate;
> he finally actively invites Lulu, but still does not understand how both sides classify the relationship;
> Kew lets him briefly see the world before him, and under the cherry tree forces him to receive a real answer.

It can span multiple presentation nodes. It does not require every encounter and every plant to become an independent scene.

## Candidate Position in the Whole Work

## Early Relationship History: Encounters Caused by System Failure

### Current Selected Sequence and Effect

1. **Peanut-butter-toast collision and niche short talk**

   Xuyuan instead occupies the classic shoujo-manga role of the person "running late with bread in their mouth" and collides with Lulu. After confirming neither of them is hurt, Lulu directly analyses to a stranger that this feels like shoujo manga; Xuyuan fully understands, but offers an irrelevant objection that the toast has peanut butter on it. Their apologies, physical distance and subsequent separate actions naturally show that they did not previously know each other, without requiring the narrator to say so. They then discover that they are both heading to the same small talk, but can sit a few rows apart, stop talking, and still not exchange names here.

   The talk does not name the discipline or full event title, and does not write out the lecturer's script; once the text enters the room, it can simply call it `the talk`. Three sampled slide moments indicate that about ten minutes pass, rather than that the talk has only three slides: `PATCHWORK GIRL — SHELLEY JACKSON / AFTER MARY SHELLEY`, `CALVINO — THE BURNING OF THE ABOMINABLE HOUSE`, `STORY SIFTING — SELECT THE UNEXPECTED`. At the third moment, Xuyuan begins to drift and notices Lulu again. The title also partially describes his attention sifting an unexpected and salient person out of the room, but the narration does not explain this relation. The slides are only background in the first pass; on rereading, they form delayed irony with his future theory, choices and enumeration. Lulu may later explain that she originally walked into the wrong room, but found it interesting and therefore did not leave midway.

   After the talk ends, the two still do not speak. The current draft closes with Xuyuan arriving in the corridor and finding that she has already left: this is not the moment when he begins resisting or amplifying a normal encounter, but only a small point of honest attention that only he can perceive. Its intensity stays below the later continuous chance encounters that produce the "fate" explanation.

   The visible Calvino text uses *The Burning of the Abominable House*, because compared with the theoretical essay it more directly echoes the reconstruction of possible histories from limited characters, actions and clues. "Cybernetics and Ghosts" is kept only as author-side background for combinatory literature. *Invisible Cities* is not used here; it is reserved for a later moment when China Miéville's *The City & the City*, London and "Folding Beijing" can be discussed together.

2. **Several very short transport-failure encounters**

   Three transport failures happen on the same commuting day across three consecutive weeks. Before the montage enters the first announcement, third-person narration close to Xuyuan first compresses this period: `For three weeks running, the one morning he took the Tube was the morning something went wrong.` This sentence does not explain in advance why he takes the Tube only on this day each week; the reason for oversleeping is left for the conversation during their shared walk.

   The montage's progression does not depend on the failures becoming more serious, but on the two characters' relational actions shifting from "seeing" to "seeing each other" and then to "trying to communicate". The function of repetition is to establish an absurd rhythm of encounter; it does not require every encounter to carry dialogue or equal game time.

   The three encounters share the syntax of "transport ambience - third-person narration writes a temporary announcement / failure information enters the sound - the crowd changes direction - recognition of the other person", but the endings change in sequence:

   - The first time, Xuyuan recognises her in the crowd; before he can confirm whether she has also seen him, the scene cuts away.
   - The second time compresses the preceding sound and crowd-flow steps. Lulu recognises him first and says `You again.`; before Xuyuan can make a full reply, the crowd changing direction separates them, and the scene cuts away again.
   - After the partial suspension, the third time still begins with the same syntax, but once the crowd reaches street level and disperses, the two remain on the same side. This time the scene does not cut. The transport-system announcement gives way to footsteps, traffic and city sound, and the shared walk becomes the only opened time segment in the montage.

   The current third time uses `He found her in the crowd. She saw him too.` The first sentence still makes Xuyuan carry the subjective action of "searching / sifting someone out of the crowd"; the second only confirms that Lulu has also recognised him. It no longer uses the symmetrical `found / found`, which would too early imply that Lulu was actively searching in the same way, and would also make the work layer strengthen Xuyuan's sense of "fate".

   The specific announcements are not written one by one as player text, and there is no need to explain complex failures for the first two times. The transport section keeps dialogue sparse but does not keep the stillness of the opening ceremony: short sentences, rapidly redirecting crowds, continuous cuts and unfinished responses provide movement density. Longer sentences and pauses only enter after the third time becomes a walk.

3. **Shared walk after a Tube partial suspension**

   The preceding quick grammar no longer cuts away this time. Xuyuan says he is going to walk, and Lulu independently decides to go with him; on the way they exchange names, completing the first long conversation and natural silence not dependent on the event itself. Xuyuan usually finds Tube fares too expensive, so he walks on most school days; on one fixed day each week he tends to oversleep, and only then takes the Tube instead.

   The current draft has Xuyuan explicitly say he is going to walk to the Strand. Lulu first states that she is going to the same destination, then chooses to abandon the alternative-transport queue. The two only use `a lecture` and `a seminar` to say what they each need to do at university, giving their walk, arrival and separation practical schedule support; no specific course is named.

   Xuyuan's current educational trajectory uses "an undergraduate basis in English Literature + an interdisciplinary Master's in digital culture / AI, data and culture". The former gives his complex cognition a form shaped by narrative branching, counterfactuals, textual splicing and interpretive uncertainty; the latter provides a conceptual entrance into AI, data, institutions and social systems. Neither turns him into an all-purpose engineer who single-handedly completes later technical implementation. The shared walk exchanges only colloquial programme labels, and does not explain departmental structures, research directions or career plans. The official programme name remains `TBD`.

   The current visual direction is not to use a dedicated two-person CG, but two consecutive image states: first, the background outside the station / at the alternative-transport queue completes the turn from the third short cut into a sustained scene; then the scene enters a road background with horizontal traffic-light trails, carrying the names, walking habits, the talk callback and natural silence. The previous image can reuse the ending of the third transport encounter, so in practice only one main walking background needs to be added. The traffic trails show that the city and commute remain in motion, and that the two have not been isolated by the composition into a romantic still image; the area near the characters and the road contours still need to be clear, so the whole image does not become a long-exposure night-scene effect. The specific road section remains `TBD` and is not named in the text.

   The quiet of the walk is not blankness or stillness. At the beginning, traffic, crowds and shorter dialogue still occupy the rhythm; as the two walk away, footsteps, passing vehicles, waiting to cross and pauses that do not have to be filled immediately gradually gain space. This can preserve the effect that the two can be silent naturally, while giving that silence a different texture from the quiet of the cold open and the talk.

   Two waits at crossings segment the walking conversation: the first follows the name exchange and enters that day's classes and commuting habits; the second follows `War Studies`, making room for external silence. They are not traffic-light motifs that the player needs to identify. The second currently changes to `At the next crossing, they stopped with everyone else. Traffic passed.`, preserving the walking rhythm without repeating `changed against them`, and without implying that the city is intentionally obstructing them.

4. **Very short normal carriage recurrence in the fourth week**

   The week after the shared walk can first use `The following week, nothing went wrong.` to recover the syntax of the previous three weeks. The two still actively recognise and talk to each other, showing that the relationship no longer depends on system failure.

5. **Friend conversation before the maid cafe (length TBD)**

   After the normal carriage recurrence, Xuyuan first tells his mate about Lulu in a concentrated way. This section should not let him reach the conclusion "I should act"; it only makes him admit, willingly or unwillingly, that he is already paying attention to her, and that this attention predates his awareness that he is making a judgement. The continuous encounters here mainly make him repeatedly acquire the desire to express himself and add details that were originally unnecessary. The further beautification of the low-probability sequence into pressure to act is left until the maid cafe adds another coincidence.

   The friend currently uses the everyday name `Yuankai`. He is an all-rounder friend familiar with simplified-Chinese pan-ACG internet memes and very widely read, able to basically catch the niche science-fiction, media or thought discussions Xuyuan actively throws out. Related content does not enter the work as one-way explanation from the friend to the player, but through dialogue in which the two already share part of the context and can question and rebut each other. In different plots he can also bring lively air into heavier narration, or expose the ridiculous parts of Xuyuan's speech through basically non-malicious mockery. His first appearance does not need to prove all functions at once; internet memes and breadth of knowledge are his speaking resources, and do not make him an always-correct authorial explainer.

   The name joke stays very short. It does not restore the dense comedy of the old summary, with its continuous listing of characters and search results. The current version does not use a `Lulu / Ruru` misrecognition: in this spoken English environment, the two cannot form a reliable pronunciation difference, and Xuyuan may not yet know whether Lulu uses the spelling `L` or `R`. A one-beat reaction that can continue to be compared is `VTuber?`, or compressing the simplified-Chinese template "that is very ... / this ... has force" into an absurd compliment that makes no real judgement about the name itself, for example `That's a strong name.` If the *Pretty Cure* translation meme is used later, only the latter half is considered for retention: "'light' is an attribute, 'beautiful girl' is gender, 'warrior' is species". It should be placed in a context where title translation or classification dispute actually appears, not forced into Lulu's name joke.

   `So TfL is your matchmaker now.` is no longer an opening candidate. The specific interest Xuyuan solemnly wants to introduce is not electronic literature or another niche activity, but "bishoujo". The Chinese semantic core to preserve is: `My interest is bishoujo. The three dimensions of beautiful, young and girl are all read by me.` He places ordinary people's activity-based interests, such as films, games and sports, alongside his own interest in "bishoujo", then treats the three characters as three dimensions whose right of interpretation he controls. This external statement recovers the internal sentence from the talk, `by his own private reading of each of the word's three characters`, while also exposing that his attraction truly exists, his classification standards are highly private, and they can almost be rewritten by him at any time.

   Yuankai has heard the core of this statement before, but "the three characters each form a dimension" is the new development this time, so he responds with `That part is new.` The feel of a simplified-Chinese translation meme can be compressed in English into a readable slight translationese, such as `Is that so? Why are you telling me?`; `Meaning?` can likewise compress the meme source without explanation from the narration. The two lines do not both need to be used side by side. English does not use `I define ... for myself`: `define` can imply that Xuyuan has set relatively stable classification boundaries, while the Chinese "interpreted by me" allows his private reading of the three characters to continue changing, even serving new judgements according to his mood. A literal translation as `explain` would only mean explaining existing meanings to someone else. The draft uses `My interest is bishoujo. Each of its three characters is a separate dimension. How I read all three is entirely up to me.`, using `read` to recover the talk's `private reading` and not arbitrarily fixing the three dimensions as `beauty / youth / womanhood`. The conversation location is an ordinary cafe near Kingsway.

   This can become a low-risk everyday expression of the protagonist's already confirmed abductive mechanism: his conclusions sometimes first appear as shifts in attention or not-yet-verbalised judgements, and are explained only afterwards. This does not prove that Lulu objectively satisfies an independent standard; the three dimensions and the judgement still all come from Xuyuan. What really makes him feel complicated is that although he retains the power to rewrite the three dimensions at any time, before he has time to consciously place Lulu inside them, he discovers that "she fits" has already appeared as a judgement. He may temporarily forget this after the talk, and the repeated encounters then keep bringing it back to his attention.

   The current version makes this section explicitly longer than the shared walk. The short questions and answers of the shared walk establish the first sustained interaction between two strangers; the friend conversation is the first full display of how Xuyuan organises experience into explanation. This asymmetry of length is also character expression: the words Xuyuan uses to explain the encounters are already more numerous than the words he has actually said to Lulu.

   The extension does not come from adding more topics or jokes, or evenly extending every round of Q&A, but from Xuyuan's single speeches becoming progressively longer: the opening uses the tighter "bishoujo" classification explanation to establish the joke; the middle lets him recount, in one continuous telling, both the event sequence and the repetition-variation structure; then the longest explanation is reserved for the distinction "absence of an arranger is not absence of form, and certainty that something is coincidence is not noise". The friend's dialogue turns are fewer than in the shared walk; he mainly catches, corrects or compresses Xuyuan at these three turning points. The ending then returns to short lines, compressing the long explanation back into `cute / It is incomplete`. This texture of longer reading time and fewer exchanges should be kept first, then trimmed by the opening rhythm after the English section works, rather than being pre-limited by a fixed word count.

   The current conversational centre shifts from "what kind of person Lulu is" to "how this sequence of events should be understood". Lulu is the attention attractor that makes Xuyuan choose and keep talking about this sequence, and she is also the way the friend finally brings the technical discussion back to a person. But the subject of the conversation can first allow the two to discuss seriously: after denying fate or heavenly arrangement, must a set of events that is certainly coincidence be downgraded into noise interfering with the main line? Xuyuan can admit there is no supernatural arrangement while insisting that "chance" only negates one causal explanation and does not automatically cancel an event group's rarity, mystery, formal beauty or real influence on later action.

   The following six plot movements are currently fixed first, without locking the final English length:

   1. Xuyuan opens with "ordinary people's interests are activities such as films, games and sports; my interest is bishoujo", then proposes that the three characters are dimensions interpreted by him. The friend is familiar with the first half of the claim. Preserve:

      > “Most people’s interests are activities,” he said. “Films. Games. Sport. Mine is *bishoujo*. Each of its three characters is a separate dimension. How I read all three is entirely up to me.”
      >
      > His friend waited.
      >
      > “That part is new.”

   2. Xuyuan explains that the judgement "she fits" came before his conscious explanation. The friend can point out that the judgement still belongs to him, but should not immediately end the topic with `cute`. "Fits" itself is not a rare result, and does not constitute a strict or special threshold for partner selection; the anomaly in this beat is only that a judgement which should have been subjectively controlled by Xuyuan appeared before he noticed himself making it. The current English uses `The result was not unusual. The order was.` to limit the intensity, and no longer uses `found the conclusion there / annoying` to write the result itself as a major discovery.

   3. Xuyuan tells the complete event chain only once in chronological order, and does not list it again at the end with `A blind corner. A wrong room...`. He needs to explain how each recurrence changes one condition: at first only he recognises her; then Lulu speaks first but is separated by the crowd; the third time the system sends both of them to street level and opens a walking segment; the fourth time the system does not fail, yet Lulu still actively approaches. In this way, "repetition and variation of the same structure" comes from the relation between events rather than from a list of nouns.

   4. During the telling, he may temporarily use the classic fate filter, for example `It began to feel as though fate were staging the same meeting again, each time letting it run a little longer.` He then denies for himself that there is an arranger, without cancelling the repetition, variation and form he has already experienced. `rhythm` can still be kept, but it needs to be combined with `variation`, `refrain`, or a concrete structure such as "one fewer obstacle each time"; `The causes changed. She was the refrain.` is a stronger beautifying direction, but has not yet been adopted.

   5. After the active approach in the fourth week, the friend can joke:

      > “You’re not usually one of those ‘a woman spoke to me, so she must be interested’ people.”

      Xuyuan explicitly denies that he has already inferred that Lulu has romantic interest in him; what he cares about is that the event structure has changed. The first three approaches depended on external failure; the fourth time, the external cause disappears but the interaction still happens. The friend can compress this technical phrasing back into "she came over and talked to you", but Xuyuan should not be written as someone who misrecognises any woman's goodwill.

   6. The technical discussion revolves around a still-undrafted core distinction: `not arranged` is not equivalent to `irrelevant`, and being certain something is coincidence is not equivalent to deleting it as noise. Possible English meanings include:

      > “Being certain it was coincidence would only remove the arranger. It wouldn’t turn what happened into noise.”
      >
      > “Probability is not an instruction to be unimpressed.”

      The friend can rebut through after-the-fact selection, the absence of a pre-specified event group, or "noise only exists relative to a question"; Xuyuan admits that he indeed chose this set of events because of Lulu. In this way Lulu finally returns to the centre of the conversation as his attention attractor, rather than being the object analysed by both of them from the opening onward.

   The realism of the conversation depends on the fact that the first telling is itself selection and organisation, rather than making Xuyuan painstakingly review something he has already fully said. The friend's action pauses can be made specific after the location is fixed; the current `His friend waited` only means that he does not rush to interrupt, so Xuyuan keeps making the account more complete by himself. The ending can still use the friend compressing the long explanation into `cute`, and Xuyuan answering `It is incomplete`: it acknowledges attraction, without producing probability proof, an action decision or a disillusionment stress test too early.

   This conversation can also leave material for his later "dating-sim strategy on himself": he may later reinterpret "judgement before awareness" as evidence that this attraction was not temporarily assembled, reinterpret the "continuous encounters" as further evidence that should not be ignored, then use words he had previously said to persuade himself to act. The work does not confirm this derivation for him; the friend's questions and later Lulu can both make this self-closing explanation meet resistance.

6. **Maid cafe table-sharing**

   Xuyuan is brought there by his friend; Lulu is brought there by hers. The shop is crowded, and the two groups temporarily share a table. It is located after the transport montage and the shared walk, allowing further conversation under the protection of friends and a special setting, and letting the curry / rice argument first expose the everyday-life imagination Xuyuan has formed too early.

   Because the previous friend conversation has already happened, this unarranged table-sharing will also look to Xuyuan like fate immediately adding another piece of evidence. No character needs to explain it; one look from the friend is enough. This formal echo strengthens Xuyuan's romanticisation of repeated coincidences, and does not prove that Lulu has a stable preference for accident.

   The current version no longer prioritises having Xuyuan immediately state an action judgement after leaving the shop. The friend has already completed the callback of "listener becomes witness" through one look at the table; after the meal, if the two walk together, it can either avoid becoming a separate conversation plot block or keep only one or two ordinary reactions to the people and scene just experienced. `I know it doesn’t prove anything. It still makes doing nothing feel unreasonable.` is temporarily detached from any specific node and is not placed on the way back just after the meal. If it is kept later, it is more suitable at the real action threshold, when Xuyuan has already checked the blossom period and written or nearly written the invitation but still has not sent it. The previously kept `Four Thursdays / order of magnitude / a number you don’t have` is also not repeated in shortened form after leaving the shop.

   Questions such as "does he like Lulu, or the sequence that keeps returning her to his sight?" and "is what he likes actually the feeling of being with Lulu?" do not enter the friend conversation before the maid cafe. These questions should first be placed after the maid cafe has provided another real interaction, or perhaps delayed until Kew has accumulated more shared experience; the specific node remains `TBD`. Leaving the shop no longer carries a required cognitive upgrade. Let the dining-table experience first exist as experience; Xuyuan's later check of the blossom period and invitation can already show change through action.

   How much he actually knows about Lulu, and the stress test of "what if part of her disillusions him after he truly knows her", also remain for later and are not preset to be completed when leaving the shop. When the friend enters the last question, he stops using memes and terminology. Xuyuan is not without self-criticism; he will repeatedly rebut his own fate explanation, yet may still stop reflection at a conclusion he wants to believe. The currently retained strong English version is:

   > “Then I’d still rather know. I can’t spend the rest of my life liking someone I made up.”

   The scale of `the rest of my life` deliberately arrives earlier than the relationship itself: the line both sincerely expresses his willingness to face disillusionment and exposes that, not long after getting to know Lulu, he has already impulsively expanded the question to his whole life. It does not resolve unknowns such as how Lulu classifies the relationship or whether other relationship states exist.

7. **A small amount of functional online communication, blossom-period topic and one invitation**

   The blossom-period topic has already been placed in the quiet ending of the maid cafe, and does not repeat the shared walk's function. Lulu actively says she wants to see the flowers, and Xuyuan later investigates the blossom period and invites her to Kew. The contact channel is established when Lulu says while discussing flowers, `If you find somewhere good, send it to me`; the two exchange WhatsApp before leaving the shop. This gives Xuyuan clear permission, without completing for him the later step of turning information into an invitation.

These encounters do not all need to be developed with equal weight. The shared walk carries the first truly sustained interaction; the transport montage stays short, and the maid cafe stays medium-short. The player should actually experience that the two can talk and be silent, rather than only receiving Xuyuan's explanation of "fate".

#### Psychological Density Before the Confession and Narrative Distance

Third-person narration and psychological description are not a binary choice. The current priority is to continue using the free indirect surface already established by the opening: sentences remain in third person, but gradually adopt Xuyuan's vocabulary, conditions, revisions and reasoning rhythm. The main means of increasing psychological density should not be the continuous addition of `Xuyuan thought` / `he thought`. Attributed indirect thoughts carry anchoring only when he consciously forms a proposition or makes a decision, or when the player might mistake a judgement for Lulu's or ordinary narration's; very short italicised direct thoughts are kept only for fragments that genuinely break into consciousness.

The change in psychological density should not become a monotonic rise from entering the gardens to the confession, but should form a breathing pattern: Palm House, the map and Great Pagoda mainly use practical judgements of low to medium density; Temperate House lets concepts temporarily dissolve into humidity, memory and water sound; the central-lake water birds are the lowest point of self-monitoring; the carbon sculpture, yawn, lowered light, fatigue and approaching exit bring practical problems back in; after they walk toward the cherry tree and sit down, conditional sentences, future hypotheses, self-rebuttal and unfinished phrasings rapidly accumulate to the highest point of the whole section.

The original line under the tree, `The path, the gate and the train arranged themselves ahead of them`, tried to compress the three spaces after leaving the bench into a countdown: first walking along the path back to Victoria Gate, then leaving the gardens, then boarding the train, with each step making private conversation harder. But `arranged themselves` needs additional explanation before its function appears, so it is currently changed to `Beyond the bench, the path returned to Victoria Gate. After the gate would come the train.`, preserving Xuyuan's beginning sense of pressure from the sequence ahead without the narration summarising that "the chance is closing".

The increase in density should also be carried jointly by prose and presentation: external scenery is no longer described evenly; details such as the lowering light, the short remaining route on the map and the renewed approach to Victoria Gate can gradually become more salient. But the text should not summarise them as signals that "the chance is closing", and should not let Xuyuan analyse this meaning into a complete proposition. Sentences can first grow longer, accommodating added conditions and revisions, then suddenly fall into short sentences. Character dialogue shortens, while pauses and environmental sound occupy more time. Certain words or unsaid sentence openings can return repeatedly. The gradually happier atmosphere must also happen in sentences, repetition, perceptual selection and pause structure, rather than being announced by the narration. The actual length of the confession remains open; the key contrast is between the high density of internal language beforehand and the disappearance of the whole psychological entrance after the rejection, not a required exchange of long interiority for one short confession.

After the rejection happens, the issue is not only reducing "explanatory" interior language, but sharply closing narrative access: the third-person surface no longer allows the player to observe Xuyuan's psychology. Free indirect style, indirect thoughts with `he thought` / `Xuyuan thought`, italicised direct thoughts, internal explanations of wishes and decisions, and psychological reasons added to explain an external action all stop at once. Cold as a subjective sensation Xuyuan is experiencing is also cut off with it: even though the body cannot in reality suddenly become warm, the text no longer tells the player that he feels cold, how his hands and feet are, or what the shivering means. The text keeps only language, actions, pauses, space, light and sound that can be seen or heard externally. This cliff retroactively proves that the continuously thickening language before the confession did indeed belong to a consciousness that was still accessible.

### 7. Rejection and External Language Inertia

Lulu uses the minimum information to state that she is already in a relationship.

The current first-pass uses two dialogue boxes: `I’m sorry.` first lands alone for one beat, and only the next box says `I’m already seeing someone.` A single box delivers apology and boundary all at once too quickly and too cruelly. The main function of the two boxes is not to induce the player to misjudge the answer, but to let Lulu first complete a response to Xuyuan, then state the relationship fact she must explain. Between the two boxes, do not add a close-up, musical pause, narration or hopeful Xuyuan interiority, avoiding the active enlargement of this beat into suspense; psychological access is still cut off only after the reason in the second box has appeared in full.

After `But I still wanted to tell you.`, the old `Lulu’s hands stayed together in her lap. A branch shadow moved over them.` is not restored. `stayed` writes Lulu as if she were already prepared and almost without reaction, and the branch shadow aestheticises that posture into a hard-to-read hesitation. Placed together with her later admission `I didn’t know.`, it may make readers suspect instead that she had already noticed Xuyuan's feelings, or had weighed another answer. A text-box breath is still needed before the rejection. The current version only writes `A branch shadow moved across the bench.`: the environment carries time passing, and Lulu's body is not used to assign psychological meaning to the pause. Still no content is inserted between the two rejection boxes.

After the rejection is fully spoken, all narrative access to Xuyuan's psychology is immediately withdrawn, but his mouth keeps running from shock: he explains when he began to like her, or tries to use `doki-doki` to name the genuinely heart-racing feeling that happened whenever he met Lulu. The feeling itself is not a joke temporarily invented to ease the scene; comedy and failure come from his choice, after a serious confession, of an otaku-ish expression that he also knows is otaku-ish, and from how inappropriate it seems after being actually spoken.

If `doki-doki` is used, the currently stronger performance order remains that Xuyuan, after saying it, first gives a short laugh himself; Lulu then laughs once and says `I didn’t know.` But the old sentence `Lulu’s began just after his and ended first`, although describing only physical sequence, still places her laugh too precisely in comparative relation to Xuyuan's, feeling like a relational observation close to Xuyuan. The first-pass therefore changes it to only give sequence and simple duration:

> Xuyuan gave a short laugh. Lulu laughed too, briefly.
>
> **Lulu:** I didn’t know.

Xuyuan laughing first makes Lulu's laugh harder to read as mockery of his heart-racing feeling or failed confession; `I didn’t know.` immediately closes the shared laughter, pointing to the fact that she previously did not know he felt this way, rather than that she does not understand the otaku meme `doki-doki`. The condition for keeping this elliptical line is that Xuyuan's preceding sentence has already formed a complete proposition such as "every time I saw you, there was this feeling", rather than only throwing out `doki-doki` in isolation. Lulu's existing anime literacy and the object of the proposition together make the reference naturally clear. If the first-pass still produces misreading, the clearer but also more mature and processed alternative is `I hadn’t realised.`, not the more template-like `I didn’t know you felt that way.` The text does not explain why Xuyuan keeps talking or laughs first, and does not explain why Lulu laughs with him.

The continued trembling can still form a cold-joke candidate after the rejection, but the current version does not prioritise `The shaking isn’t entirely emotional.` That half-sentence divides the proportion of cold and nervousness for Xuyuan, and also explains to Lulu and the player how the trembling should be understood, making meanings that could be carried by the scene too direct. Lighter candidates include the direct fact `I’m actually very cold.`, and the more indirect and currently stronger `I should have worn another layer.` The latter only delivers an ordinary life judgement, letting the visible trembling connect cold and confession by itself.

If the `doki-doki` shared laugh above is used, it should become the only clear shared joke after the rejection. Do not make Lulu obviously laugh again because of the cold line, lest two consecutive easing jokes turn the scene into a set of social repair procedures. Xuyuan can still later say `I should have worn another layer.`, and Lulu simply answers `You should have said.` without laughing. Lulu's slight innocence comes from the fact that, after Xuyuan laughs first, she follows very briefly with laughter, without managing every low-risk reaction into perfect politeness. Her goodwill is shown together by the sequence of laughter, the following `I didn’t know.`, the fact that after clearly rejecting him she still remains on site, notices the cold, and says on the return journey that she really enjoyed seeing the cherry blossoms. The text does not write that "she laughed to take care of his feelings", and does not summarise one laugh into a fixed character conclusion about Lulu.

This treatment also preserves Lulu's current voice base: at a high-cost relationship boundary, she uses clear, unambiguous language to say she already has a relationship; once the boundary is established, she can still react to an ordinary and slightly clumsy joke with a somewhat innocent immediacy. Indirection should be placed in how trembling, laughter and pauses explain one another; the core confession or rejection should not also become unrecognisable.

"I will never like anyone else again" is no longer an absolute declaration blurted to Lulu after the Kew rejection. The player-first-seen line currently moves it under the church magnolia, where it becomes the analytic starting point Xuyuan says to Yuankai. Another world line can still let it appear in a different external context, but it does not need to copy the same dialogue.

The later task-intervention world-line structure no longer depends on Xuyuan making a lifelong romantic promise here that he may not be able to guarantee. Therefore the current version does not use a formal `I promise`. A promise made despite knowingly being impossible to guarantee can be reserved for a later moment when Xuyuan and Interface face action, subject identity or Poincaré recurrence. For the top-level relationship, see [Player-first-seen line: curatorial hints and task-intervention world line](../creative_work/presentation/玩家首见线_策展暗示与任务干预世界线.md).

### 9. Yuankai Short Talk, Walking West and the Psychological Restart in Hyde Park

The current version connects the post-Kew Yuankai conversation, the westward walk and the Hyde Park cherry tree into the same ending of the opening, but no longer requires them to happen on the day of Kew. The currently stronger time combination is: Kew remains on Saturday; the conversation under the magnolia and the westward walk occur together on another weekday afternoon not long after. This avoids cramming Kew, rejection, a long conversation and a one-hour walk into the same day, while preserving the geographic and emotional continuity by which the conversation pushes Xuyuan into walking. If the conversation and walk are separated by another day, the psychological channel can stay closed across one night, but setting out again the next day would require a new trigger, and would weaken the complete route from campus to Hyde Park. Even if time is omitted between Kew and the conversation, the narration still does not reopen Xuyuan's psychology to the player. This Yuankai and the Yuankai who returns more than ten years later to transfer a task are the same character in two different appearances; presentation compression must not merge them into one action.

This version currently omits why the two meet: it can cut directly to the two people under the church magnolia on another weekday afternoon, without adding appointment messages, a shared activity or an explanation that Yuankai was temporarily called over. Yuankai having a group meeting later can appear only when he checks the time, giving the conversation a natural pressure to end. The current version explicitly does not have the two drink in this scene: alcohol would provide an overly convenient external cause for Xuyuan's unreasonable statements, and would also make the later video call with his mother, bench sleep and exhaustion be misread as results of alcohol.

For most of the time from the rejection and return journey to the Yuankai conversation and the walk, the narration still does not enter Xuyuan's mind. Yuankai can clearly point out that Xuyuan wanted Lulu to happily see flowers once, and she personally said that she enjoyed seeing cherry blossoms, so that objective at least has been achieved. This judgement belongs to Yuankai's external position and is not upgraded by the narration into unquestionable fact; Xuyuan answers with "she said she enjoyed them" or more directly that he does not know whether she was sincere. Here, Xuyuan's doubt about the truth of Lulu's words is not made the discussion object of `abductive reasoning / feeling`; the dispute about the precision of abductive intuition is triggered separately when he later makes a life-scale prediction and uses this ability as the basis for that prediction. The conversation then returns to him another unanswered question: how to arrange life after the course ends, and whether London still has reasons independent of Lulu. Yuankai can suggest that he keep walking, but does not give him the geographic answer.

"Xuyuan is very sad" is not completed through crying, choking or narrative naming. A more appropriate set of manifestations is: he precisely retains Lulu's return-journey line `I really enjoyed seeing the cherry blossoms today.`; when Yuankai summarises it as "she was happy", he narrows the object back to "she said she liked the cherry blossoms"; he can still say very long and accurate things, but pauses slightly longer before simple questions, or after Yuankai checks the time still finishes a long sentence that could have been closed earlier. The text only needs two or three of these signals; stacking them all would instead become the author proving he is sad. The key is not that he suddenly loses analytic ability, but that this ability begins building an excessively large future model around a rejection that has just happened.

Yuankai does not mock Xuyuan here. He can still question the life-scale prediction, but does not gain scene advantage by grabbing a witty line or compressing Xuyuan's sentence into a joke. When Xuyuan says the unbalanced, almost unreasonable conditions for waiting, the mild ridiculousness comes only from the scale of these words and the calm way they are spoken. Yuankai only pauses, confirms or gently questions. Sharper internet memes are suitable only for a conversation candidate years later, when the two-person relationship and what Xuyuan can bear at that time are different.

The magnolia beside the campus church is currently the location of Xuyuan and Yuankai's conversation. Because psychological access has not yet recovered, "thinking where to go" is not written as readable interiority; it can be shown through him checking a route, naming a direction, standing up or beginning to walk west. The magnolia provides a still point between the short enclosed return Tube shot and the long walk, and also lets London spring flowers outside Kew begin entering the post-rejection visual sequence.

Xuyuan can first give here an extreme life-scale conclusion: not that he will completely never like anyone again, but that he does not think he will meet another girl in his lifetime who makes him like her to the same degree. This conclusion first comes from his `abductive reasoning / feeling`: past experience, many small observations not listed one by one and the current feeling together produce a very strong holistic judgement.

The line "he does not think he can immediately stop liking her because it would be convenient" is no longer used: "convenient" does not clarify whether it means reducing pain, lowering life complexity or avoiding waiting, and would also put the work's moral defence into the character's mouth. Similarly, Xuyuan should not explicitly distinguish "respecting the existing boundary" from "whether the feeling has ended"; the former is shown through his later action of not intervening in Lulu's existing relationship, and does not become his self-testimony. "Waiting" remains an active candidate. After the exit conditions, Yuankai does not predict "that will be a long time", but only says `None of those has a date.` The slight absurdity still comes from Xuyuan delivering, in an extremely calm tone, a set of conditions that could swallow many years, rather than from the friend actively establishing a waiting proposition for him or turning him into a joke.

Under the church magnolia, Yuankai needs to preserve two independent propositions: rejection is not overturned by happiness, and happiness is not erased by rejection.
