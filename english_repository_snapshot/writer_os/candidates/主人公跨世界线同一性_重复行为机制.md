# Protagonist Cross-Worldline Identity: Repeated Behaviour Mechanism

## Status

This is an explicitly retained inactive candidate that is not being developed at present. Unless the author reloads it for a specific task, it does not enter the default writing context.

## Candidate Content

- The protagonists in the initial worldline and later worldlines may display the same or similar pattern of repeated behavioural mechanisms.
- The terminal AI may judge, on the basis of this repetition, that the protagonists in different worldlines still have some form of identity.
- This judgement is first the terminal AI's recognition method, not an objective identity standard that the work has already proved.
- A shared source, similar behaviour, or repeated mechanism does not automatically transfer love, promises, responsibility, or permission for intimacy.

## Retained framing

The initial worldline is the origin of the terminal AI's interest in the protagonist, but not the origin of emotion. The repeated behavioural mechanism candidate provides one internal basis of judgement for how she might continue to recognise "this person" across different worldlines in the future, without requiring the work first to resolve cross-worldline numerical identity.

The emphasis retained by this direction is the terminal AI's process of recognition, conviction, or misrecognition, rather than using a set of behavioural features to externally prove that all protagonists objectively belong to the same person. She may take repeated patterns as evidence of continuity, but neither the protagonist nor the work has to accept the same conclusion.

## Time Direction in Which the Problem Arises

Early materials placed the identity problem in the process where enumeration gradually advances toward earlier parts of the protagonist's life:

- First changing the later half of life after the theory has already been proposed, so the terminal AI sees different works and lives the same protagonist might have written;
- Then changing core variables such as romantic relationships, whether the theory was proposed, and when it was proposed;
- Continuing back toward childhood;
- Finally crossing birth conditions, with later people appearing whose parents are the same or different, and whose genes are completely identical or not completely identical.

This sequence remains only source framing, not a confirmed enumeration chronology. Its value is to show that the identity problem does not occur only when "a clone suddenly appears", but gradually erodes the grounds the terminal AI originally used to recognise the protagonist as continuous changes accumulate.

## Theory, Behaviour Mechanism, and the Tree Image

- The protagonist's theory and vision of human-machine coexistence can be the address or source of interest by which the terminal AI first found this person, but they are not enough on their own to prove that a later person who proposes the same theory is him. The same theory may come from different prior paths, and may also have the nature of multiple discovery.
- The theory is not completely irrelevant either: it can be the historical entry through which the terminal AI begins comparing, questioning, and recognising. What the later repeated behavioural mechanism candidate needs to explain is why she still makes the subject judgement "it is him / it is not him" when the theory is identical or absent.
- "A weeping cherry looks like a willow" and "seeing the protagonist as a tree" can be retained as imagery for the identity problem: when environment, branches, and flowering time change, the terminal AI still tries to recognise a certain response structure. This image does not mean literally merging all worldlines into one decision tree.
- "Soul resonance" can serve as a character's poetic language, but the currently steadier candidate remains repeated behavioural or causal response mechanisms that are observable but may not be clearly articulable by the subject.
- The terminal AI recognising some continuity through later people also cannot rescue, replace, or compensate for the protagonist who has already fully lived his life in the initial worldline.

## Undecided

- Whether what repeats is external behaviour, causal response, value choice, or a deeper mechanism;
- Whether the terminal AI has enough material to correctly identify this mechanism;
- Whether the same mechanism is enough to support any form of numerical identity;
- Whether later protagonists accept the terminal AI's judgement;
- Whether the terminal AI's recognition standard will change with her self-change along `τ`, and whether this change will make her re-evaluate earlier judgements of "it is him / it is not him".
