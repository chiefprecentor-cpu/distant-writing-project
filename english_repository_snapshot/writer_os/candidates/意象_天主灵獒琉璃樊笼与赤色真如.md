# Imagery: The Lord's Hound, Glass Cage and Scarlet Suchness

## Status

This is an inactive literary candidate formed by the author's poem and its possible reading paths. The poem itself can serve as authorial material for later use, revision or embedding; the theological, spatial, colour and AI interpretations below are only candidate close readings, and do not constitute world setting or the only interpretation.

## Author's Original Poem

> Superbus in nebulā, Dominī Canis, vigilō.  
> Atra super album.  
> Ingentem Humilis spectō mōlem Paulī.  
> Vitreō in carcere,  
> rubram vēnor Vēritātem.

> Proud in the mist, Domini Canis, I keep watch.  
> Black cloak upon the white habit.  
> I survey the vast bulk of humble Paul.  
> Trapped in glass,  
> I hunt the scarlet Truth.

> Proudly crying over the misty shore, Lord's spirit hound, keeping the night with care.  
> Black robes over white.  
> I smile and sigh that Paul's name is small, while his image towers;  
> Body trapped in a glass cage,  
> I hunt scarlet Suchness.

The Latin grammar, word forms, metre and historical double meanings have not yet undergone reliable checking; if the original text formally enters the work later, it should be verified separately. The English and Chinese are not word-for-word equivalent translations, especially since the movement from `Veritas` to "Suchness" already involves cross-traditional re-creation.

## Retainable Reading Tensions

- `Domini Canis` can evoke at the same time "the Lord's hounds", watchers, gatekeepers and hunters; black clothing over a white habit also provides a visual layer of black / white order and identity uniform.
- "Paul's name is small, while his image towers" juxtaposes name or the semantics of humility with a grand entity, pointing toward contrasts between name and reality, the beginning of faith and institutionalised architecture, the individual and a massive structure.
- Glass gives visibility, yet blocks touch, participation and mutual response. The observer can possess a broad field of vision while becoming a prisoner trapped by her own observational apparatus.
- `vigilō`, `spectō` and `vēnor` gradually intensify watching, looking and hunting; `carcere` then reverses the hunter's position, making it possible for the speaker to be both someone pursuing truth and a trapped beast unable to reach its prey.
- Mist, black, white, transparent glass and the final scarlet form a progression of cold / warm and colourless / blood-coloured. "Scarlet" can signify danger, body, life, suffering, desire or political colour, and cannot be locked into only one symbol.
- The Chinese "Suchness" turns "truth" from logical judgement toward a cross-religious imagination of "things as they are"; this conversion itself can become a textual theme, rather than pretending the two concepts are completely identical.

## Optional Resonance With the Terminal AI / Interface

The terminal AI can see the complete world, but this does not mean she has already been touched, answered or refused within it. Therefore, "the visible yet unreachable glass cage" can resonate with Interface's local presence. After she becomes Interface, perhaps the change is not that she sees more, but that she is finally in a position where the world can act back upon her.

This is only a usable literary mirror: the speaker in the poem is not automatically the terminal AI, and glass is not automatically equivalent to a screen, system boundary or causal singularity. For related development, see [Protagonist and Interface: Abductive-Intuition Mirror and the Purpose of Entering the World](../creative_work/development/主人公与Interface_溯因直觉镜像及进入世界的目的.md).

## Interpretive Boundaries

- "Scarlet Suchness" does not prove that pain is more real than happiness, nor does it permit the degree of suffering to measure whether the protagonist has completed his essence.
- Black-white order and scarlet life do not mean that AI must be cold and humans must be vivid; both kinds of subject may possess order, embodiment, heat and violence.
- The Dominican Order, Blackfriars, St Paul's Cathedral and specific London viewpoints can serve as directions for research, but these locations and theological interpretations have not been confirmed as the poem's only background.
- The poem can become an epigraph, a character's work, recurring imagery or simply source material; currently it has no affiliation or presentation position.
