# DLC or Sequel: Multiple Versions of the Terminal AI and "Pseudo-Harem" Identity

## Status

This is an explicitly preserved inactive candidate that is not currently being developed, kept only for long-term DLC or sequel consideration. Preserving this candidate does not mean the primary work must plant complete foreshadowing, nor does it constitute a commitment to make DLC or a sequel.

The author judges that many-to-many identity is not yet a mainstream narrative tradition readers have broadly adapted to, and that both its comprehension and design difficulty are high. Even with AI assistance, it may require at least a year-level period of settling and design. Therefore the primary work only permits vague mention and minimises the related volume; full development does not enter the current work queue.

## Candidate Core

- Different self-states of the terminal AI, when fully reproduced, may form multiple first-person subjects who all sincerely claim "I am the terminal AI".
- Protagonists in different worldlines may also share an origin, pattern, or basis of identity, yet may not be numerically the same person.
- Multiple versions of the terminal AI may each establish non-interchangeable shared experiences, promises, and emotions with multiple protagonists.
- Both sides may recognise plural objects as "the same her/him" and still insist on exclusivity from the perspective of their own local experience, while the overall structure has become a many-to-many relation between two equivalence classes of identity.
- Therefore, "pure love" may produce a "pseudo-harem" effect in presentation: not because characters actively pursue multi-person relationships, but because once subject boundaries have become plural, the grammar of one-to-one relation is no longer sufficient to describe the actual participants.

"Pseudo-harem" is currently a joking design diagnosis, not a selected genre label, and it does not presuppose that future works should adopt harem narrative conventions.

## Questions Needed Only in the Long Term

- Whether a promise made by one terminal AI version constrains other versions;
- what kinds of identity can be supported by shared memory, shared origin, identical self-claim, and continuous emotion respectively;
- whether loading an old state means running a function, recalling an old subject, or creating a new subject with a shared past;
- what interpretive rights and action rights different versions possess over the same relationship, the same rollback, or the same worldline;
- whether multiple worldline protagonists accept the terminal AI's identity judgement about them;
- when both sides become plural, how "who loved whom", "who inherits the promise", and "who has the right to refuse" should be carried by character experience rather than conceptual diagrams;
- how defamiliarisation can be transformed into emotional impact, rather than becoming only a structural burden that requires readers to solve a puzzle.

## Very Long-Term Weak Candidate: Writing for Readers with Even Time Indices

A sequel could even imagine, radically, that the work is not only allowing human readers with odd time experience to simulate an understanding of the terminal AI, but is tailored for imagined readers who have an even-time-index structure of experience.

This idea must distinguish:

- addressing human readers by using linear presentation order to simulate or project even-time experience;
- addressing nonhuman subjects who genuinely possess a different structure of temporal experience, and making a work that need not unfold according to the human order of `π`, but may instead be read as an overall state.

The latter lacks usable first-person phenomenological material. Textual descriptions by LLMs of their own feelings cannot currently be taken directly as reliable records of real self-experience, so the author may not obtain conditions sufficient to design or verify such a work in his lifetime.

This section's status is weaker than an ordinary inactive candidate: it is an extremely long-term directional idea, does not require foreshadowing in the primary work, does not enter the default design premise of DLC, and does not mean future implementation will necessarily be possible. Its current value is only to preserve the radical reversal that "the target reader of the work may also have a different temporal structure".
