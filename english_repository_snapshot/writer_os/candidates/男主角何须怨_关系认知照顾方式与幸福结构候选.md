# Xuyuan (male protagonist): relationship cognition, care style, and happiness structure candidate

## Status

This is the current inactive development candidate for Xuyuan. It supplements intimate relationships, communication, family, and happiness structure, and does not overwrite the already confirmed [egalitarianism, responsibility ethics, and self-risk](../creative_work/story_state/主人公_平等主义责任伦理与自我风险.md) or [abductive reasoning and seismographic sensitivity](../creative_work/story_state/主人公_溯因推理与地震仪式敏感.md).

## Cognitive structure in relationships

Xuyuan is afraid of uncertainty, and therefore checks weather, routes, word meanings, and transport; but when facing the relationship status most likely to destroy hope, he avoids the simplest confirmation.

This can form a stable set of contrasts:

- seeing the `First Date` route on the Kew website, then backing out because he does not dare name the outing as a date, and finally walking too much inside the gardens;
- not asking whether Lulu is single, yet building a complete narrative around "she is about to leave, so now is the last chance";
- assuming he must leave the United Kingdom after graduation, yet never really reading through possible work routes;
- being able to infer the future of civilisation from microscopic signs, while actively leaving data gaps around his own desire.

The two hidden variables that appear in succession after the Kew rejection therefore form a mirrored pair:

1. He assumes Lulu is single, but she is already in a relationship; one door closes.
2. He assumes he must leave, but later learns that a career route still exists; another door opens.

His growth is not mistaking the second door for the reopening of the first, but acknowledging that London, career, family, and Lulu are different problems.

## Projection and real liking

"Self-projection" does not automatically make liking false. The current character problem is:

> Love first passes through one's own desire and imagination;
> after projection meets the resistance of a real other, is he willing to revise himself?

The reasons Xuyuan is first attracted can be ungenerous and honest at the same time:

- Lulu's appearance matches the "beautiful girl" defined by himself;
- when spending time with her one-on-one, his self-monitoring briefly stops, and he gains a peace close to "gulls and egrets forgetting calculation";
- her attention to ordinary small things pulls his world back from concepts to the senses;
- repeated accidental meetings lead him to further interpret real comfort as fate.

He does not use education, intellectual output, or social ranking as filters for romance. When a friend asks about his interests, he can answer:

> "My interest is beautiful girls. The three dimensions of beauty, youth, and girlhood are all interpreted by me."

In a casual conversation about *My Youth Romantic Comedy Is Wrong, As I Expected*, he sees netizens mock Yukino Yukinoshita as "third-rate" because she attends Chiba University, using university rankings against her. Xuyuan does not understand why educational sorting should enter the question of whom one likes:

> "I like a person; I am not hiring a research assistant."

This joke grounds his anti-elitist view of romantic selection in a concrete reaction: he himself can study extremely grand theories, but refuses to rank the person he likes by education, intellectual output, or social status.

This does not mean he looks down on Lulu's thought. It only means intellect is not an entry criterion; after the relationship continues, unwillingness to understand her field and choices would likewise not count as truly approaching her.

A friend can use a "disillusionment stress test" to force him to acknowledge the unknown:

> "If you really get to know her later, what if part of her disillusions you?"
>
> He hesitated for a moment.
>
> "Then I still want to know. I cannot spend my whole life liking only the version of her I imagined just to avoid disillusionment."

The hesitation matters more than immediately promising "I will love her no matter what." The latter may still be continuing to project onto an unknown object.

## Filtered self-presentation

Xuyuan worries that Lulu will likewise become disillusioned after learning the real him:

- he can say that he is interested in technology and society, but leave the long passage on AI, data governance, and the future of civilisation in a draft, afraid that Lulu will think he is too nerdy;
- he will say his favourite is *Steins;Gate*, and temporarily not mention that another anime he likes very much is the harem work *Date A Live*, with its stereotype of a flirtatious otaku audience;
- when asked further about other works he likes, he may also think of *Neon Genesis Evangelion*, then treat it as another self-presentation item needing review because the answer is too mainstream and because he has recently seen mockery of its viewers;
- another worldline or another conversation can make him remember that he also likes *Violet Evergarden*. Different works surface in different contexts; he does not need a fixed list of favourites that is repeated item by item in every line;
- he is not lying, but only offering the "approved version" during first acquaintance.

This keeps the information deficit symmetrical on both sides: Xuyuan cannot only interrogate whether Lulu fits his imagination; he must also acknowledge that the self she currently knows is equally incomplete.

Xuyuan mainly encounters anime through Chinese titles, Chinese subtitles, and Chinese discussions; his familiarity with the content of a work may be much higher than his ability to align its Chinese name, Japanese original title, and English/international release title in real time. In an English conversation, when he encounters a transparent title he can first translate it in his head; when he encounters a title that does not correspond at all across markets, he may search on the spot and only then realise that he has in fact seen it. This is a difference between media pathway and second-language retrieval, and should not write him as unfamiliar with the work.

Use this difference at most once per scene, and do not develop it into a fixed verbal tic. Follow-up triggers that can currently be retained include: *ERASED* / *The Town Where Only I Am Missing*, *Whisper of the Heart*, *Princess Tutu*, and *Kids on the Slope*. *Porco Rosso* is an Italian international title used in English contexts, and it also does not directly share sound with the Chinese title *Red Pig*; because Hayao Miyazaki has multiple war-related works, merely adding `Miyazaki` is still insufficient. The current priority is to help Xuyuan recognise it through the minimal plot cue "the pilot is a pig", without needing to take out his phone.

The literary association around *Violet Evergarden* is currently retained only as a connection Xuyuan might make and still needs to examine, not as a fact about the work's source. In episode 7 of the anime, Oscar Webster is a playwright who has lost his wife and his ill daughter Olivia, and Violet helps him complete a children's play written for his daughter; see the [official anime episode 7 introduction](https://tv.violet-evergarden.jp/story/) and the [wife-and-daughter information in a retrospective on the work](https://www.crunchyroll.com/news/deep-dives/2020/7/14/loving-words-will-always-watch-over-you-a-violet-evergarden-tribute). The British literary figure closest to the author's existing memory may be John Milton: his second wife Katherine Woodcock died in 1658, and their infant daughter died in the same year; after losing his sight, his writing also long relied on dictation and amanuenses. For biographical facts, see the [Poetry Foundation introduction to Milton](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/john-milton). There is currently no reliable source proving that Oscar intentionally alludes to Milton, so if the main text uses it, it can only be a literary analogy Xuyuan makes from similar experience.

### War anime and future battlefield candidate

Xuyuan can clearly like *86--Eighty-Six*, especially attending to how it splits "unmanned war" into multiple institutional and technical problems: Legion are autonomous unmanned combat machines; the Republic packages human pilots who have been stripped of personhood and citizenship as its own "unmanned weapons", using the narrative of `zero casualties` to conceal sacrifice. The official synopsis for the English first volume clearly establishes this contrast; see [Yen Press volume 1](https://yenpress.com/titles/9781975303129-86-eighty-six-vol-1-light-novel).

He will also be interested in the posthuman boundary through which automated weapons absorb human brain structures, memory, or intelligence. The official anime mechanical description explicitly states that certain high-level Legion units absorbed the brain of a former human character; see the [official anime Legion mechanical page](https://anime-86.com/mechanic/enemy/). Xuyuan can therefore organise his reading through `wetware`, remnants of personhood, weapons inheriting human memory, and the subjectivity of war machines. `wetware` is his conceptualising term; it does not pretend to be the work's official classification, nor does it treat the setting as a direct prediction of real future battlefields.

This preference can connect to his long-term interest in future battlefields, unmanned operations, automated weapons, human labour erased by institutions, and human-machine subject boundaries. *Patlabor 2: The Movie* and *Mobile Suit Gundam 0080: War in the Pocket* are still retained as possible war anime he may discuss, but the author currently thinks they are too mainstream to prioritise for the function of "accidentally exposing Xuyuan's deeper interests". Different worldlines can draw out different works because the questions differ; every war-anime conversation does not need to repeat the same set of titles.

In the player's first-seen line, the car journey only needs Xuyuan to say that *86--Eighty-Six* does not initially look like it has much conspicuous high technology, but that its design has real potential; this sentence first exposes the direction of interest, without unfolding Legion, wetware, or future battlefields on the spot. Other worldlines can open those mechanisms according to the specific question.

## Communication mechanism candidate

Xuyuan's communication state changes with the situation:

- introverted: group settings drain him quickly, and he often speaks less when there are four or more people;
- he lacks experience with certain relationship scenes, and does not know how they usually proceed;
- the more he cares about the other person, the more he monitors his own performance;
- in one-on-one or three-person conversation, when the topic interests him, he may instead speak too much;
- real-time retrieval in a second language has a delay, but his reading, writing, and complex-concept ability are strong;
- even when using Chinese, he is not guaranteed to be fluent in unfamiliar relationship situations.

A common swing can be: rehearsing an opening line -> none of it being usable on site -> filling the gap with questions and jokes -> immediately doubting himself after speaking -> replaying it again and again after returning.

Language lag can be shown through local mutual help, not grammatical errors: Xuyuan briefly cannot remember `pomegranate`, and can only describe "a red fruit with many seeds inside"; Lulu thinks for a few seconds and supplies the word. In another topic, Xuyuan may provide a plant or cultural term she does not know. Their knowledge is local on both sides, and neither thereby becomes a fixed teacher.

## Three stages of joking

Xuyuan's absurd humour is not only character decoration:

1. Shared amusement: strange buildings, curry and rice, and so on really do make both sides laugh.
2. Covering and filling: he does not dare say the issue he truly cares about, so he grabs onto anime memes and objects in front of him to keep talking.
3. Emergency mechanism after impact: after rejection, his inner language loses power, but his mouth restarts at higher density.

The confession passage can therefore appear as follows: before rejection, the inner monologue is extremely dense; after rejection, the inner monologue suddenly disappears, while Xuyuan still explains, supplements, and tells one failed joke; after parting, even external language stops. The loss of control can be visible, but should not be praised as "loving too deeply".

## Service-oriented care and boundary learning

Drinks, coats, seats, snacks, routes, and wanting to make better rice all belong to the same service-oriented expression. It has goodwill, and it can also easily become answering again on someone else's behalf.

Recommended growth line:

- early: repeatedly confirming, assuming that asking one more time means caring a little more;
- middle: asking once and accepting the answer once, even if he still wants to offer help;
- late: distinguishing when to offer, when to wait for a request, and when to let the other person decide independently.

For example, in the greenhouse he actually asks only once whether she needs him to get her coat. A few minutes later he wants to ask again, sees that she has already handled it, and swallows the words. This small action is more effective than declaring "he learned to respect boundaries".

## Curry and rice

"Do you like curry or rice?" can become a miniature model of this care style:

- Xuyuan asks whether she likes curry or rice, and Lulu says she likes curry;
- Xuyuan has already begun calculating in his head how to make the rice better;
- sincere desire, premature fantasy of living together, and wanting to change her preference coexist.

Early on, what he thinks is "I will make her like rice"; a later return to the motif may not be her changing her answer, but him understanding that curry can already be carried by his rice.

Different routes can return to it differently:

- non-romance line: this meal is never made for her, becoming an unrealised possibility that does not need compensation;
- Lulu line: she still prefers curry, but is willing to add one more spoonful of his rice;
- China line: Xuyuan first genuinely learns to cook for his family, and imagined care becomes a life skill.

## Protective desire, ability, and consent

Because future risks are rising, Xuyuan will want to stay beside Lulu, while also worrying that he lacks the ability to protect her; at the same time, he feels responsibility for his parents' later years, his hometown, and public undertakings in China.

This set of contradictions must pass through four questions:

- whether he is near her;
- whether he has genuinely useful ability;
- whether she needs and consents to his protection;
- what responsibilities he bears toward other people and communities.

Physical proximity is not equal to protection, and protection cannot become surveillance, concealment of risk, or substitute decision-making. The more mature direction is not finding the single answer to "which person must I guard beside", but obtaining concrete ability, taking responsibility when others allow it, and acknowledging that she also has the right to choose risk.

## Levels of happiness

What Xuyuan pursues is not a total quantity of happiness that can be mutually exchanged:

- inner-circle self: himself, family, lover;
- bridge relationships: friends;
- macro subjects: nation, civilisation, intelligent life.

The happiness of family and lover is often directly included in "my happiness"; friends sit between extension of the self and independent others; macro happiness is sometimes the infrastructure that protects the inner circle, sometimes a substitute and anaesthetic after private loss, and it also does have intrinsic value that is not fully instrumental.

This can be called the working understanding of "extended egoism + conditional universalism", not a theoretical name the character would self-report.

### Two key tests

1. If Lulu's happiness does not include Xuyuan, is that happiness still real happiness he is willing to protect?
2. If the future of civilisation cannot compensate for his unrealised private future, does he still acknowledge that happiness not belonging to him has value?

He can say both:

> I hope she is happy.
> I will also not pretend that her not choosing me does not hurt.

Acknowledging non-substitutability does not imply that other happiness has no value.

## "Fuel for the era" and its shadow

"Since I cannot obtain personal happiness, I will make myself fuel for the era" can be a long-standing character flaw, and one that may also be repaired:

- since I cannot be happy, at least make myself useful;
- since no one needs me as a lover, let history need me as a tool;
- as long as the crisis is large enough, private loss need not be heard.

The deepest risk is not sacrifice, but that he begins to need the crisis to keep existing. If the world no longer needs anyone to burn, he loses the way of proving existence through "usefulness".

Romantic success cannot automatically repair this either. He may still say "as long as she can live, it is fine if I disappear", and Lulu can refuse to be treated as a justification for self-destruction.

Currently recognisable macro-responsibility candidates include:

| Private happiness | Bearing macro responsibility | Working shorthand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unrealised | Accept | Fuel line |
| Unrealised | Refuse | Refusing paradise line |
| Realised | Accept | Integration line |
| Realised | Refuse | Closed garden line |

These are only problem axes. They do not require all four to be produced, and do not imply that private happiness has only romance as its form.

"Refusing paradise" must be Xuyuan's choice after long-term psychological evolution, not "a girl rejected him, so the world is destroyed". His inaction can at most affect one disaster-mitigation effort, a future possibility, or the handoff of a solution; Lulu is only a symbol used while he organises his reasons, and responsibility still belongs to him.

## Quiet ripples: endogenous repair candidate

Xuyuan does not have to choose only between "obtaining intense happiness" and "becoming fuel for the era". One candidate answer gradually formed by himself is:

> Life is not only loving or hating someone very intensely.
> There are also many quiet days, with a small ripple now and then.
> Those days, too, I want to cherish properly in the future.

The sources of thought can include Epicurean, Stoic, and Daoist backgrounds, but the main text does not need to name them. The key is that behaviour happens first: cooking, walking, accompanying family, drinking with friends, saying goodbye to Lulu in an ordinary way, noticing white flowers he had not seen before; only at the end does he find language for the change.

Quietness is not deathly stillness, giving up the mission, or replacing loss with small daily pleasures. Lulu cannot be replaced, and civilisational responsibility cannot be replaced either; but ordinary days already have value in themselves.

## Friend candidate

For the concentrated character base that can be reused across scenes, see [Yuankai: character, forms of address, and cross-line bridge-function candidate](元凯_人物称呼与跨线桥梁作用候选.md).

Friends sit between private happiness and macro responsibility, and can take on:

- dismantling Xuyuan's projection and fate narrative;
- providing visa, work, or action possibilities, without deciding for him;
- pushing him back from a passive state into one autonomous action during a civilisational crisis;
- reminding him that friends themselves are part of private life, not "maintenance personnel supplied by the era".

The friend's ability to understand and handle complex problems is very strong; his intelligence, judgement, action capacity, and perseverance are all at a high level. He likes knowingly taking two mutually incompatible theories and deliberately splicing them into jokes that seem like "conceptual accidents". For example: "As a left-wing accelerationist, my lifelong ideal is to realise the Dark Enlightenment."

These jokes are built on his accurate grasp of conceptual conflicts. In serious action he can usually provide high-quality judgement, and he still has blind spots of his own; his field, verbal habits, errors, and action line all need to work in later design.

## Education and action-capacity candidate

- Xuyuan can read English Literature as an undergraduate; his master's programme is provisionally set as an interdisciplinary KCL programme, such as `AI, Data and Culture` or `Digital Culture and AI`.
- The programme can be co-taught by Digital Humanities, Law, Informatics, and so on; the specific name and duration remain `TBD`.
- He does not need to become a computer genius or destined saviour. His advantage is forming macro hypotheses from micro anomalies; abductive reasoning and intuition suggest directions, but do not automatically supply evidence.
- The civilisational-scale crisis requires him to make one non-transferable choice only in a few narrow lines; others remain responsible for evidence, technology, and execution.
- "Approaching the vortex" does not require a mysterious core profession. Public lectures in London, technical deployment, institutional conflict, finance, and ordinary labour can all become observational interfaces.

## Must not be automatically inferred

- Xuyuan's dullness or nervousness in relationship scenes does not exempt him from the responsibility to respect boundaries after rejection.
- Sincere care does not prove that the other person needs, likes, or should reciprocate that care.
- Projection is not equal to false feeling, and is not equal to the work needing to preserve the projection for him.
- Being able to infer macro problems does not mean he understands people better in relationships.
- He needs to involve his family in family choices, and also needs to include his own future within overall welfare.
- Mission, love, family, and friends cannot be written as automatic medicines that repair another gap.
- "Quiet ripples" is a candidate life capacity, not a completed personality arc.
