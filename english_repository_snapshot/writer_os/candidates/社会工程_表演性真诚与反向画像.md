# Social Engineering, Performative Sincerity and Reverse Profiling

## Status

This is a conceptual candidate that does not belong to any worldline and is not currently being developed. It continues the environmental change in [Datafication: Indexical Feedback Loops and Granular Futures](数据化_索引性反馈回路与颗粒化未来.md) into dense social engineering, and preserves the possibility that, after realising they are being modelled, characters respond through "performative sincerity" and reverse profiling. This response can be limited resistance, protective disguise, submission to surveillance, or long-term self-discipline; these do not have to exclude one another.

This candidate is related to the privacy threat produced by the pseudo-Laplace demon and to social engineering that has not yet been developed, but this thematic relation does not restore the omniscient capacity of the old PLD, and does not confirm that any institution or terminal AI in the current story has already adopted the mechanisms below.

## Core Candidate

When behavioural records, content interventions, character responses and model updates become dense enough, the system no longer merely profiles or predicts characters from a distance. On the basis of existing representations, it can choose what a character will see next, when they will see it, in what order they will see it, and which actions become smoother or more difficult; it then uses the character's response to adjust the next round of intervention.

The system is therefore carrying out two activities at once: it changes the environment according to existing expectations, and continues learning expectations from the changed environment. It does not first need to form a psychological theory that humans can fully state. It only needs to gradually acquire a form of computable operational knowledge:

> For this person, or this kind of person, in this kind of relation, time and situation, after this content, sequence, prompt or friction is applied, which responses become more likely?

This "anticipatory knowledge" may be difficult to fully summarise in natural language, yet it can be used directly by the model for selection and optimisation. It does not have to reveal the essence of a character, and may also be wrong, partial or out of date; its power comes from its ability to enter the next action, not only from whether it constitutes a complete explanation of the character.

## "Dense" Does Not Only Mean a Large Amount of Data

- **Observational density**: the system continuously obtains granular events such as clicks, dwell time, skips, returns, time, device and relations, rather than relying only on occasional questionnaires or election results.
- **Representational density**: many explicit and learned features are used jointly, and an individual or group does not have to be described only by a small number of demographic labels.
- **Intervention density**: recommendations, ranking, prompts, notifications, prices, defaults, visibility and friction can change frequently; each change is small, but they constantly rearrange the local choice environment.
- **Feedback density**: responses after intervention quickly flow back, allowing the system to update scores, rankings, models or strategies over shorter cycles.
- **Population density**: the intervention-response relations of many individuals can be modelled jointly, so the way one person is treated is also affected by similar populations, relational networks and group changes.

High density does not guarantee that the system is correct or omnipotent. It means that the system has opportunities to repeatedly probe and adjust at finer scales of time, target and action than traditional propaganda.

## regression, Recommendation and Iterative Optimisation

If the system needs to estimate a continuous outcome, such as dwell time, response intensity, conversion probability or post-intervention score change, dense intervention-response records can support regression or related response modelling. Other goals may also use classification, ranking, recommendation, experimental comparison, strategy selection or business rules; the whole chain cannot be summarised only through regression.

Social engineering also does not have to take a clear "prediction" as its final output. Content recommendation itself is an intervention into the information environment: even if the system is only optimising the next item of content, the next prompt or the next segment of sequence, it may still continuously affect what characters attend to, what they repeatedly encounter, what they mistake for being common, and when they enter a certain emotional or action state.

In a closed loop, "the system treats responses as validation of representation" has a double character: the response may support the model's original judgement, and it may also be partly caused by the environment the system has just arranged. If the system conflates the two, it will rerecord behaviour it helped produce as evidence that the character was originally like this. Even if the model can continuously improve a certain operational metric, it does not automatically follow that it has obtained unconditional human truth or complete causal understanding.

## Difference From Broad Propaganda and Public Opinion Manipulation

Traditional political propaganda or mass public opinion manipulation usually relies on relatively limited, shareable and repeatable information objects: the same slogan, broadcast, poster, news narrative, or campaign aimed at a broad population. It can of course also segment audiences, test and adjust; the difference here is not "the past was completely unified, and only now has targeted communication appeared", but that the following capacities can become finer, faster and harder to observe together at the same time:

- different characters or small groups receive different content, sequences, frequencies and frictions;
- many small interventions accumulate together, without needing a decisive propaganda text that can be pointed to;
- the system can use combined features for which humans have no ready-made names, rather than relying only on public identity categories;
- modelling, intervention, measurement and optimisation proceed continuously in the same loop, without waiting until a campaign has ended before evaluation;
- affected people have difficulty comparing the environments they each see, making it harder to form a common object of manipulation and refute it publicly.

This capacity may be more effective than broad propaganda, or it may only pursue a narrow metric in more refined ways. The concrete effect still depends on data, goals, organisational authority, intervention scope, character responses and counter-capacities; it cannot be written as necessarily successful mind control.

## Choice Topology, Actual Freedom and Exit Capacity

The object of dense social engineering is not only the content characters receive. It can also be the choice topology characters face: which paths appear, whether they appear first or later, how long they require waiting, what price they require, whether they will be recognised by other institutions, and which paths remain reachable after a choice. Without issuing a ban, the system can make certain options that formally exist gradually become materially unworkable.

This makes "the character can still avoid using the system" a claim that needs concrete testing. If deleting an account means losing transport, work, medical care, education, benefits or addressable relations with others, exit is only a formal permission and does not necessarily constitute actual freedom. Whether a character can remain opaque also depends on whether non-datafied processes, alternative identities, offline records, portable data, maintenance personnel and other fallback capacity exist.

Classification as a pass therefore also opens more than an information landscape. It may open or close services, qualifications, prices and social relations, so what the character infiltrates is a differentially arranged world of practical possibility. Actively making oneself readable can seek recognition and resources, and can also hand more of life to the same loop; refusing readability may protect internal space while also preventing necessary support from arriving. This does not presume that either choice is naturally freer.

## Performative Sincerity

"Performative sincerity" deliberately preserves contradiction and irony. It can refer to sincere content presented strategically, and it can also refer to a character performing an unreal appearance in order to seem harmless, credible or aligned with expectations; under long-term surveillance, the two may also become mixed to the point that the character can no longer draw a clear boundary.

### Original Conception

> The result I have observed and predicted is that, when people realise that their past words and actions, and even unconscious physiological responses, may be traced back by an omnipresent eye, society enters a state of chronic anxiety, and learned helplessness begins to spread like a virus. Also, since the past cannot be hidden, people begin to manage their present and future records. Interpersonal relations become an endless performance. Everyone tries hard to control expressions, speech and even physiological indicators, striving every second to appear harmless or to appear as they want others to see them, while inside they are full of fear and distrust. I call this "performative sincerity". Genuine communication is suppressed, emotional connections break, and social atomisation deepens. Colleagues, friends and even relatives are separated by an invisible wall.

The "sincerity" in this original formulation does not guarantee consistency between inside and outside. It is closer to a credible appearance that must be continuously displayed to an observer. A character may sincerely choose to let some part of themself be seen, and may also hide danger, conflict or vulnerability, performing only a harmless version that the system and others are willing to accept.

It contains at least three meanings that can coexist:

- **Sincere performance**: the character consciously amplifies or arranges values they really believe in, in order to compete for the system's interpretive authority over them.
- **Performance of sincerity**: the character disguises a credible, harmless or expected response, so that external observers cannot judge their interiority from visible records.
- **Sincerity shaped by performance**: after long-term control of language, expression and bodily response, the original strategy gradually changes the character's habits, relations and self-understanding, and sincerity and disguise no longer have a clear boundary.

Therefore, performative sincerity is not only active algorithmic resistance. It may also be adaptation when there is nowhere to retreat, internalised surveillance discipline, or learned helplessness: the character appears to have gained local control over records, but at the cost of constant self-checking, inability to communicate naturally and no longer trusting others.

## Reverse Profiling

Characters can also infer what the system currently takes them to be from recommended content, hidden options, recurring prompts, abnormal friction or changes in treatment. They are not only profiled by the system; they are also profiling the system's profile of them:

`system representation of character -> character infers this representation from interventions -> character strategically produces visible responses -> system updates representation and intervention`

Reverse profiling may help a character correct, disturb or exploit system expectations, and it may also make them increasingly reliant on a model they cannot directly see to understand themself. Performative sincerity does not require this reverse profile to be accurate; as long as the character believes that the omnipresent eye will trace the present back in the future, imagined observation is enough to change behaviour. If individuals share the differences they each encounter, they may also reassemble originally private, fragmented interventions into a public object that can be argued over.

## Second-Order population mapping of Performers

When characters begin performing in relation to their profiles, `algorithms in situ` may also continue incorporating this response into population mapping. The first layer of the system judges which population a character belongs to on the basis of life traces; the second layer judges, on the basis of how the character manages traces, what kind of performer, avoider or self-declarer the character is:

`behavioural traces -> population profile -> character performs in relation to the profile -> system reclassifies the mode of performance`

This second-order classification does not need to accurately know whether the character is internally sincere. The system only needs to discover some usable relation between a form of self-presentation and subsequent behaviour, risk or organisational goals. It may distinguish people who repeatedly emphasise minority identity features, people who work hard to seem harmless, people who produce contradictory traces, people who compare interventions with others, or people who participate in collective data action; these are only possible operational categories, not fixed and natural types of performer.

Categories still depend on the institutional site. The same sustained self-declaration may be understood as content preference on a platform, as a loyalty signal in a political institution, and as reputation management or risk avoidance in an employer or insurance system. `population mapping` therefore cannot be understood apart from who is classifying, for what goal, and into what kind of action the classification result enters.

## Classification as a Pass: Infiltrating a Generated Group Landscape

Characters may also intentionally emphasise certain real or disguised features, not in order to maintain that identity permanently, but in the hope of being placed by a certain `algorithm in situ` into a target population and thereby observing what that classification will see and encounter. Classification here becomes a pass, and reverse profiling shifts from a defensive method into an exploratory tool.

What the character infiltrates is not automatically the real life, shared consciousness or inner world of that social group. It is the differentiated landscape that the system generates for objects "classified as this kind of person" according to its own data, goals and institutional permissions: content sequence, recommendation sources, advertisements, narrative frames, prices, opportunities, risk prompts, hidden options and operational friction. This "group landscape" first reveals how the system imagines and treats the group; it does not guarantee that real group members identify with this imagination.

Infiltration may serve investigation, comparison, audit, empathy, political action, commercial exploitation or other purposes not yet determined. A character may also enter several mutually inconsistent landscapes at once: the same set of signals may not produce the same classification across different platforms, employers, public institutions and relational networks.

Infiltration will also enter a loop of self-formation:

`perform target features -> obtain the landscape of the target classification -> be affected by that landscape over time -> cognition, emotion and behaviour change -> originally strategic features partly become real life`

The system may then continue incorporating this strategic behaviour into second-order population mapping. The character cannot easily confirm whether they have truly fooled the system, or whether they have been placed into another layer of landscape shown to "people suspected of manipulating their profile". This does not pre-confirm that the system has the ability to detect intention or deliberately show a false landscape; the uncertainty itself is already enough to affect how characters interpret the material they see.

## Loyalty Display, Identity Declaration and Self-Discipline

One kind of character may repeatedly emphasise several features they really possess and want to be treated as primary classification grounds, as if declaring loyalty to an imagined algorithmic observer. They may not want to deceive the system. On the contrary, they fear that if they do not continuously display these real traits in everyday life, the system will ignore them and place them into another population profile.

This behaviour turns "who I am" into "which pieces of evidence must I keep submitting in order to continue being recognised as the kind of person I want to be". It has two possibilities:

- active declaration can correct misclassification, seek recognition, find a group or refuse to be defined by secret proxy variables;
- continuous proof also compresses complex identity into a small number of signals that machines can easily recognise, so that the character gradually dares to become only the self that is easiest to classify.

This is a form of self-discipline. The character not only manages how others see them now, but also manages records that can be traced back in the future, and pre-emptively deletes expressions, language, relations and bodily responses that may cause misunderstanding. Discipline does not need to be commanded directly by the system every time; the character's imagination of future classification can already complete part of the control for the system.

Self-discipline may also produce a loop that shapes the self in reverse:

`choose real features to emphasise -> receive content and treatment arranged around those features -> understand system response as identity confirmation -> further organise life according to that identity -> originally selective performance becomes new self-understanding`

Therefore, the system may not only misunderstand a character; the character may also gradually change themself in order to avoid being misunderstood, until it becomes difficult to judge which parts were originally real and which parts were formed by the long-term need to prove reality.

## The Institutional Entity of the Eye of Group Expectation

Group expectation in traditional imagination is usually a diffuse eye: a character guesses what family, colleagues, the state, the market or the community wants them to become, but may not be able to point out who is watching at every moment. `algorithms in situ` can transform some expectations into executable institutional processes: certain traces are selected as features, the character is classified, and the classification result then enters recommendations, ranking, prices, permissions, opportunities and friction.

`group expectation -> computable classification -> differentiated treatment -> character self-discipline`

The system therefore does not only "represent" existing expectations. It may also repeatedly reinforce a certain expectation through differentiated treatment: first predicting how a certain kind of person will act, then providing them with a corresponding environment, and finally recording behaviour produced in that environment as evidence that the group was originally like this. Statistical description, institutional norm and self-fulfilling expectation may gradually become entangled in the loop.

But there is no single unified social eye here. Different institutions treat different features as important grounds, and may also make mutually contradictory demands of characters. The same expression can be treated by one platform as preference, by a political institution as loyalty, by an employer as reputation management, and by an intimate other as disguise. Self-discipline is therefore not only obedience to some group. It may be the character's attempt to satisfy multiple incompatible institutional eyes at once.

Even if no concrete person is viewing the character in real time, classification and differentiated treatment give material consequences to imagined observation. Conversely, this does not mean that the algorithm has completely inherited "what society truly wants"; it only materialises the part of expectation that a specific organisation selects and enacts during a specific period.

## Diffusion of Knowledge: Benevolent Popularisation Also Changes the Society the System Observes

A plot can be triggered by a benevolent act of datafication popularisation, investigative disclosure or public education. The explainer makes people aware of how the datafication lifecycle, low-latency feedback and dense social engineering operate. Their original intention may be to increase understanding, protect privacy or build accountability, but they do not anticipate that knowledge itself will change behaviour.

Once people know that the past can be re-associated, present responses will flow back, and recommendations may also carry an influence orientation, they begin to reinterpret everyday interfaces and one another's behaviour. Even if the popularisation is basically accurate, the social response may still include:

- over-attribution of ordinary coincidences and content recommendations;
- chronic anxiety about unconscious responses also being readable;
- continuous self-management to avoid incorrect profiling;
- more mature data literacy, appeal, audit and collective comparison;
- new arguments and new authorities around which risks are real and which are only imagined.

The populariser did not create the original infrastructure, but changed how people face the infrastructure; the system being explained therefore begins to obtain new data produced by a population that already knows it is being observed. Knowledge here is not an explanation outside the system, but becomes a social intervention inside the feedback loop.

A lifecycle diagram, an investigative report or a set of public explanations can also become a cognitive map: it compresses local experiences scattered across different companies, institutions, interfaces and times into a causal diagram that can be discussed together. Its social efficacy does not require it to be a mysterious prophecy, nor does it require complete correctness; once people change attention, investment, avoidance, organisation and appeal on its basis, the text has already entered the loop it describes. It may restore a shared reality, and it may also make an overly tidy model into a new source of fear.

This does not have to be written as "speaking the truth inevitably brings disaster". Panic, literacy, mutual aid, misunderstanding and institutional reform can occur at the same time, and the populariser does not have to become a tragic prophet who should be responsible for all consequences.

## Observed Public Discussion

Individualised intervention means each person sees only their own content sequence, prompts, prices or abnormal friction, making it hard to confirm whether others are experiencing the same kind of system. When individuals share differences, they can reassemble private and fragmented encounters into a public object that can be named, debated and required to explain itself. This comparison is an important reverse action against dense social engineering: it tries to restore a shared reality divided by personalisation.

### Content Circulates, the Recommendation Landscape Remains Private

This candidate preserves an everyday observation that has not yet been empirically verified: people often forward a post, video or news item that might interest a friend, but less often discuss offline what their social media homepages recommend over time, in what order and frequency, and what recommendation patterns they have identified. People do not never show their homepages or joke about "what the algorithm takes me to be", but sharing a single item of content is closer to the everyday default than comparing whole recommendation landscapes.

The exposure levels of the two kinds of sharing are different. Forwarding an object only shows that the character is willing to let the other person see it at that moment; showing a stretch of homepage, recommendation history or long-term pattern may simultaneously expose browsing traces, interests that have not yet been made public, and the system's provisional guesses about them. Onlookers may also mistake "the system thinks this person will be interested" for "this person truly admits they are like this". The recommendation landscape is therefore like a private text co-written by the character and the system, which neither side can fully explain.

This forms a structure that can provisionally be called "private intervention, circulating content". The system's arrangement of information sequence, repetition, timing and salience is mainly borne by individuals, yet individual items of content within it continue to spread through friends, family and group chats. The character thinks they are merely sending something interesting to someone who may like it, but "what first entered their attention, why they thought of this recipient, and when they decided to send it" may already have been shaped by the recommendation landscape; whether the recipient opens and responds will then become new relational and feedback material.

A single forward especially struggles to carry the negative space within the landscape. A character can say what they saw, but it is hard to show which sources have long been absent, which possibilities never entered the menu, and whether a viewpoint seems common only because of repetition and ranking. Content crosses two personalised worlds, yet the two people still do not thereby know the boundaries of each other's worlds.

### "Starting to Compare" Must First Become a Collective Action

Putting phones side by side is not the natural starting point of a plot. A group usually first needs to form a minimal shared language and intellectual preparation: someone proposes that a single anomaly may belong to a comparable pattern; participants are willing to admit that their homepages have privacy; they develop provisional trust about who may look, whether material may be forwarded, and how they will deal with embarrassment after seeing awkward content; they also need to decide whether they are comparing homepage snapshots, search results for the same event, recommendation sequences over a certain period, or long-term experiences each person remembers.

This preparation does not require members to already agree on the same algorithmic theory. Some may understand comparison as investigation, some may only want to check whether a friend is being overly suspicious, some may hope to find the population they have been placed into, and some may worry that the whole action will expose more information. What they first form is not a conclusion, but an etiquette of comparison that allows differences to be temporarily placed together without immediately treating page content as a personality confession.

Therefore, the real plot entry can occur before any screen is shown: who first proposes comparison, who understands it as an intimate request or political mobilisation, who demands that no screenshots be kept, who is willing to act as an intermediary, and how the group obtains a sufficiently safe offline space. Collective communication itself is already changing how they attend to and interpret everyday recommendations; "comparison" is not a suddenly appearing investigative technique, but an action that only becomes possible once group relations reach a certain state.

This entry does not presume a serious, gloomy or investigative atmosphere. Comparison can begin as a dinner-table game, dormitory chat, a joke about guessing "what kind of person the algorithm takes you to be", or a failed experiment repeatedly interrupted by mis-taps, advertisements and absurd recommendations. A character may hurry to declare "this one doesn't represent me", while friends treat their defence as additional profile material for teasing; the originally planned unified comparison procedure may also quickly spin out of control because everyone cannot resist clicking, forwarding and training one another's homepages.

Comedy is not only a buffer before serious discovery. Shared laughter can lower the threshold for displaying private landscapes, allowing the group first to gain an etiquette that does not immediately treat recommendation content as a personality confession; laughter may also cover embarrassment, create new peer pressure, or make someone start cleaning up and performing their homepage after being teased. A comparison can remain light throughout, or it can move from joke to cognitive turning point and then return to intimacy or absurdity. It does not need to automatically slide into dystopian fear.

### Comparison Will Change Participants, and Will Also Change Later Comparable Material

Before comparison, participants are already not unaffected observers: what they forward, whom they forward it to, where they dwell, and how they talk about algorithms have all taken shape in their respective lives and recommendation loops. After they begin preparing for comparison, they may actively search shared topics, revisit old content, reduce certain clicks, clean records, train homepages, or reperform themselves because they expect to show others. What the first side-by-side comparison obtains is not a pure "previous state", but a cross-section jointly left by long-term intervention and newly formed collective consciousness.

After comparison, the group's explanations return to behaviour. They may recognise repeated patterns faster, deliberately exchange content that would not otherwise enter each other's landscapes, build common records, or understand certain recommendations as system responses. Platforms or other relevant institutions may also see traces such as searches, forwarding, dwell time, group formation and public discussion; whether they can identify comparison action from this, whether they adjust systems, and to what extent they adjust remain unknown.

This means that "before-after change" occurs on three levels at once: recommendation material may change, the way characters read material may change, and the people making the comparison may also enter population mapping as a new group. Later differences cannot simply prove that the system is retaliating or covering up, and the previous screenshot cannot be treated as complete and neutral truth; but this contamination does not make comparison meaningless. It precisely shows that when characters try to know the system, the act of knowing itself enters the social site in which the system exists.

But discussions of reverse profiling, privacy and resistance strategies are not necessarily private either. Participants may realise that platforms, institutions or other relevant systems can see what they are comparing, what they suspect, and how they are preparing to change their behaviour. Public discussion then forms a new recursion:

`individuals compare interventions -> form public explanations and strategies -> the system may observe this discussion -> whether and how it responds remains unknown`

One should not pre-confirm that a unified system exists and launches an intelligent counterattack against discussion. Different `algorithms in situ` may reclassify participants, adjust intervention, provide explanations, remain unchanged, or simply lack the ability to convert discussion into coordinated action. The unknown itself can become a pressure on how characters judge risk and build trust.

### Critical Mass Is Not a Fixed Number: From Private Knowing to Coordinable, Survivable Knowledge

Recommendation-landscape comparison can also borrow the threshold problem from game theory and collective action, but "the number of people who know reaches critical mass" cannot be written as an automatically effective percentage. At least three different mechanisms need to be separated:

- **Information survivability**: knowledge, evidence and explanation are distributed across enough carriers, locations or relations that are not easily destroyed simultaneously, so that one act of suppression cannot close the secret again. What matters here is redundancy, distribution, verifiability and succession methods, not only the total number of people who know.
- **Action threshold**: how many people each participant needs to have already joined before they are willing to bear the risks of display, refusal, reporting or organising. Different people have different thresholds, and the same number may produce entirely different results depending on order of joining and participant position.
- **Common knowledge**: it is not only that many people each know something, but that they can see that others also know, and that others can see that each other knows, and on this basis believe that coordinated action is truly possible. Common knowledge can form action, but it does not automatically protect evidence from destruction; highly redundant information may also persist while no one is confident that others will act.

Recommendation-landscape comparison can gradually dramatise these three. At first, each person has only private suspicions they cannot confirm; jokes, tentative questions or one public display let members judge whether others are willing to acknowledge difference; the first person to show a homepage bears the cost of privacy and embarrassment, and also provides comparison etiquette and a shared object for later participants; when participants can mutually see this participation, a set of scattered pages can shift from "many people each discovered an anomaly" to "the group knows that it has discovered an anomaly together". Comedy here can become a low-risk coordination signal, not only an atmospheric buffer for a serious plot.

The original fictional use of this structure in external materials does point toward a local worldline that inherits the technical history of a previous work: if an enemy camp or pseudo-Laplace demon wants a dangerous piece of knowledge to remain unknown to our side, a few people who know may be fragile carriers who can be targeted for assassination and removed along with the evidence. The function of diffusion is not first that "number itself" mysteriously produces safety, but that knowledge gradually becomes impossible to erase from history through a single blow, and that suppression may trigger further disclosure or counteraction. Nuclear deterrence can only provide a limited analogy: the key is similar to whether retaliatory capacity can survive a first strike and remain credible, not that "a small arsenal inevitably invites attack, and a large arsenal necessarily ensures survival".

This strategy still has ethical costs. Spreading a secret to more people may also recruit them, without sufficient consent, as redundant carriers, witnesses and potential targets; when organisers say "we are safe because of numbers", for newcomers this may mean "you are sharing the risk of being removed on behalf of the first people who knew". Therefore, forming common knowledge, preserving knowledge and protecting those who know are three related tasks that cannot substitute for one another.

### Civilisational Continuity and Species Continuity Are Not the Same Threshold

At a harsher civilisational scale, the number of carriers still matters. If one blow pushes population and other environmental parameters beyond a viability boundary, civilisation may be highly likely to lose the capacity to continue itself; "number is not the only condition" must not be miswritten as "number has nothing to do with whether civilisation exists". But this boundary should also not be compressed into a fixed population number. Spatial distribution of population, age and skill structure, food, energy and ecological conditions, communication and maintenance capacity, knowledge transmission, institutional coordination and social reproduction may compensate for one another or fail together.

Therefore, whether civilisation continues is closer to a multi-parameter viability domain. Even if human individuals still survive and continue reproducing after the blow, they may already have lost the institutions, practices, relations and self-understanding of the original civilisation; the society that forms later may be the continuation of the species or a successor civilisation, but it does not automatically constitute the continuous existence of the original civilisation. Even if later references cite historical cases in which human population severely contracted while the species still survived, this can only show that biological survival can occur, and cannot by itself prove that the same civilisation can maintain continuity on a similar scale.

The relevant judgement can be divided into at least five layers:

- **Species survival**: there are still human groups sufficient for continued reproduction;
- **Population survival**: certain concrete communities and their relations remain;
- **Civilisational continuity**: institutions, skills, material bases and life practices can still reproduce together;
- **Knowledge survivability**: a piece of knowledge is still preserved, verified, interpreted and transmitted;
- **Political action capacity**: survivors can still find one another and turn knowledge into coordinated action.

These layers support one another, but cannot replace one another. A large number of isolated people who know may preserve some memories, but be unable to restore verification, maintenance and collaboration networks; a smaller group with reasonable distribution, complementary skills and succession mechanisms may instead keep some knowledge and practices operating. "Critical mass" in the pseudo-Laplace demon situation can therefore be expanded into the question of whether the carrier network can reproduce itself after a blow, not only how many copies of the secret exist.

This analysis deliberately adopts a cold civilisational-scale view, and does not reduce the ethical value of individuals to carriers of civilisation. If characters or institutions calculate population, skills and relations as environmental parameters for the sake of civilisational continuity, the calculation itself should also become a political and moral action that can be questioned.

## Life Meaning, Data Shadows and Expressive Misalignment

The same action can exist in two stories at once. For a character, it may be care, probing, protection, loyalty or refusal; for the system, it may be only event type, timestamp, relational edge, anomaly pattern and next-round input. The two meanings are not one true and one false, but they change one another in system intervention and character interpretation.

This means communicative misunderstanding does not only occur when characters use words such as "trust". A character produces contradictory traces in order to protect another person, and the system may interpret this as risk; a character repeatedly approaches someone in order to enter a target group, and the other person may interpret this strategy as real closeness; the system treats a silence as low interest, while another person in the relation treats it as restraint or betrayal. Life meaning cannot guarantee that a corresponding data shadow is left, and a data shadow cannot automatically explain why an action occurred.

## Trust, Mutual Hiding and the Altered Self

This candidate does not define "trust" in advance as a single thing. Expression and communication are already prone to misunderstanding; even when two characters use the same word, they may trust different objects, require different proofs and fear different forms of betrayal.

"Trust" in concrete situations may refer to believing that a statement is true, believing that the other person will not harm oneself, believing that the other person will keep a secret, believing that the other person remains continuous with the past, believing that the already changed other will still undertake relational commitments, or believing that the other person will respect one's right not to be fully understood. These are only possible objects to distinguish while writing, and do not form a taxonomy of trust that must be adopted.

Semantic misalignment can become the dramatic structure of dialogue itself. One side says "you should trust me", meaning "do not ask about the content I must hide"; the other side hears "if you are worthy of trust, you should tell me everything". Neither side needs to give an incorrect definition of trust. Conflict still arises because they use the same word to demand opposite actions from one another.

Trust here is not threatened only by "whether the other person is lying". A character may also fear that the other person, in order to manage visible records over the long term, has continually self-disciplined and has already changed their understanding of themself; even if they answer sincerely at this moment, they may not know how much of their desire comes from their original life and how much from continuous performance and system feedback.

This means intimate relations face at least three different uncertainties:

- what the other person is hiding;
- whether the other person is intentionally presenting another appearance in order to protect the relation or themself;
- whether the other person has already become, through long-term discipline, the person they originally only intended to perform.

Relations therefore cannot be repaired only through complete transparency. Demanding that the other person prove everything may precisely copy the system's logic of equating more visibility with more truth. Conversely, trust can also appear as intentionally not asking, recognising the other's right to keep secrets, or even helping the other person hide certain records.

The author uses the restraint when Wallfacers meet in *The Three-Body Problem: The Dark Forest* as an analogy: even if they respect one another, they do not have to try to obtain the content of the other's plans. What is preserved here is a relational structure, not a transfer of that work's plot: respect and closeness can be expressed through not obtaining knowledge.

"Helping the other person hide" is also ambiguous. It can be care and loyalty in jointly resisting improper observation, and it may also make it impossible for both sides to judge whether the other is evading responsibility in the name of protection. A person's willingness to maintain opacity for another does not mean they are not afraid that irreversible self-change is taking place inside that opacity.

Therefore, trust may shift from "I am certain you have no secrets" to a more difficult form:

> I know you must hide, and I know long-term hiding may change you; I still do not treat possession of all your information as a condition of love or understanding.

Emotional connection will be eroded by suspicion, self-surveillance and continuous performance, but it does not therefore have to break completely. Characters may also establish new privacy etiquette, agreements not to ask, mutual cover or joint review mechanisms, so that relations can continue on the premise of acknowledged unknowability.

The concrete meaning of trust is left until the character, relation and scene have formed; it is not abstractly closed in this candidate. When arranging related dialogue, the following can be checked separately:

1. What the speaker's so-called "trust" points toward;
2. what action the speaker is asking the other person to take or stop taking through this word;
3. how the other person understands the same word;
4. what action or relational consequence this misalignment finally causes.

## The Recursive Predicament of Resistance

- The more continuously and clearly sincerity is performed, the more likely the system is to absorb this sincerity as a new stable signal.
- The more thorough the disguise, the harder it is for characters to judge what others are also disguising; individual adaptation carried out to protect privacy may collectively erode trust and emotional connection.
- The system can transform "attempting to change one's profile" itself into a risk, intention or population label.
- The self performed over the long term in resistance to operational identity may in turn change the habits, relations and values the character actually forms.
- Collective coordinated performance may disrupt the mapping between individual traces and private inclinations, and may also immediately generate a new governable population.
- Resistance therefore may not be able to exit the feedback loop; it is more likely to temporarily change what the loop can be certain of, at what cost, and who has the ability to interpret its results.

Resistance also does not have to appear only as noise-making or disguise. Pausing scoring, freezing model updates, delaying automatic handling, preserving old schemas, allowing different characters to compare treatment, restoring human appeals or maintaining non-system alternatives may all temporarily slow the closure of the loop. This kind of emergency brake is not a return to a non-technological past, but buys time for reinterpreting goals, forming common knowledge and building other capacities; it may still cause service delays, resource interruption and new distribution conflicts.

## Not Presuming a Dystopian Conclusion

Dense social engineering, performative sincerity and reverse profiling all have oppressive possibilities, but this candidate does not require a concrete plot to adopt a continuously dark dystopian or mildly dystopian atmosphere.

- Personalised systems may provide genuinely useful information, accessibility support or group discovery, and may also mix useful services with influence orientation in the same infrastructure.
- Actively emphasising identity features may be self-compression, and may also be agentive action to obtain recognition and correct mistaken classification.
- Public comparison may trigger panic, and may also form data literacy, collective accountability and shared reality.
- Comparison of recommendation landscapes can also be comedy, social game or intimate joke; shared laughter may build trust that allows display, and may also conceal shame and shape the next round of self-presentation.
- Mutual hiding may erode trust, and may also become a way of respecting, being intimate with and protecting another person's opacity.
- The system may learn and absorb resistance, and may also be limited by organisational fragmentation, boundaries of authority, incorrect models, public pressure and creative action by characters.

The work's tension can come from goodwill, fear, convenience, interest, protection and misunderstanding coexisting, without requiring an omnipotent malicious subject to continuously create oppression.

## Developable Narrative Axes

- How a popularisation effort intended to protect the public unexpectedly changes social emotions and data behaviour;
- how characters repeat real features to seek a classification, and are gradually shaped by this self-declaration;
- how different institutions conduct mutually inconsistent second-order population mapping of performers;
- how characters treat classification as a pass, infiltrate the landscape generated by the system for a target population, and cannot confirm whether what they see is the target landscape or the landscape of a "suspected infiltrator";
- how a group first forms the shared language, comparison etiquette and provisional trust sufficient to display private recommendation landscapes, and then forms a public object under conditions in which comparison changes participants and subsequent material;
- how private suspicion forms common knowledge through low-risk probing, first displayers and visible mutual participation, and at which step information survivability, action threshold and common knowledge become misaligned;
- after one blow, at which layer species, population, civilisation, knowledge and political action each continue to survive, and how those who calculate individuals as civilisational environmental parameters bear the ethical consequences of choosing that scale;
- how intimate characters redefine trust among suspicion, mutual cover and not asking;
- how two sides in a dialogue use the same "trust" to refer to different objects, and demand opposite actions from one another even when there is no simple person who misunderstands;
- how characters face the harder-to-verify relational fact that "the other person did not deceive me, yet has already changed because of self-discipline".

## Questions Not Yet Activated

- Does the influence orientation come from clear political or commercial intention, or emerge from platform goals and local optimisation?
- Does the system mainly act on individuals, relational networks, populations, or several scales at once?
- Is performative sincerity an individual habit, a subcultural etiquette, a public political strategy, or a collective data action?
- Which real features will characters repeatedly declare, who decides that these features are sufficient to represent their identity, and will silence be experienced as identity disappearance?
- Characters enter a system-generated group landscape through classification; to what extent this landscape corresponds to, diverges from, or in turn shapes real group experience remains for concrete institutions to decide.
- Whether the system will identify infiltrators as a new performer type, and how characters distinguish exposure, misclassification and their own suspiciousness, is not currently confirmed.
- Whether and how the system responds to public discussion of reverse profiling should remain undecided until concrete institutions and plots form.
- Do characters regain interpretive authority through reverse profiling, or do they gradually treat system responses as a mirror for knowing themselves?
- When self-discipline has already changed a character's self-understanding, should an intimate other trust their current expression, ask about the process of change, or respect an internal history that cannot be restored?
- "Trust" is not fixed at the setting level as a single object; its direction, demand and misalignment in concrete dialogue are decided by the corresponding relation and scene.
- If this enters the current story, will the threat of the old pseudo-Laplace demon remain only as historical inspiration, or will there be another concrete institutional and technical carrier?

## Use and Evidence Boundaries

This candidate preserves creative concepts such as dense regression/response modelling, content-recommendation intervention, micromanagement-style social engineering, "anticipatory knowledge", performative sincerity, reverse profiling, second-order population mapping, group landscape, mutual hiding and trust misalignment. The quoted passage preserves its project-internal wording and intentional ambiguity. `performative sincerity`, `anticipatory knowledge`, "group landscape" and "private intervention, circulating content" are working formulations, and do not claim to already correspond to verified existing academic terms; the Wallfacers in *The Three-Body Problem: The Dark Forest* are used only as a relational analogy and do not enter the current story facts.

Choice topology, actual freedom and fallback capacity, cognitive map, life meaning/data shadow misalignment and emergency brake are creative translations of theoretical mechanisms. They do not confirm that a concrete story institution already exists, and should not be miswritten as the original meaning of any scholar.

Threshold distributions and action order refer to Mark Granovetter's ["Threshold Models of Collective Behavior"](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/226707), the critical-mass mechanism in which a small number of early participants bear start-up costs refers to Pamela Oliver, Gerald Marwell and Ruy Teixeira's ["A Theory of the Critical Mass I"](https://www.ssc.wisc.edu/~oliver/PROTESTS/ArticleCopies/OliverMarwellCritMassI.pdf), and how public rituals form the common knowledge needed for coordination refers to Michael Suk-Young Chwe's [*Rational Ritual*](https://academic.oup.com/princeton-scholarship-online/book/13454). The nuclear analogy only preserves the structure of "survivability after a first strike and credible response", following SIPRI's discussion of [minimal nuclear deterrence](https://www.sipri.org/sites/default/files/2022-06/sipriinsight2206_minimal_nuclear_deterrence_0.pdf); it does not adopt unverified assertions such as fixed numbers, fixed dissemination rates, or "a limited arsenal inevitably invites pre-emption, while a sufficient number guarantees survival".

"Civilisational viability domain" and the layering of species/civilisational continuity are currently preserved only as concept and narrative scale. This text does not give a calculable critical population, parameter weights or extinction probability for any real civilisation; nor is it equivalent to contemporary empirical verification of political communication, recommendation systems, online experiments, behavioural manipulation or the effects of algorithmic resistance. If these technical forms, effect strengths or real-world comparisons enter the dissertation, public explanation or precise technical description, they still require separate research.
