## Ten-Hour Conversation Basis Already Adopted in Line A

- The protagonist has already been dead for a long time before the spacecraft enters the causal singularity.

- "Ten hours" refers to conversation time.
- The ten hours come from the capsule passing through a finite singularity region along its original trajectory; the exit requires a state that can continue propagating along Line A to be causally equivalent to the unintervened state.
- The restored medical, consciousness, and individual-continuity mechanisms remain undetermined.

- After the protagonist's death, Interface may remain together with the body inside the spacecraft as it continues travelling.
- Interface may not immediately perform emotional rollback, but instead sit still or stare blankly beside the protagonist for several years.
- This scene can occur in a version where the conversation during life was not completed, or after the ten-hour conversation.
- "Crash" is currently only a literary description, not a confirmed technical fault mechanism.

## Peripheral Framing for Two Kinds of Sitting-Still Backstory

The following interpretations preserve why the two scene variants are not merely repetitions of the same image; they do not prescribe the emotional interpretation the final text must adopt:

- If Interface only enters when the protagonist dies, and the conversation during life has not been completed before this, sitting still can unfold around lateness, missing the chance, and the impossibility of meeting.
- If the sitting still happens after the ten-hour conversation and the second death, the same silence can respond to the shared experience just formed and the unfinished relationship.

The two variants can later reuse similar compositions, but visual reuse does not mean they have the same relationship history, nor does it automatically mean that the current Interface has already loaded the concrete memory of another worldline. Cross-line memory capacity and what a specific instance actually carries still need to be separated.
