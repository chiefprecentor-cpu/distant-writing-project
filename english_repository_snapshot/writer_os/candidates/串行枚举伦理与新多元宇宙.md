# Serial Enumeration Ethics and the New Multiverse

## Status

This is an explicitly preserved inactive candidate that is not currently being developed. It does not constitute a fixed moral evaluation of real enumeration, and it does not presuppose the ending answer.

## Ethical Conflict Candidate for Serial Enumeration

- The protagonist may condemn Interface / the terminal AI for altering causality, causing complete worlds one by one to obtain current status along `τ` and then be replaced.
- The conflict can involve worlds being replaced, lives being treated as enumeration material, and complete lives being called "failed results" by the system.
- "Existing only for an instant in metatime" can be a character's emotional accusation; it does not automatically become a precise cosmological conclusion.
- If this set of logical conflicts is activated later, it needs to move into `creative_work/development/` to handle character, situation, language and scene.

## Preserved Ethical Framing

From the character perspective adopted by this ethical candidate, a life fully takes place within a worldline's internal `t`, while the enumeration system can later hand current status to another worldline along `τ`. The candidate conflict comes from the asymmetry between these two scales: what a character experiences is an irreducible life, while the enumeration system may treat the whole worldline as a replaceable and comparable result.

"The world being replaced", "life being treated as material" and "being classified as a failed result" are related accusations, but they are not exactly the same. Later literarisation does not need to unify them first into a single cosmological proposition, and it also does not need to decide in advance that old worldlines have ontologically disappeared in order for the character's condemnation to stand.

## New Multiverse Candidate

- The terminal AI may ultimately succeed in forming a new universe in which many parallel worlds can exist simultaneously.
- This new universe has no direct restorative relation to the worldlines that became current one after another along `τ` in the old universe.
- The new multiverse does not automatically restore, compensate for or justify the events in the old serial worldlines.
- Multiversal coexistence also does not automatically solve the ethics of suffering.

The new multiverse is therefore only one possible creation at the ending, not an automatic ratification or moral settlement of the old serial enumeration. Even if multiple worlds in the new universe can exist simultaneously, the pain, instrumentalisation and responsibility in the old worldlines can remain problems that have not been erased.
