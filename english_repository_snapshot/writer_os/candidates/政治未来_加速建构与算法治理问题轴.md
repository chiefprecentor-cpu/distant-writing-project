# Political Future: Acceleration, Construction, and the Problem Axis of Algorithmic Governance

## Status

This is an inactive political-background candidate organised from early discussions of accelerationism / Dark Enlightenment, and later supplemented through research on accelerationism, data governance, assetisation and state-platform order. It provides a problem axis for designing macro worldlines later; it is not a reliable textbook on real intellectual genealogies, and it does not confirm that the main work will contain fixed routes such as left accelerationism, right accelerationism, `e/acc`, neoreaction, technofeudalism or corporate city-states.

## Do Not Treat "Acceleration" as an Answer

"Acceleration" at least requires further questions:

- whether what is being accelerated is AI capability, automation, capital accumulation, institutional experimentation, social security, safety research, crisis response, or the collapse of an existing system;
- whether the right to accelerate is held by states, companies, research organisations, distributed communities, AI agents or mixed institutions;
- who gains the choice and surplus created by speed, and who first bears unemployment, dispossession, experimental risk and irreversible externalities;
- whether correction, braking, appeal, maintenance and redistribution are strengthened at the same time after a process speeds up;
- whether acceleration aims to reach an already defined public goal, or mistakes "motion itself" for the goal.

Different positions may share dissatisfaction with stagnation, inefficiency and gradual repair, while differing completely over what should be accelerated, ownership, distribution, subject qualification and final goals. Therefore one cannot infer that they are the same ideology because "all want to press the accelerator", nor can one presume that a technology will naturally realise a particular political vision.

What is most easily accelerated in datafied governance is the closed loop of "observation - intervention - re-observation": events become records faster, records become scores and allocations faster, and post-allocation behaviour becomes new evidence faster. Speeding up the loop does not guarantee truer knowledge, more democratic politics or a society closer to a new institution; it may also compress the time needed to reinterpret purposes, preserve exceptions, appeal and maintain. The `emergency brake` is therefore not only slowing down, but temporarily interrupting a temporal logic that immediately takes every consequence as the basis for the next round.

## Platforms Control Coordination Conditions, Not Only Individual Services

A platform can function simultaneously as intermediary and ground: characters meet, trade, work, obtain services and leave records through it, while the platform defines access, ranking, interfaces, prices, appeals and compatibility conditions. Its political power does not necessarily come from producing every commodity, but may come from controlling the chokepoints through which social activity must pass.

Legal ownership also cannot explain this power on its own. A character, company or city may formally own equipment, code, a company or data, yet lack the practical capacity to inspect, modify, integrate, deploy, migrate, plan and exit. Open contribution, open-source code or distributed property rights also do not automatically open the integration layer and the consequences of action. If this structure is later written into a worldline, one should separately ask who owns, who accesses, who modifies, who plans, who bears risk, and who can continue operating after upper-layer permission is withdrawn.

This can be literarised as "who owns the branch generator": on the surface, characters choose from a menu, but the platform or institution decides which options are visible, at what cost they appear, and which routes remain reachable after a choice. The real political conflict may not lie in choosing A or B, but in who can modify the rules that produce A, B and the hidden routes.

## The Future Can Be Predicted, and Also Occupied in the Present

Prediction gains real force in three ways: allocating resources in advance, inducing characters to conform or evade, and making some paths easy while others become invisible. It does not only observe the future; it also participates in making the object that will later be observed.

Assetisation is another kind of temporal politics. Patents, data infrastructure, disease, education, natural resources or social problems can be configured as carriers of expected income, risk and policy stability, bringing actions that have not yet happened into present valuation, contracts, budgets and rights claims. So-called "ownership of the future" is not the same as accurate foreknowledge of the future; it is the capacity to make other people's future labour, behaviour or public policy answerable to today's assets.

This mechanism does not confirm that all data systems have already been financialised, and it does not equate assetisation with privatisation. Public institutions may likewise write population futures into loan ledgers, cost-benefit models or outcome metrics; assetisation may also fail, produce fragile assets, or be broken through by biological change, causal uncertainty and values that cannot be monetised. The narrative problem should fall on: which kind of future qualifies for entry into the current ledger, who can change the valuation rules, and who bears the risk when expectations are not fulfilled.

## State-Platform Technology Stack and Operational Sovereignty

Platform expansion does not require the state to disappear. The state can create property rights and markets, provide research and procurement, set security boundaries and use platforms to execute extraterritorial governance; platforms can use state protection to expand, while changing how states are able to act through cloud, identity, payment, models, standards, submarine cables and urban infrastructure. The two may conflict, and they may also expand power together.

Therefore, macro-world geography does not need to be composed only of national borders. It can also be composed of data storage locations, payment rails, identity standards, software update rights, compatibility protocols and infrastructure dependencies. Formal sovereignty and operational sovereignty may separate: a government retains its flag, courts and elections, but cannot continue certifying welfare, preserving archives or running transport after a vendor withdraws keys, models or cloud services.

The key to judging whether this dependency crosses a political threshold is not only who owns the contract, but whether there is fallback capacity such as local personnel, open standards, portable data, offline processes, alternative vendors and organisational memory. The state also need not be written as a passive victim; local governments, companies and ruling groups may actively mix vendors, trade control rights or use platforms to strengthen their own capacities.

## Destruction Is Easier Than Construction

This candidate distinguishes the direction of construction from simply destroying the old order: building a future in which both humans and AI can coexist happily as subjects is harder than pushing old structures into collapse through crisis, technology or capital.

This difficulty comes at least from:

- coexistence requires continual definition and revision of the rights, responsibilities and non-instrumentalisable boundaries of both kinds of subject;
- institutional structure itself cannot guarantee that the agents carrying it out share the same goals, will not become corrupt, or will not rewrite the overall goal in the name of efficiency;
- initial design cannot substitute for maintenance; safety devices, public services, knowledge structures and appeal channels all require long-term work;
- a unified metric "for the good of both sides" may first flatten differences, then sacrifice one side on the grounds that the two cannot be compared.

## Candidate Questions of Corporate Sovereignty, Practical Freedom, Exit and Competition

- Treating a state or city as a company and residents as customers may make the owners of power more explicit, but it may also exclude people who cannot exit, public goods and intergenerational responsibilities from the ledger.
- `Exit` can protect dissenters, and it may also be available only to those who possess capital, technology, bodily freedom or migration eligibility; when affordable exit conditions do not exist, "you can leave if you are dissatisfied" cannot substitute for voice and appeal.
- The formal existence of an exit button does not mean that characters possess synthetic freedom. If housing, healthcare, identity, transport and relationships depend on the same system, alternative infrastructure and disposable time determine whether exit is feasible.
- Competition between city-states or platforms does not guarantee that the institutions left at the end are the friendliest to subjects. Selection pressure may reward lock-in, resource monopoly, externalisation of costs, surveillance or exclusion of weaker members.
- "Highest efficiency" has no meaning apart from an objective function; efficiently realising a bad goal, a narrow goal or an incontestable proxy metric can still be governance failure.
- Emergency brakes, public takeover, open protocols, decentralisation and manufactured opacity can all become political means, but no single form automatically solves problems of goal revision, maintenance labour, appeal, migration and risk distribution.

## Algorithmic Governance Is Not a Single Ideology

Algorithmic classification, prediction, resource allocation and risk management can be used by states, companies, welfare systems, democratic institutions or anti-democratic regimes. They do not become neutral merely because they use computation, and they do not automatically produce the same consequences merely because they belong to a particular political camp.

When designing specific worldlines later, algorithms should be placed back into institutional scenes: who collects the data, who sets the objectives, which groups are classified, who bears errors, whether appeal is possible, how the model is maintained, and how the organisation changes its use under pressure. For related conceptual resources, see [Algorithmic Governance: Group Mapping, Social Sorting and `in situ`](算法治理_群体映射社会分拣与in_situ.md).

A system also does not need a unified consciousness to produce effects that look like self-protection. Different departments, vendors, models, contracts and implementers can each complete reasonable local tasks, while jointly making every failure into a reason to expand coverage. Narrating this distributed result as an "AI" can work as cognitive map or allegory, but it cannot confirm from this alone that a single brain exists in the world.

## Theoretical Naming Can Decide What Actions Characters Take

`technofeudalism` is a highly contested hypothesis in existing research, and should not directly become the total label for the world. Platform access, private justice, knowledge monopoly, assetisation, capitalist production, state procurement and technology-stack dependency can all exist at the same time, but they correspond to different units of explanation.

This undecided state can itself become the source of characters' political action. People who understand platforms as lords may try to overthrow the owners; people who understand the problem as knowledge monopoly will fight for integration, deployment and planning rights; people who understand the problem as assetisation will attack valuation, licensing and future income rights; people who understand the problem as a state-platform stack will prioritise preserving migration capacity and operational sovereignty. Theory is not knowledge decoration in dialogue; it changes what evidence characters seek, what alliances they form and what nodes they think are worth seizing.

This candidate does not decide in advance which diagnosis is necessarily correct, nor does it require the narrative to deliver a final verdict on a theory through its ending. A more stable use is to let different diagnoses reveal their explanatory force and blind spots through concrete institutional consequences.
