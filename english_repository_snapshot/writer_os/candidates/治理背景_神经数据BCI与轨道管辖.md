## Do Not Treat "Brain-Computer Interface" as One Single Object

Future governance needs at least to distinguish:

- invasive, minimally invasive, non-invasive and ordinary wearable devices;
- medical repair, assisted communication, consumer entertainment, workplace management, military use and human enhancement;
- reading raw neural signals, inferring mental states from signals, feeding information back to the user, and directly stimulating or changing neural activity;
- the device itself, decoding models, cloud services, data storage, remote updates and third-party components;
- currently observable signals, long-term profiles, inferred sensitive features and probabilistic judgments about future behaviour.

Different combinations bring different bodily risks, privacy risks, agency risks and chains of responsibility. The work should not summarise all technologies only as "implanted chips", nor jump directly from "able to read some signals" to "able to read complete thoughts".

## Axes of Rights and Institutional Problems

- **Mental privacy**: what signals, inferences or model states may be collected, stored and reused; whether derived profiles remain after raw data is deleted.
- **Mental and bodily integrity**: whether the device can only observe, or can stimulate, suppress, alter attention or change the capacity to act; who can modify its functions.
- **Cognitive liberty**: whether people can refuse implantation, exit a service, use it offline, choose different algorithms, or continue living without enhancement.
- **Consent in context**: "voluntariness" in employment, education, medicine, the military, imprisonment or service-dependent relations may be constrained by power and by available alternatives.
- **Continuous control and maintenance**: who is responsible after implantation for security updates, service shutdown, bankruptcy, key loss, cloud migration, vulnerability repair and device removal.
- **Inference and differential treatment**: even without storing readable "thought-text", institutions may use neural signals to place people into risk or capability categories.
- **Responsibility for hybrid action**: when human intention, model suggestions, automatic execution and neural stimulation jointly cause consequences, responsibility cannot simply be assigned entirely to the wearer or to the developer.

These questions can place pressure on the binary-subjectivity vision in [Protagonist: Egalitarianism, Responsibility Ethics and Self-Risk](../creative_work/story_state/主人公_平等主义责任伦理与自我风险.md), but they do not pre-decide what kind of enhancement the protagonist supports or opposes.

## Exceptions and "Back Doors" Should Be Written as Institutional Evolution, Not Conspiracy Buttons

National security, defence, scientific research, medical emergencies, major public interest, trade secrets and regulatory sandboxes may all become reasons for allowing different data uses or lowering some procedural requirements. They are not inherently malicious back doors, and the existence of provisions does not mean future misuse is certain.

If governance slippage is written, it needs to show the whole chain: what problem the exception initially solves, who interprets it, how supervision weakens, how uses expand, how data and infrastructure are recombined, which agents benefit or evade responsibility, and why affected people find it hard to exit or appeal.

## Candidate Conflict: Orbital Deployment and Concurrent / Overlapping Jurisdiction

This file preserves several research leads worth verifying later: state responsibility and continuing supervision in outer-space activities, the registry state's jurisdiction and control over space objects, multiple jurisdictions asserting authority at the same time, and the gap between obligations on paper and physical enforcement capacity.

This can form candidate scenarios:

- users, operators, registered entities, cloud facilities and physical hardware of BCI, AI or data services are distributed across different jurisdictions;
- orbital facilities may be affected at the same time by laws concerning registration, nationality, market access, the data subject's location and ground control stations;
- the extraterritorial effect of Earth law and registered jurisdiction over space objects may overlap, rather than simply failing at the edge of the atmosphere;
- the absence of normalised orbital police or convenient seizure capacity can make enforcement slower, more expensive or dependent on state cooperation, but does not automatically turn space into a lawless safe harbour;
- private conduct may raise questions of state authorisation, supervision or international responsibility, while responsibility, jurisdiction, compensation and actual enforcement still need to be distinguished.

The official text of Articles VI / VIII of the Outer Space Treaty can serve as a minimum legal skeleton. Specific application, other domestic / regional law and the precise meaning of "concurrent and overlapping jurisdiction" still need to be researched from primary materials at the time of formal use. This section preserves only the problem structure. The mission in the next section remains an inactive candidate, not worldline fact.

### Articles VI / VIII and Concurrent Jurisdiction

- Article VI provides that States Parties bear international responsibility for their national activities in outer space, including activities by non-governmental entities; outer-space activities by non-governmental entities require authorisation and continuing supervision by the appropriate State Party.
- Article VIII provides that the State Party on whose registry a space object is carried retains jurisdiction and control over that object and over any personnel thereof.
- `legally concurrent jurisdiction` is an interpretive frame formed by overlapping legal connecting points, not original wording from Article VI or Article VIII. Registry states, states connected to operators or personnel, launch and ground-control locations, affected people and service markets may each generate claims of jurisdiction, responsibility or regulation of different kinds.
- The official treaty text is available from the [United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs](https://www.unoosa.org/oosa/en/ourwork/spacelaw/treaties/outerspacetreaty.html?stream=science). The points above confirm only the skeleton of the provisions and do not pre-decide specific application outcomes in future worldlines.

### Orbital Data Centre and Dozen-Plus-Person Temporary Mission Candidate

An orbital data centre can endanger human interests through processing, inference, deployment or continuous operation, and be found by one or more jurisdictions to violate relevant obligations. It does not have to process BCI or neural data. Orbit is not a legal vacuum: the conflict comes from overlapping jurisdiction and responsibility, the failure of state cooperation, and Earth's lack of a standing enforcement system able to arrive, investigate, seize or stop operations in time.

A temporary team of a dozen-plus people can use commercial or other existing spaceflight capacity to reach the facility. "Unofficial" only means that it is not part of a standing public enforcement body; it does not automatically mean completely unauthorised. The team may separately obtain limited authorisations from some courts, regulators, facility rights-holders or affected parties, while the registry state or other jurisdictions do not recognise those authorisations. The target facility's unlawfulness also does not automatically make all methods used by the team lawful.

The currently adopted direction divides the mission relation into an evidence-integration core, a temporary action group holding some formal authority, and a non-obstruction contact circle formed by officials from multiple countries' regulatory, prosecutorial, police / intelligence, aerospace and communications authorities. The registry state provides narrow technical-assistance / safety-inspection documents. Other subjects separately share information and move the action obstacles they can control, but they do not form a joint command, complete authorisation or post-facto immunity.

Only after the craft has passed the final abort / interception window, and after Earth authorities no longer possess any craft, on-site personnel or effective remote control capable of actually stopping the team before the action is completed, does the action group carry out forced entry and evidence acquisition that uncontroversially exceed the registry state's documents. The ground can still issue orders, state objections and pursue responsibility afterwards. The disappearance of physical enforcement capacity is not the disappearance of law and jurisdiction.

Xuyuan does not serve as an expert in flight, engineering, combat or space law. Lines A and B have already confirmed that he was previously in a key position in the evidence chain through the continuous integration of cross-vendor materials, and that he is the best on-site integrator available among the existing personnel and within the time window. This position comes from the source relationships he maintains, negative hypotheses, action thresholds and capacity for subsequent organisation. It does not come from chosen-one talent, and it does not cancel his right to refuse. Yuankai has already participated in the team's evidence sorting and action argument, and is responsible for re-establishing contact, passing on the request and proving that it is worth hearing out. But he is not the mission commander and cannot choose Xuyuan on the team's behalf or accept on Xuyuan's behalf.

The mission succeeds in handling the facility and transmitting the minimum evidence, but fails to recover the complete originals and evacuate Xuyuan. His sealed capsule is the only crewed unit that is not evacuated; the other on-site members complete evacuation. After Xuyuan's capsule is ejected, he completes evidence integration and sends the minimally sufficient materials to the ground, but does not receive final confirmation before losing consciousness. The capsule then enters a heliocentric escape orbit through continuous propulsion and Jupiter gravity assist. Xuyuan dies from long-term resource exhaustion, and many years later the capsule, carrying his body and the most complete original materials, drifts into the [causal singularity](../creative_work/story_state/局部设定_因果奇异点空间.md). The team's internal actions, communication implementation, long-term preservation engineering and the mechanism of entering the singularity remain undecided.

## Do Not Automatically Adopt

- Do not adopt unverified EU / US trends in BCI deregulation, green channels, bill names, policy dates or specific article-based inferences.
- Do not assume that cloud updates naturally allow governments to remotely change people's emotions, or that consumer devices are already able to form real-time thought streams.
- Do not confirm that human-machine hybrid subjects, orbital servers, space-enterprise city-states or cross-jurisdictional neural-data wars already exist in any worldline.
- If legal questions enter a specific plot, they need to be modelled again within the period and institutional environment of that worldline. Real law cannot simply be frozen unchanged into the distant future.
- Articles VI / VIII do not directly authorise a dozen-plus-person team to board a facility and conduct coercive action. "The target is unlawful" and "the temporary team's action is lawful" must be established separately.
