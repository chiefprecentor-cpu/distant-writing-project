# Protagonist Name: English Rendering and Japanese Ateji Candidates

## Status

The protagonist's Chinese real name, `何须怨`, has already been confirmed by the author; see [Protagonist Name: He Xuyuan](../creative_work/story_state/主人公姓名_何须怨.md). This file preserves inactive literary explanations, full English renderings, and Japanese ateji candidates. These unresolved renderings should not override the already confirmed Chinese real name. The author has explicitly stated that the Japanese ateji discussed here are pseudonyms used by the protagonist, and do not need to become Japanese names that already exist in reality or can be registered. None of the retained Japanese written forms has yet been assigned to a specific worldline.

This file keeps using "宛字" as the Chinese working label; modern Japanese usually writes this as "当て字".

## Confirmed Core of the Chinese Real Name and Interpretive Directions

- The Chinese real name is confirmed as `何须怨`.
- `何` is both the surname and, within the full name, part of the question structure `何须怨`, making the name feel both like a personal name and like an unanswered sentence.
- This naming logic can be compared to names such as Huo Qubing, which negate negative intentions such as illness or resentment through the name. This is only a creative analogy, not a factual judgement about the historical origin of the person's name.
- The surname `何` can evoke the protagonist's self-caution toward He Jin, but it is not necessary to preserve as factual backstory that the protagonist is He Jin's descendant.

## English Rendering Direction

The English text does not translate the literal meaning of `何须怨` into an English name. `He Xuyuan` has already appeared in two local dialogues: the current near-final opening, where Xuyuan introduces himself to Lulu, and the B-line Interface doorway usage `He Xuyuan?`, which the author has explicitly confirmed. These uses preserve the Chinese family-name-first order and also keep the arrangement of `何-须-怨` and the three pinyin components.

The confirmed local dialogues do not mean that `He Xuyuan` has already become the universal full rendering for all English identity documents, work signatures, narration, or worldlines. `Xuyuan He` remains a viable candidate for contexts adapting to English given-name-first convention, but it would interrupt the visual order of the name as a complete Chinese phrase. The general English full-name rule remains unconfirmed.

One as-yet unselected presentation candidate is this: if future name boxes use `He Xuyuan`, the first name exchange can explain that `He` is the surname, or it can leave this unexplained for the moment, allowing other characters and players to potentially divide the family name and given name according to English naming habits. The repeated sentence-initial pronoun `He` in the opening would give this misreading a homographic relation that can be noticed on rereading. Whether to use this point, how long the misreading lasts, and whether clarification occurs only when the situation naturally requires it must still be decided together with the parallel plot.

## Source Check for `なんぞもちいん`

Japanese kambun-reading materials do indeed read "何須" in "Liangzhou Ci" as "何ぞ須（もち）いん". Therefore, `なんぞもちいん` is not an arbitrary sound assembled from Mandarin pronunciation, but a literary sound that can be extracted from the Japanese reading of the whole line.

However, "何ぞ須いん" originally depends on the following "楊柳を怨むを". Once taken out on its own, it is an intentional omission and renaming, not a naturally formed modern Japanese name.

## Investigation Conclusion: Can This Be Used as Ateji?

The conclusion has two layers:

1. **As a pen name, screen name, chuunibyou-style alias, or deliberately designed literary name in fiction, yes.** As long as furigana `なんぞ もちいん` is given at first appearance, ateji allows the sound and meaning of a whole group of kanji not to correspond to ordinary character-by-character readings. The Agency for Cultural Affairs also makes clear that the Joyo Kanji Table is not intended to restrict proper nouns or personal written forms, and treats ateji and jukujikun as phenomena where the reading can be determined by the whole word.
2. **As a newly registered legal name in a real Japanese family register, it cannot confidently be said that it would be accepted.** Since 2025, Japan has required family-register name furigana to be, in principle, within the range of "readings generally recognisable as readings of that kanji name"; readings with almost no relation to the kanji's sounds or meanings may be rejected. Existing users have separate arrangements for proving that their reading is already in use. The real issue here is not whether each character can be written, but whether the relation between the whole reading and the written form is sufficiently established.

The author has already confirmed that this is narratively positioned as a pseudonym. Therefore, whether it could be accepted in a real Japanese family register is no longer an acceptance criterion for this design. The boundary above remains only to avoid future publicity, annotation, or localisation mistakenly calling it an ordinary Japanese name that naturally exists in reality. The work only needs the text to give the furigana clearly and let the written form carry the added meanings actively chosen by the protagonist.

## Readability of Each Option

Modern Japanese written forms usually use `曽` and `遅`; `曾` is the old form of `曽`, while `迟` is a simplified Chinese form. If the work interface uses modern Japanese notation, `南曽` and `遅` can be prioritised; whether to deliberately preserve the archaic feel of `南曾` should be decided separately.

| Candidate written form | Proposed reading | Parts supported by regular readings | Parts that need furigana |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `南曽 望遅韻` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `南=ナン`, `曽=ゾ`, `遅=チ`, `韻=イン` | The common on/kun readings of `望` are not simply `モ`; the whole requires an ateji explanation |
| `南曽 百千韻` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `南=ナン`, `曽=ゾ`, `韻=イン`; `百千` also has the non-table reading `ももち` | If forced to read as `もちいん`, it is effectively taking one fewer `も` from `ももち`; `百舌鳥（もず）` can only provide whole-word association, and cannot prove `百=モ` |
| `南曽 忘遅韻` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `南=ナン`, `曽=ゾ`, `遅=チ`, `韻=イン` | The common on/kun readings of `忘` are farther from `モ`; unless opacity is deliberately sought, this is the hardest of the three to read out by itself |
| `何曽 望韻` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `何=なん`, `曽=ゾ`, `望=もち`, and `韻=イン` all have reading support | The normal spelling is `何ぞ`, not `何曽`; the whole written form is still not an existing name or word |
| `南曽 模知音` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `南=ナン`, `曽=ゾ`, `模=モ`, `知音=チイン` | `模知音` is a newly made combination for the pseudonym, not an existing name or fixed word |
| `南曽 模知因` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `南=ナン`, `曽=ゾ`, `模=モ`, `知=チ`, `因=イン` | The character-by-character sound is clear, but the whole looks more like a conceptual code name than an ordinary name |
| `何曽 用韻` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `何=なん`, `曽=ゾ`, `用いる=もちいる`, `韻=イン` | `用韻` is a real word but is usually read `よういん`; here furigana deliberately overrides the regular reading |
| `何曽 望音` | `なんぞ もちいん` | `何=なん`, `曽=ゾ`, `望=もち`, and `音=イン` all have reading support | `望音` is not a fixed word, and still needs furigana to establish the whole reading |

None of these written forms is an ordinary name that readers can reliably read as `なんぞもちいん` just by seeing the kanji. The way they can work is: the text clearly gives the reading, and the kanji carry added meaning. They do not work by having every kanji provide an uncontested single-character reading.

### Input-Method Misuse Is Itself Characterisation

- Early candidates can be explained at the character level as the protagonist experimenting with written forms through a Japanese input method and, relying on the fact that he does not know Japanese very well, "doing whatever he wants".
- Therefore, this design no longer pursues disguising the pseudonym as a natural Japanese name. It pursues "being wrong with a basis": the input method can provide written forms or segmentation associations, and the protagonist then uses furigana to force the whole sound he wants.
- Unnatural word segmentation, rare readings, turning particles into kanji, or overriding the regular reading of an existing word can all become traces of the protagonist's active choice, rather than mistakes the author needs to hide.
- This still requires the work to present its artificiality honestly. If a Japanese speaker later points out that "normally this would not be read this way", the protagonist can acknowledge it, ignore it, or give his own reason; the specific response and timing of revelation belong to presentation and remain undecided.

### Newly Added Candidates Already Retained by the Author

- `何曽 望韻`: currently the most balanced new candidate overall. `何曽` deliberately turns the normal `何ぞ` fully into kanji and directly preserves the original surname `何`; `望韻` provides meanings of wish, poetic rhyme, and "hoping to obtain resonance" at the same time. The cost is that it exposes the structure of the Chinese real name relatively early.
- `南曽 模知音`: the clearest in sound segmentation, and also the strongest in relational implication. `知音` is a person able to understand music and the inner self, while `模` can evoke model, imitation, or a modelled confidant; it may hint too early at the relation between the terminal AI/Interface and the protagonist, so it is more suitable as a late reveal or as a candidate for a specific worldline.
- `南曽 模知因`: compresses model, knowledge, and cause into the same pseudonym, echoing modelling, causality, and abductive reasoning. It is more conceptual than emotional, and may feel more like a researcher's online name, theoretical signature, or in-worldline code name.
- `何曽 用韻`: best shows the protagonist's character as someone who "does not know Japanese well but insists on reading it this way". `用韻` originally means the use of rhyme in poetry and is usually read `よういん`; the protagonist borrows the `もちいる` of `用いる` and forces the reading into `もちいん`, making error, poetic allusion, and self-mockery coexist.
- `何曽 望音`: points more directly than `望韻` toward sound, messages, awaiting response, and echoes across distance, and can also link back to the Qiang flute; the cost is losing part of the explicit poetic-metre meaning.

`何曽` and `南曽` currently represent two different directions: the former preserves `何` and the interrogative structure of `何ぞ`, while the latter is more like an input-method pseudonym that conceals the Chinese real name. They can be discussed for interchange later, but this file does not make a Cartesian product of all surnames and given names, and does not automatically expand more candidates.

### `百舌鳥`, Shrike, and the *Hyperion* Echo

- `百舌鳥（もず）` is a special whole-word written form for a shrike-like bird name. Linguistically, it cannot prove that the single character `百` can be freely read as `も`, but the current goal is a pseudonym with furigana, so this limit does not obstruct the literary design.
- If `百千韻` is adopted, `百` can intentionally evoke `百舌鳥`, then form an intertextual relation with *Hyperion* through the shrike/Shrike image. This connection is the author's chosen literary association, not an argument about Japanese naming convention.
- In this way, `百千韻` can carry "hundreds and thousands", distant writing/enumerated futures, and the shrike image at the same time. Whether to let readers explicitly recognise the *Hyperion* echo, or keep it only as a more hidden naming layer, remains to be decided.

## Worldline and Presentation Boundaries

- If a particular ateji is adopted later, it can become the local truth of one worldline. It has not currently been assigned, so it remains an inactive candidate rather than being written into `story_state/` in advance.
- Different worldlines can use different ateji, but it must also be decided separately whether the change occurs in the character's real name, an actively chosen pseudonym, a work signature, or the nameplate shown to the player.
- If different written forms carry the function of revealing character stages or worldline differences, the corresponding timing of appearance, whether furigana is displayed, and when the player discovers the homophonic relation should later enter `presentation/`.
- Whether the Chinese real name is constant across worldlines is also not decided by this file.
