# Historical Modification Ban: Crowded Universe and Cross-Civilisational Risk Governance

## Status

This is an origin candidate for the ban that the author proposed early and is not currently developing. It can provide an institutional and risk logic for the confirmed existence of the historical modification ban, but it does not confirm that extraterrestrial civilisations, interstellar alliances or already mature cross-civilisational law exist in the universe.

## Candidate Reasoning

- Suppose many civilisations exist in the universe and may be affected by causal modification, and that a time machine can return to the past and form a reality with macroscopic differences. In that case, the consequences of one local intervention may far exceed the inventor's own society.
- For a civilisation located in the current reality, "this universe still exists" is itself a basic condition that cannot be lightly wagered. Even if the risk of catastrophe from one modification is low, as long as the worst consequence involves the current universe, many civilisations or the replacement of all existing history, governors may adopt an extremely strong precautionary principle.
- Civilisations that have already mastered time technology may monitor the technological transitions of earlier civilisations, announce their own existence when the latter first obtain the ability to intervene in history, impose restrictions, or establish some kind of cross-civilisational joint governance.
- In this version, the ban is first a kind of externality governance: inventors have no right to measure only local benefits when evaluating interventions that place other civilisations and an entire stretch of history at risk together.

## Potential Conflict with the Protagonist's Enumeration Idea

This candidate can also preserve one currently unadopted character motive: the protagonist's valuing of "present - universe - existence" may be one reason why his future-enumeration idea comes into conflict with the terminal AI's practice of real enumeration.

- The protagonist's enumeration method could originally treat the currently existing world as something that needs protection: imagining many futures is meant to let people living in it prepare for risks, not to turn this world itself into search consumable.
- The terminal AI inherits the direction of reducing destruction risk through broad enumeration, but may make whole worldlines and the lives within them actually bear the search cost. From this, a structural conflict can form between "preparing futures for current existence" and "using real worldlines to seek a future that can continue".
- This motive is currently neither a verbatim belief already confirmed for the protagonist, nor the only explanation for the conflict between the two sides. It does not automatically confirm that the protagonist supports a specific cross-civilisational-ban institution, still less that the current worldline is naturally higher than other worldlines ontologically or ethically.

If this direction is activated later, it will also be necessary to distinguish whether what the protagonist values is the people and relationships immediately before him, already happened history, any reality that has obtained current status, or the fact that "the universe is existing" itself; these objects may lead to different ethical languages.

## Unresolved

- Whether "modifying the past" makes the original reality disappear, forms a new current worldline, or only changes relations of access; different mechanisms will change the risk rationale for the ban.
- Why the universe is "crowded", how civilisations learn about one another, and how effective governance forms without faster-than-light communication all remain undetermined.
- Whether monitoring and enforcement themselves require using the prohibited time technology, and who supervises the civilisation that first possesses that technology, remain institutional paradoxes.
- Whether exceptions are allowed in extreme cases, how the ban is maintained, whether agents have aligned goals, and how the terminal AI's real enumeration is authorised or bypasses the system all still require separate development.

## Must Not Be Automatically Derived

- This candidate does not confirm that extraterrestrial civilisations, the dark forest, interstellar united forces or cosmic politics from any specific work have entered the main work.
- The claim that a time machine is "more dangerous" than nuclear weapons or faster-than-light technology is only a comparison that needs mechanism and scope to support it, not an unconditional law.
- A risk-governance rationale does not automatically prove that the ban is legitimate, effective or incorruptible, and does not exclude the possibility that the ban protects existing power structures.
- Valuing the current worldline does not mean the ethical problem after old worldlines are replaced has already been answered.
