# Dangerous Knowledge Elimination: Ghost System and Carrier Network

## Status

This is an inactive conflict and narrative-structure candidate. It does not belong to any established worldline, and it does not confirm specific technologies, organisations, characters, death counts, or endings.

It preserves only a set of structures that can be developed independently: dangerous knowledge can survive only through dissemination; dissemination in turn turns new knowers into potential targets; an action network without a single centre, whose members do not share identical motives, can continuously eliminate carriers through shared risk classifications and infrastructure; characters must decide how to make facts into verifiable common knowledge without fully releasing dangerous capabilities.

For related problem axes, see [Social Engineering, Performative Sincerity, and Reverse Profiling](社会工程_表演性真诚与反向画像.md) and [Serial Enumeration Ethics and the New Multiverse](串行枚举伦理与新多元宇宙.md). These connections do not mean that this candidate has already been adopted by them.

## One-Sentence Conflict Engine

If a fact is held by only a few people, it may disappear with them; yet every additional person able to prove it may add another name to the elimination network.

The characters do not face a simple choice between "public" and "secret", but four questions that must be handled separately:

1. How to make it impossible for "something really happened" to be erased by one strike;
2. How to let the public or relevant groups know who else knows, forming the common knowledge needed for action;
3. How to preserve independent verification and correction;
4. How to avoid giving any one recipient all the material needed to reproduce the dangerous capability.

## Conflict Structure

### Knowledge Is Not a Fixed Headcount Threshold

"Once enough people know, it is safe" is too simple. At least the following must be distinguished:

- Information survival: a claim will not disappear with one person or one server;
- Verifiability: later people can still judge that the claim is not forged;
- Reproducibility: whether recipients can rebuild that capability;
- Common knowledge: whether participants know that others also know, and can therefore coordinate;
- Capacity for action: even if the fact survives, whether the characters still have the resources to protect people, pursue accountability, or change institutions.

These thresholds can be reached one after another, and they can also conflict with one another. The safest way to preserve information may not be enough to support immediate action; the mode of publicity that most quickly forms common knowledge may also release an irreversible danger.

### The Eliminating Side Need Not Be a Unified Villain

Elimination can come from a real convergence of action without a single visible centre:

- Different institutions share the same risk label, list, contracting chain, or minimal handling signal, while not knowing the whole picture;
- Some people protect monopoly and existing responsibility, some try to prevent panic or an arms race, and some merely execute local compliance rules;
- Some participants may sincerely believe that suppressing dissemination will reduce overall harm;
- Open attacks, revocation of qualifications, itinerary and identity friction, forced silence, and ordinary accidents can appear at the same time;
- The eliminating side will misjudge, omit, suffer internal conflict, and mistakenly include people who were originally unrelated.

This dispersion cannot absolve any participant of responsibility. It only means that the conflict does not depend on one omniscient villain, and that the characters cannot solve the problem automatically by defeating one centre.

### What the "Ghost System" Is

"Ghost system" is, for now, only the author's working label. It means that the characters first see repeated consequences, but cannot find a single subject that can be held responsible for all of them.

It cannot be merely an imagined enemy. If adopted, elimination, resource scheduling, risk classification, and institutional friction must have real material carriers. Its ghostliness comes from:

- Each participant acknowledges only that they completed one local operation;
- The same judgement appears repeatedly across systems that have no direct hierarchy between them;
- No single document can draw the complete organisational chart;
- Even if one node exits, other nodes will still infer similar actions from shared conditions.

The characters' naming of it can also change it in return. Once an investigator provides a unified explanation, a relationship diagram, and reusable terminology, the previously loose actors, resisters, and bystanders may all coordinate through it. The characters do not create a non-existent enemy through language; by describing real links, they lower the cost for those links to self-organise. This version sits near self-fulfilling language or hyperstition, but it does not need to write language as magic.

### Dissemination Is Both Protection and Conscription

Every act of disclosure should have a recognisable ethical object:

- Whether the recipient knows what risk they will bear;
- Whether they can refuse to receive more content;
- Whether someone who receives only verification fragments will still be treated by the eliminating side as a knower;
- Whether the risk classification will really be updated after they exit;
- Whether the person hosting dissemination can arrange succession, correction, and stopping conditions, instead of permanently handing emergency responsibility to a few people.

For this reason, "letting more people know" cannot naturally be treated as brave or democratic. It may also turn others into carrier media without their consent.

## Combinable Secret Objects

The following eight items provide only conflict shapes. They are not settings that must coexist in the same worldline. When reactivating this file, first choose one secret that players can understand earliest, then judge whether a second item is needed to carry a tool, evidence, or consequence.

### Limited Capacity to Reconstruct the Past

Records, model changes, and institutional trajectories scattered across everyday systems can already reconstruct events that supposedly disappeared. The truly dangerous claim is not that the system can know the whole past, but that a limited capability has already been used in secret, and is enough to re-expose characters, reclassify old events, or support targeted action.

This object suits combination with the causal residue of "the records have been deleted, but the change still exists." It must preserve multiple interpretations, misjudgements, and limits of resolution, and cannot directly escalate into the terminal observer's ability to read the complete world.

### A Self-Fulfilling List of High-Leverage People

Several institutions use a rough risk classification to find people who might turn local knowledge into public action. The list cannot accurately predict the future; notification, isolation, resource withdrawal, and elimination nevertheless force people on the list to contact one another and take action, gradually manufacturing "potential high-leverage people" into a real network.

This object provides concrete targets and mortality pressure, but it can easily fall into the familiar type of list-based manhunt. It needs at least one misjudgement, and one person who does not suddenly become a hero just because they are listed.

### Artificially Maintained Cooperation Failure

If evidence around relations between humans and AI or other factions is used, it does not have to be written as a successful ceasefire that was erased. The more tense object is this: a cooperation really did fail, but that failure was not the natural result of the two sides' incompatible natures; a number of institutional arrangements, incentives, information separations, or third-party interventions repeatedly produced the same kind of failure.

The secret, then, is not "peace once succeeded", but "conflict requires continuous investment to be maintained." The evidence can only prove that the failure mechanism can be identified and partially removed. It cannot guarantee that the next cooperation will necessarily succeed.

### Operational Sovereignty of Urban Public Systems

A switch described as a technical upgrade or security update will make a city, institution, or community lose the ability to keep operating outside a single technology stack. The characters' actions are not the publication of principles, but the building of a minimal alternative stack before the deadline, the cutting of unauditable dependencies, or the enabling of multiple sites to continue working after the centre stops service.

This object most easily produces material texture around power cuts, transport, identity, medicine, and wages, and it also most easily turns Xuyuan into an all-purpose engineer. Technical execution must be given to multiple people with specific posts; Xuyuan occupies only a relational, procedural, or non-substitutable judgement position.

### Deregistered Subjects in "Unmanned War"

So-called unmanned systems may preserve human or AI subjects who have been institutionally reclassified and denied casualty status: remote operators, wetware, copied learners, personality fragments left in weapon-control chains, or labourers erased from war by propaganda language.

The secret is not the one-line reversal that "machines actually feel pain too", but which subjects are deregistered in what way, who benefits from the unmanned narrative, and how existing war responsibility would be redistributed after testimony is acknowledged. Adopting it would clearly change the work's genre, and cannot rely on early anime topics alone as foreshadowing.

### Evidence of the First Historical Intervention

Someone discovers that a seemingly ordinary historical anomaly actually comes from an intervention that has not yet been made public, and that later prohibitions on time machines may be forming from this failure. The characters must decide whether to preserve, publicise, or isolate the evidence, while also facing the problem that "knowing an intervention happened" may itself change the target of the next intervention.

It has the strongest connection to the global setting, and it also opens the top-level mechanism too early most easily. If adopted, players should first come to know the ordinary life that was changed, then understand its place in the historical structure.

### A Secret Existing Only in Differences Between Private Information Landscapes

No one person's records contain the complete secret. It appears only among differences in routes, eligibility, recommendations, absences, and timing across multiple people; any single screenshot can be reasonably explained, and only active comparison produces a common object.

This object is closest to everyday social engineering, but if it is not connected to public resources, loss of rights, or named casualties, it cannot support large-scale elimination by itself. See the next section for concrete scenes.

### A Capability That Becomes Realisable Through Dissemination

The original knowledge may be only a set of fragments that cannot yet operate independently. The danger of publication comes not only from leaking an already-made weapon, but also from publication simultaneously providing:

- A conceptual map that recognises different fragments as one scheme;
- Coordinating language for finding complementary personnel and resources;
- Testing standards for judging which gaps have not yet been filled;
- Shared expectations that let multiple actors believe others will also continue investing.

Knowledge therefore "manufactures its own object", but what it manufactures is collaborative conditions and acting subjects, not material outcomes generated without cause. This version most easily becomes abstract again, and must be paired with an on-site action that can fail, hurt people, and be misread.

## How Private Information Landscapes Become Visible Collectively

If this structure is written only as "everyone compares recommendation pages and discovers manipulation", it will feel weak. A more concrete candidate is to have several people originally trying to approach the same place, person, or public procedure, while each receives a completely reasonable but mutually different blockage:

- One person's route is diverted;
- One person's appointment is delayed;
- One person's eligibility page shows that supplementary materials are required;
- One person sees only another event that better fits their profile;
- Another person encounters no anomaly, and therefore first suspects that the others are overinterpreting.

Seen alone, none of these is enough to prove coordination. Only when the people involved actively compare timestamps, interface states, and later outcomes at the same time and place does the shared exclusion target become visible.

This scene can also immediately produce a second-order change: the act of comparison itself enters the system, new pages begin to synchronise, one eligibility status suddenly recovers, or a previously safe person is added to the same risk cluster. The characters discover from this that they are not only observing the system; they are also marking out a new relationship network for the system.

This design remains a candidate. If the secret object has no connection to public resources, casualties, or irreversible consequences for rights, recommendation differences alone are not enough to support large-scale elimination.

## Three Main Axes for Combination

### Ghost System

Limited past reconstruction explains what the secret is, the high-leverage list explains how elimination finds people, and private landscape differences explain how players see convergent action through everyday consequences. learner can preserve a local change that cannot be explained by existing records, but does not carry the whole task.

### Manufactured Failure

Cooperation failure provides characters and relationships, causal residue provides incomplete evidence, and private landscape comparison shows how different institutions continuously manufacture failure conditions. The focus is not the restoration of a perfect ceasefire, but enabling the characters to recognise why conflict needs to be maintained again and again.

### Knowledge Manufactures Its Own Object

A transmissible capability serves as the secret, the high-leverage list provides pre-emptive elimination, and operational sovereignty or unmanned war provides the material consequences after publication. It has the strongest ethical echo of real enumeration, and also most needs named characters and on-site failure to limit theoretical inflation.

All three groups are only detachable combinations. They cannot be evenly mixed without character need, and they do not constitute the current order of recommendation.

## Reusable Narrative Sequence

The following is a skeleton of plot blocks. It is not a set of player-visible chapters, and it does not require each step to become a separate scene.

### 1. A Useful Everyday System

First let the relevant infrastructure genuinely help people. Later repression cannot rewrite all earlier convenience as disguise.

### 2. A Person Not Yet Marked

The player first comes to know, in ordinary relationships, a person who later becomes a carrier. This person should have desires, work, and mistakes unrelated to the main conflict, and can also disagree with the protagonist.

### 3. A First Loss Still Explainable as an Ordinary Event

Death, disappearance, rollback, or forced silence first appears through unanswered messages, an empty seat, a changed voice, and the institutional language received by family members. Theoretical explanation comes after the loss.

### 4. Private Anomalies Begin to Align

Several people actively compare their routes, eligibility, recommendations, records, or model responses. The player first sees repeated operating conditions, then hears the structure behind them named.

### 5. A Continuation Request Arrives

The messenger brings not a complete explanation of a conspiracy, but a small number of independently verifiable facts and one continuation condition left in advance by the dead or missing person. The protagonist is important because he must make a decision that cannot be answered on his behalf by archives or profiles, not because only he can understand the world.

### 6. The First Person Who Refuses to Know

A potential witness explicitly refuses to receive more information. The narrative must acknowledge this refusal; at the same time, the risk system may already have included this person on the basis of relationships or behaviour, so that "refusing knowledge" and "leaving danger" are no longer equivalent.

### 7. An Incomplete Residue That Cannot Be Easily Forged

The evidence does not have to restate the secret itself. A changed judgement, mutually misaligned records, an abnormal choice by a non-human witness, or an institutional trajectory that cannot be explained by existing causes can only prove that a piece of cause is missing here.

### 8. The First Limited Dissemination Fails

The network opens only minimal fragments to one independent verifier; that person then disappears, suffers public handling, or is forced to deny. The first attempt to add redundancy becomes a perceptible human cost.

### 9. Designing Public Witnessing

The core decision is not only whether to upload, but who can voluntarily join, how much each person knows, how they confirm one another, how correction works, when they exit, and whether the procedure can continue after the death of any centre.

### 10. Elimination and Publicity Happen at the Same Time

One transmission is cut off, one chair becomes empty, one version is replaced; elsewhere, someone still finishes saying the same verifiable fact. Action success and character loss must be able to hold at the same time.

### 11. A Bad World After Knowledge Survives

After common knowledge forms, real accountability, panic, imitation, performative sincerity, reverse profiling, and new mutual-aid etiquette appear together. Publicity is not a victory subtitle, and it will not restore the killed.

### 12. The Ending Answers the Organisational Question

Different endings should not merely swap death images; they should answer, respectively, at which step knowledge, organisation, and responsibility failed or survived.

## Ending Grammar

- The secret disappears with the last key interpreter: the characters avoid conscripting others, but underestimate that the eliminating side does not recognise exit.
- The centralised network is decapitated in one strike: the materials remain, but the relationship structure capable of explanation, verification, and correction disappears.
- Knowledge can no longer disappear with the protagonist's death: the task succeeds in a limited sense, the character is still killed, and later people can continue correcting without having to deify him.
- A character dies at the hands of another side after publicity: dissemination has changed factions and risk, and repression can no longer be summarised by one hidden alliance.
- A character encounters "civil death" in the sense of social identity, capacity for action, or relational meaning: biological life continues, but ordinary life has already been interrupted.
- A character survives long-term and becomes an irreplaceable maintainer: the task has not failed, but emergency responsibility consumes his time because exit and succession conditions are missing.

Death itself cannot automatically prove that a character's choice was correct, and cannot alone constitute a sufficient reason for a far-future observer to intervene personally.

## Presentation Principles

- Give names before numbers; at least one loss needs full development, while other scale can be expanded through montage.
- Repeat empty chairs, avatars that stop updating, overwritten voices, identical risk phrasing, and messages with no recipient, instead of repeatedly explaining theory.
- Preserve at least one mistaken attribution; the protagonist noticing a pattern does not mean every suspicion is correct.
- Do not show a villain meeting capable of explaining the whole picture. Let the structure become visible through mutually contradictory documents, partial admissions, executors who refuse, and different modes of handling.
- Do not write recipients as faceless data redundancy. Acceptance, refusal, misunderstanding, and exit should all change relationships.
- Let one concrete action carry the abstract claim: comparing private interfaces at the same location, verifying a causal residue, a witness disappearing during publicity, or the continuation procedure still running after its host dies.

If this structure is later connected to Xuyuan's task line, death and unhappiness can only provide pressure. They cannot by themselves explain why the terminal AI must become Interface. The complete reason for her personal entry--ethical resonance, being pointed to by a future observer, private feeling, the difference between complete records and a shared future, and double motivation--is maintained centrally by [The Protagonist and Interface: Abductive-Intuition Mirror and the Purpose of Entering the World](../creative_work/development/主人公与Interface_溯因直觉镜像及进入世界的目的.md).

This candidate provides only one possible ethical mirror: Xuyuan makes real characters bear the cost of dissemination paths, and the terminal AI makes real worldlines bear the cost of enumeration. The similarity can raise the question, but it cannot decide the answer for her, and it cannot prove that she must enter.

## What Must Be Decided Before Reactivation

1. What does the player first lose concretely: a person, a relationship, many people's safety, a city's operational capacity, or current history itself?
2. What ordinary-language sentence can explain the dangerous knowledge? Does it prove that a fact exists, provide a method of verification, or also allow a capability to be reproduced?
3. Who was the first carrier before the danger appeared? Why would the player care about their empty seat?
4. What visible action must the protagonist complete? Who can refuse to be included?
5. After publicity succeeds, which bad consequence still cannot be reversed?

Before these questions are answered, this candidate should not be expanded into fixed factions, a technological history, or cross-chapter official history.
