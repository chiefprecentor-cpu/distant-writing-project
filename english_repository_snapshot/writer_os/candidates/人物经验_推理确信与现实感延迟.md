# Character Experience: Inferential Certainty and Delayed Sense of Reality

## Status

This is a phenomenological candidate not assigned to any concrete character or event. It is not a medical or psychological model; the concrete character, worldline, triggering event, and narrative function are all `TBD`.

## Core Image of the Experience

When an event or inferential result seriously conflicts with a character's previous experiential frame, the character may successively experience:

1. denial or the search for an ordinary explanation;
2. in a certain instant, becoming certain with an intensity like ecstasy, fear, or collapse that "it really happened/this inference really points here";
3. still being unable to deny it cognitively, while the sensory and emotional sense of reality begins to recede or become numb.

The most concise literary expression is:

> It happened, but it does not feel like reality.

The focus of this dislocation is not that the character does not know the fact, but that "knowing" and "living inside this fact" have not been completed synchronously. The propositional judgment may already have crossed the threshold, while the body, emotions, and everyday world remain in the old frame.

## Possible Uses

- After the protagonist infers an overly grand conclusion from scattered signs, facing the rupture between his own inferential capacity and his limited position for action;
- a character's first confirmation that the terminal AI, real enumeration, or worldline succession is not a metaphor;
- Interface already possesses propositional knowledge, but for the first time obtains from local presence the indexical understanding that "this is happening to me";
- after experiencing an irreversible event, a character can still recite the facts, but for the moment cannot let the facts acquire the texture of everyday reality.

## Boundaries

- This is not a fixed three-stage diagnosis, and does not claim that numbness must be some single "physiological protective mechanism".
- Intense certainty is not automatically treated as evidence that the inference is correct; the character may be certain and wrong, or may misunderstand the cause when the conclusion is correct.
- It is different from the protagonist's "seismographic sensitivity": the latter occurs when sensing the direction of the future from microscopic signs, while this candidate focuses more on how cognition and sense of reality become misaligned after a conclusion has already formed.
- If used for Interface, it needs to be checked against the first-entry candidate of "knowing propositionally, understanding in the first person"; human experience cannot simply be pasted unchanged onto a nonhuman subject.
