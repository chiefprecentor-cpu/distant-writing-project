# Presentation Method: Literary Collage, Juxtaposition and Montage

## Status

This is a presentation-method candidate that is explicitly preserved and not currently activated into concrete scenes. It records an organisational language that may suit a high-information-density visual novel. It does not require the main work to adopt a structure like *The Waste Land*, and does not constitute a fixed UI scheme.

## Working Distinctions

- **Collage**: placing fragments from different texts, registers, media, periods or value levels within the same work structure, making the seams themselves visible.
- **Juxtaposition**: placing two or more materials near one another, so that difference, similarity or mutually rewriting meaning is produced by adjacency.
- **Montage**: emphasising the editing and sequence of shots, scenes or information units; it can switch quickly, and can also form a judgement through combination before and after that no single shot contains.
- **Fragmentation**: describing a state in which material appears as fragments, voices are not unified or connections are incomplete; fragmentation can be an effect of collage, but the two are not synonyms.
- **Lack of explicit transition**: making the reader bear the work of filling in relations is an optional intensity, not a rule that collage must completely cancel narrative guidance.

## Possible Value for This Project

- Juxtapose the macro-structural language of the terminal AI with a small piece of character experience occurring along `t`, so that the difference in scale is produced through reading rather than explanation.
- Let the same short phrase, image or sound recur in different worldlines and `π` positions, and then be reinterpreted because of new information.
- Contain theory, everyday speech, system records, character work and environmental details without forcing them first into a single narrative register.
- Use seams to express relations among memory, worldlines and subject versions that are connected yet discontinuous.

## Framing and Ordering Are Also Presentation Institutions

The Chaucer-Keats *Hyperion* poems-Simmons *Hyperion* line of borrowing adds a mechanism distinct from collage: placing materials inside the same work is not enough. Who speaks first, how long they can speak, who hosts and evaluates, and who can interrupt or demand retelling will also change the meaning of each piece of material. A frame is therefore not a neutral container, but an institution that distributes narrative time, attention and credibility.

This is especially important for `π`. Even if a set of fragments remains literally unchanged, its order of appearance, preceding character portrait, whether rereading is allowed, and who responds afterward may rewrite it from private memory into testimony, data, defence, entertainment or community event. Seemingly random ordering will not automatically remove existing differences of authority among characters.

For the concrete tradition and use boundaries, see [Chaucer-Keats *Hyperion* Poems-Simmons *Hyperion* Line of Borrowing](文学传统_乔叟_济慈Hyperion诗篇_Simmons海伯利安借鉴线.md). This file still preserves only a general presentation candidate, and does not specify the framing role of a Grand Curator, protagonist or Interface.

## Use Boundary

- Collage does not mean throwing all concepts onto the screen at once. The work already has a high setting burden; each juxtaposition still needs a perceptible rhythm, contrast or echo, or it will only pass responsibility for explanation to the player.
- The mixture of highbrow/popular or grand/trivial should not by default use one side to mock or certify the other. Everyday material can change the meaning of theory, and theory can also change the scale of everyday material.
- This candidate does not presume fixed UI forms such as split-screen logs, lyrics, philosophical quotations or glitch art. Before any third-party text or protected material enters the main work, authorisation and use boundaries must be confirmed separately.
- When a concrete scene adopts this, it should be moved into `creative_work/presentation/`, explaining what the player sees at that moment, how the materials are ordered, whether review is allowed, and what expected understanding the lack of transition will produce.
