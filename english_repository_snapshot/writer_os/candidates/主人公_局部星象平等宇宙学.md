# Protagonist: Local-Asterism Egalitarian Cosmology

## Status

This is an explicitly preserved inactive candidate that is not currently being developed. It preserves one possible cosmological expression the protagonist's egalitarianism might adopt, but it does not confirm this expression as the novel's true cosmic structure, political system, or a theory the protagonist has already completed.

## Core Direction Proposed by the Author

- Explore whether an egalitarian cosmology echoing "everyone can become Yao or Shun" can be constructed on the basis of local asterisms.
- The traditional celestial-pole/Son-of-Heaven analogy can be reinterpreted, because the celestial pole is not the same as the observer's zenith, the observing location changes the local sky, and the pole star does not permanently occupy the same position.
- This cosmology first serves the protagonist's thought, work, or conjecture. It does not automatically describe the novel's universe itself.

## Intellectual-Historical Context to Preserve

This candidate cannot be reduced to an abstract geometric metaphor of "zenith, celestial pole, and observing location". At minimum, it needs to preserve intellectual-historical anchors such as Taiyi, Qi-region fangshi, Confucian scholars, the Jixia Academy, and Huang-Lao, so that the Chinese cosmological-political tradition it rewrites remains visible. The following content is creative context and intellectual-historical anchors pending verification, not laws of the novel's true universe.

### Taiyi: A Polysemous Centre Among Origin, Deity, and Celestial Pole

- "Taiyi" can simultaneously point to the undivided "One" or cosmic origin of all things, a supreme deity above the other gods, and a star deity/spatial position related to the celestial pole, Beichen/North Star, and the dwelling place of the Heavenly Emperor. It is not a single personified god whose image and functions remained entirely fixed from the pre-Qin period to later periods.
- *Taiyi Generates Water* from Warring States Chu and *Nine Songs: Donghuang Taiyi* give it a background of both cosmic generation and sacrificial honoured deity; the Qin and Han state then reorganised different local sacrifices, astronomical order, and imperial power, allowing Taiyi to be interpreted as the central cosmic deity "above the Five Emperors".
- During Emperor Wu of Han's reign, Taiyi entered a high position in state sacrifice. Fangshi, astronomical knowledge, and the emperor's pursuit of immortality all participated in this construction; after the later Western Han, Confucian classical learning and ritual officials tended to absorb or reinterpret Taiyi through "Haotian Shangdi/Huangtian Shangdi", which had stronger classical grounding, causing Taiyi's independent supreme-god status to decline and differentiate.
- What matters most for this candidate is not reconstructing a single correct divine genealogy of Taiyi, but preserving a question: why were the abstract "One", the invisible position of the celestial pole, and the real-world emperor who claimed to represent it merged, and can the protagonist separate the three again?

### Qi-Region Fangshi and Confucian Scholars: Who Has the Right to Interpret Heaven's Intention

- In this candidate, the Qi-Yan fangshi tradition is provisionally summarised through forms of knowledge such as yin-yang, the Five Emperors, astral divination, calendrical numbers, fengshan sacrifices, the pursuit of immortality, auspicious signs, and special sacrificial methods. This kind of authority often appears as techniques or secret interpretations held by a small number of experts.
- Confucian scholars did not simply oppose spirits or sacrifice. They were more likely to use public classics such as the *Odes*, *Documents*, and *Rites*, precedents of sage kings, and ritual order to contest jurisdiction over "who should be sacrificed to, how sacrifices should be made, and what interpretation of Heaven's intention is valid".
- Therefore the conflict between the two should not be written as "reason against superstition", but can be understood as different knowledge groups competing over the authority to interpret the cosmos, design rituals, and define imperial legitimacy. The Han court often absorbed both sides at once, then let the emperor decide which interpretations became state institutions.
- This adds an institutional problem to the protagonist's egalitarian cosmology: even if one recognises a "Heaven" or public centre higher than the ruler, if interpretive authority is still monopolised by the emperor, fangshi, or classical masters, it may still serve a new inequality.

### Jixia Academy and Huang-Lao: How Cosmic Order Becomes Governance Technique

- The Jixia Academy can serve as a historical image of many kinds of Qi-region knowledge meeting: Daoists, yin-yang thinkers, Legalists, Confucians, military thinkers, health-cultivation practices, and fang techniques did not always have modern disciplinary boundaries. Treating "Jixia Huang-Lao" as a single school with a strict line of transmission still requires caution; it is better used as the name for a mixed intellectual ecology.
- Huang-Lao is not a deity, and is not equivalent to Qi-region fangshi. It connects the "Dao", tranquillity, and non-action with law, names and roles, bureaucratic division of labour, and ruler technique. It can be summarised as follows: the cosmos has an order that should not be repeatedly disturbed by personal likes and dislikes, and the governor uses institutions and division of labour to keep the system operating.
- Huang-Lao and fangshi can share vocabulary such as the Yellow Emperor, yin-yang, the Way of Heaven, and nourishing life, but they respectively lean toward governance theory and techniques for communicating with spirits/divination. Their overlap and difference can help the work avoid compressing ancient thought into a vague "Eastern mysticism".
- If the protagonist borrows these resources, the question is not only "how to prove everyone is equal", but also: how to make common coordinates, perspective conversion, and transfer of power into public procedures, rather than occult techniques held by a single centre.

### Meaning of Confluence for the Candidate Cosmology

Here the historical confluence is temporarily understood as follows: the Chu Taiyi tradition, Qin-Han imperial sacrifice, Qi-region cosmological and fang-technique knowledge, and Huang-Lao governance thought later also underwent Confucian classicisation and ritualisation. This summary is only used to preserve a creative map. Specific periods, figures, textual attributions, and mutual influences still need to be checked before entering the main text.

The protagonist's local-asterism egalitarian cosmology is therefore not simply deriving ethics directly from natural facts, but rewriting a history of "who can occupy the cosmic centre, and who can interpret Heaven's intention". It can place Taiyi's unity beside the fact that everyone has their own zenith, transform secret interpretive authority into observation from multiple locations and public conversion, and turn the celestial pole monopolised by emperors into a public position with which no one can become identical.

## Boundaries Already Confirmed by the Author: Anti-Structural Determinism and Ongoing Maintenance

The following principles have been confirmed by the author. They constrain how this candidate develops later, but they do not promote the entire local-asterism cosmology into the novel's true cosmic structure or a theory the protagonist has already completed.

### Modern Analogy Between Huang-Lao and Harness Engineering

- Huang-Lao governance structure can be analogised by the protagonist or the work to modern governance systems and harness engineering: the highest actor does not need to personally handle every local problem, but enables multiple actors to work within a set of scaffolding through laws and standards, roles, permissions, interfaces, feedback, and division of labour.
- This is a modern creative analogy, not a historical equivalence that "Huang-Lao is ancient harness engineering". Huang-Lao involves the ruler, bureaucracy, and cosmic order, while harness engineering belongs to another technical and organisational context.
- What the two jointly reveal is only a structural problem: how to reduce dependence on a single actor's immediate judgment, how to constrain local behaviour, and how to make collaboration and correction possible.

### Structure Cannot Replace Actor Responsibility

- Governance structure is not the governing subject. Structure can make responsibility visible, distributable, transferable, accountable, and repairable, but it cannot itself generate actors' competence, honesty, sense of responsibility, or judgment about concrete situations.
- Structure itself cannot guarantee that an organisation will not become corrupt, and cannot guarantee that the agents within it will complete the work. Actors at least need to understand the shared goal, fulfil the responsibilities of their roles, and keep their actual actions sufficiently aligned with the overall goal the structure is meant to protect.
- But "goal alignment" is not equivalent to unconditional obedience to the current institution. The goal publicly declared by an institution may drift, be tampered with, or degenerate from protecting the shared world into maintaining the organisation's own existence; requiring all actors to remain completely aligned can instead reproduce the foolish loyalty criticised by the protagonist.
- Therefore, actors need to face four levels at once: the people and shared world protected by the institution, the basic value or mission, their own role obligations, and the observable facts in front of them. When institutional means, official goals, and the protected object come into conflict, responsible actors have a duty to reveal the problem, raise opposition, and refuse execution when necessary.
- A good agent is not the agent most obedient to the harness, but one who can work reliably within the structure without outsourcing moral judgment entirely to the structure. "Everyone can be an equal Son of Heaven" here means that every actor still retains the qualification to judge public good and evil and bear the corresponding responsibility, not that everyone competes for the single centre.

### Maintenance Is an Ongoing Constituent of Structure

- Structure and work content both require constant maintenance. There is no perfect institution that can run correctly forever after design is complete. Maintenance is not an auxiliary cost after construction, but a constituent part of structure's continued existence.
- Maintenance at least includes: continuously checking the practical meaning of goals, training and replacing personnel, preserving institutional memory and necessary information, revising permissions, incentives, and feedback mechanisms, handling accumulated maintenance debt, and modifying structure when the environment and work objects change.
- Structure can corrupt through personnel turnover, goal drift, metrics replacing real purposes, information asymmetry, incentive misalignment, and organisational self-preservation. No matter how refined the initial design is, it cannot eliminate these changes once and for all.
- Avoiding micromanagement by the highest actor in the name of "non-action" can be a reasonable principle of division of labour; ignoring observation, repair, and updating in the name of "non-action" only makes structure rigid. Maintenance itself is an action that cannot be omitted.

### Do Not Replace Public Purpose with Institutional Permanence

- It is not reliable to achieve permanent regime survival by relying on a foolproof governance structure. An organisation that elevates its own continuation into the highest goal may sacrifice the people, values, and shared world it originally meant to protect in order to preserve itself.
- A more reliable goal is not to guarantee that the structure never becomes corrupt or never fails, but to preserve the ability to discover problems, publicly oppose them, repair, transfer, and peacefully replace structures. When necessary, an institution's ability to end or be replaced can also be part of fulfilling its public responsibility.
- The "empty pole position" therefore cannot be understood as the necessary eternity of a certain office or institutional form. What can be pursued as long-lasting is the public purpose and the protected object, not the permanent identity between that purpose and a certain office-holder, regime, or harness.

This set of principles can be compressed into two formulations for later use:

> Institutions cannot bear responsibility in people's place; they can only provide scaffolding for actors to work responsibly, correct one another, and continuously maintain shared goals.
>
> Good governance does not guarantee never becoming corrupt. It must preserve the capacity to discover, limit, and repair corruption, and to replace itself when necessary.

## Preserved but Demoted AI Expansion

The following language can be developed again later, but at present it has not received line-by-line confirmation from the author:

- everyone has their own zenith, and therefore everyone has a local subject position that cannot be cancelled;
- everyone has a centre, but no one's local centre equals the only centre under Heaven;
- observations from different locations each have limitations, but can understand one another through shared coordinates and conversion rules;
- the celestial pole can be imagined as a public position that no one can permanently possess, while the pole star is only temporarily close to it;
- "Yao and Shun" do not have to be people occupying the highest position, and can also be re-understood as those best able to change perspectives, translate, and protect different local viewpoints.

The value of these formulations mainly lies in providing symbolic grammar for egalitarianism, not in letting astronomy provide natural proof for ethical conclusions.

## Issues to Handle When Restarting Later

- Whether the protagonist truly believes this cosmology, or only proposes it in a work and then personally refutes it;
- how "local centre" avoids sliding into a relativism in which each person possesses incommensurable truth;
- whether the "empty pole position" still secretly preserves a single centre and imperial order;
- how to write clearly both that "public purpose can continue" and that "specific offices and institutional forms may also need to end", so that the empty pole position does not re-sacralise one kind of structure;
- whether this thought and Interface's "local presence" are only character analogies, or will mutually explain one another in dialogue;
- if concrete astronomical facts are cited, they need to be rechecked against reliable sources before entering the main text.
- if the historical relations among Taiyi, Qi-region fangshi, Confucian scholars, Jixia, or Huang-Lao are written directly, distinguish facts with documentary support, scholarly disputed generalisations, and the protagonist's own creative rewriting; do not treat this file's compressed account as a ready-made historiographical conclusion.
