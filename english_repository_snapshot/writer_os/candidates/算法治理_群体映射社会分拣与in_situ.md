# Algorithmic Governance: Group Mapping, Social Sorting, and `in situ`

## Status

This is a governance-background candidate that is not currently being developed. It preserves a set of algorithmic-governance problematics for creative use; it does not confirm that any specific worldline has already adopted these institutions, and it does not serve as a research review of real scholars or machine-learning methods.

## From "Who Is Watching Me" to "Where Does the System Place Me"

The consequences of surveillance do not come only from precise understanding of an individual's secrets, desires, or future behaviour. A system may not care who the character fully is. It may only map him, through data associations, onto a certain group, risk tier, or opportunity category, and then give him differential treatment on that basis.

This shifts the problem beyond privacy leakage towards:

- which features, proxy variables, and historical labels gather people together;
- who names group categories, and for what institutional aims they are used;
- how classification determines loans, insurance, borders, education, employment, welfare, policing, or information visibility;
- whether a person who has not yet committed a prohibited act bears costs in advance because he "resembles a certain kind of person";
- how a contestable distance can be maintained among statistical judgement, individual fact, and responsibility for action.

"You are processed not because the system has proved what you did, but because the shape of your data resembles someone" can become a very direct literary conflict. The work still needs to specify the concrete organisation, threshold, and consequence involved in the decision, rather than writing the abstract "algorithm" as an omnipotent enemy.

## Plural `algorithms` Placed `in situ`

Real governance systems are usually not a single `the Algorithm` floating outside society, but an assemblage made of multiple technical and organisational links, such as:

- data collection, cleaning, missing-value handling, and identity matching;
- explicit records, proxy variables, and feature construction;
- clustering, classification, regression, ranking, or anomaly-detection models;
- thresholds, business rules, human review, appeal interfaces, and execution personnel;
- procurement relations, legal permissions, budget pressures, performance metrics, and model maintenance;
- new data and feedback loops produced after model outputs enter reality.

Here, `in situ` requires these links to be placed back into the institutional sites where they occur: who designs them, who buys them, who maintains them, what is optimised, in what legal environment they run, and who is able to change them. The plural form is not a stylistic preference; it prevents dispersed responsibility and concrete choices from being mystified into a subjectless technological god.

## Four Kinds of Power Can Be Held by Different Subjects

Once the complete data lifecycle is unfolded, at least four related forms of power can be distinguished, without having to be concentrated in the same institution:

- **Readability power**: deciding what can be recorded, which categories exist, and which records are treated as the same object;
- **inferential power**: inferring attributes, tendencies, and futures from material the character has not actively expressed, and deciding how much uncertainty is enough to act;
- **infrastructure power**: controlling cloud, storage, identity, standards, interfaces, migration, and retirement conditions;
- **modulation power**: altering the character's actual environment through ranking, eligibility, price, defaults, resource allocation, and friction.

One organisation may design categories, another provide cloud and identity, a third train the model, and a fourth face the public and execute consequences. Responsibility is chopped up through this division of labour, and each layer may claim that it only provided a neutral tool. `in situ` therefore requires more than adding an institution's name to an algorithm; it requires reconnecting the local decisions that together constitute one real act of disposition.

## Classification Is Not the Endpoint; Choice Topology Is the Consequence

Model outputs acquire real action power only after they are connected to thresholds, interfaces, human processes, eligibility rules, and resource allocation. A probability can remain uncertain, yet an institution can turn it into refusal at a certain threshold; a group similarity can be merely descriptive, yet ranking and access can turn it into different worlds faced by different people.

The so-called `human-in-the-loop` does not automatically restore human judgement. If staff have no time to inspect original evidence, no permission to overturn the result, or additional performance costs for overturning the model, the human may only sign for the automated result. The question to ask is not whether there is a "human" in the flowchart, but whether that person has the evidence, time, authority, and institutional position to bear the consequences of dissent.

The risks of classification also cannot be summarised only by accuracy. Even if a system accurately predicts some behaviour, it is still necessary to distinguish whether the category is legitimate, whether differential treatment is legitimate, whether the character has workable appeal and exit conditions, and whether the model is manufacturing, through treatment, behaviour that will later be treated as category evidence. For related subject responses, see [Social Engineering: Performative Sincerity and Reverse Profiling](社会工程_表演性真诚与反向画像.md).

## Association, Classification, and Causal Understanding Need to Be Separated

- A system can perform well on prediction or sorting tasks while not knowing why the correlated pattern appears, and not knowing how a character will change after some factor is intervened upon.
- Supervised learning can learn classification boundaries directly from labelled material, without first "discovering groups" through unsupervised clustering; clustering also does not automatically produce natural groups that are socially meaningful or unbiased.
- `KNN`, random forests, gradient-boosted trees, and clustering algorithms can all participate in association and classification in some systems, but no algorithm is naturally identical to "social sorting". The same method may have completely different effects in different data, aims, thresholds, and institutions.
- Classification error is not the only risk. Even if a model is statistically accurate, the category itself, the permitted features, the legitimacy of differential treatment, individuals' appeals within the group, and the feedback loop may still be problematic.

## Relation to the Novel's Existing Data Predicament

AI civilisation's lack of sufficient human causal-level behavioural data does not mean early algorithmic governance lacked scale or practical effectiveness. A civilisation could possess extremely mature group mapping, associative prediction, and behaviour-management systems, and still be unable to answer how a concrete person updates themselves in unexperienced historical conditions, unfamiliar relationships, or a real question.

This distinction also prevents the work from mistakenly writing "more surveillance" as "deeper understanding". For related global boundaries, see [Global Foundation: Real Enumeration and Metatime](../creative_work/story_state/全局底座_真实枚举与元时间.md); for synthetic data and process materials, see [AI Civilisation Data Predicament: Synthetic Data and Real Experience](AI文明数据困境_合成数据与真实经验.md).

## Literary Entry Points

- The same character is placed by the same data system into different categories in different worldlines, with the difference coming from institutional aims or thresholds rather than the character's essence;
- a character can prove an individual fact, but cannot change the system's treatment of his group risk;
- an organisation claims that "the model has not accused anyone", yet real execution has already restricted action and opportunity;
- Interface can read the character's rich state, yet discovers that institutions inside the worldline only need a crude category to produce irreversible consequences;
- the protagonist's abductive reasoning asks about the mechanisms behind micro-level signs, while the governance system only needs correlation in order to act, forming a conflict of method and responsibility.

These are only entry points; they do not confirm any particular algorithm, victim identity, governance field, or worldline affiliation.

## Source Boundaries

Accurate summaries of Shoshana Zuboff, David Lyon, Antoinette Rouvroy, and related terminology have not yet been checked directly against the original works. If these names or theories enter the work, dissertation, or public explanation, research must return to reliable sources.

The four institutional powers, dispersed responsibility, choice topology, and qualifications around `human-in-the-loop` belong to cross-literature mechanism reconstruction and the project's literary extrapolation. They are not a complete empirical review of current algorithmic-governance institutions, and they do not make this candidate a technical, legal, or policy guide.
