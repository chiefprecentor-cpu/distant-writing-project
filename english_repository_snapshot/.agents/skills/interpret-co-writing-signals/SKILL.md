# Interpret Co-writing Signals

Read a co-writing prompt at the level of meaningful sentences, clauses, quotations, and comments on quotations before deciding what the author wants from the exchange.

For live mixed feedback or trace analysis, read [signal-reading.md](references/signal-reading.md). When the task concerns the current distant-writing project, also read [aesthetic-profile.md](references/aesthetic-profile.md); treat it as incomplete and revisable.

## Work at three levels

1. Identify the function of each meaningful unit without mechanically splitting at punctuation. A unit may ask for explanation, report confusion, acknowledge an effect, open alternatives, locate a felt friction, try a counterproposal, reject, confirm, authorise, or express uncertainty about the target itself. One unit may do more than one thing.
2. Reconstruct how the units relate inside the prompt. Preserve structures such as “this works for one reason, but may cost too much elsewhere” or “the quoted opinion is useful only in this respect.”
3. Only then consider whether later confirmation, repetition, or an explicit request supports a longer-term update. A question, lack of understanding, or moment of low confidence is not by itself an aesthetic preference.

Give the author a coherent response rather than a visible taxonomy unless classification is itself the requested task. When the signal remains ambiguous, a useful low-cost response often explains the current choice, offers one genuinely different alternative, and states what preference would reverse the recommendation.

In trace work, preserve chronology and distinguish the author's words from quoted third-party or AI text. Read the target prompt in full. With structured traces, retrieve that exact record first, then expand one relevant user or assistant message at a time only when status, reference, or supersession cannot be resolved locally. Do not load a broad sequence window or tool results merely because they are adjacent. Mark supersession rather than accumulating incompatible rules.

If an aesthetic-profile update seems warranted, propose the smallest change and its scope. Do not silently turn a scene-specific observation into a project-wide commitment or write it into story state without authorisation.
