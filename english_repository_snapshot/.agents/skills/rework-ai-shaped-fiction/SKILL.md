# Rework AI-Shaped Fiction

Treat “AI fingerprint” as a reader's felt effect in the text, not proof of who or what wrote it. Look for an accumulation of default regularity, explanatory closure, and pre-solved design across the scales that actually matter in the supplied passage.

For the current distant-writing project, read the shared [aesthetic profile](../interpret-co-writing-signals/references/aesthetic-profile.md). It is an evolving calibration, not a list of prohibited forms.

## Establish the comparison

Read the full scope the author asks you to review. If the request concerns a scene or draft, locate patterns across that scope rather than polishing only the quoted examples. A local excerpt is sufficient for a local question, but cannot support a claim that all related instances have been removed.

Identify what already gives the passage specificity or force before diagnosing its regularity. If the author names a stronger passage, compare how that passage distributes perception, information, speech, action, silence, and omission. Do not imitate its sentence length or visible mannerisms.

Do not treat every line that survives in a stronger or later draft as positively confirmed. Where the author's response or revision history remains tentative, keep that line open for comparison rather than turning it into a style rule.

In the current project, the iteratively revised Opening is a useful provisional calibration when a comparison would materially help. Select the corresponding kind of passage rather than loading it automatically: early encounters and the walk for quiet external action; the friend conversation for long, character-owned reasoning; Mallowbell for multi-person comedy and private projection; Hyde Park for reflective deliberation. Difference from the Opening is not itself a defect, and individual lines within it may remain revisable.

## Find the scale producing the effect

Follow the scales that explain the felt problem; do not mechanically report every category.

- **Sentence and cadence:** Notice clusters of tricolons, parallel declaratives, balanced negations, identical turn lengths, abstract noun sequences, and polished concluding maxims. Their presence is not the problem by itself. Ask whether nearby recurrences keep performing the same rhetorical completion or have distinct local work.
- **Information movement:** Check whether development notes, taxonomies, causal chains, or legal and technical distinctions have entered narration or dialogue intact. Correct information can still arrive too completely, in the wrong voice, or before the scene has made it matter.
- **Character knowledge:** Trace what a character perceives, remembers, infers, doubts, retracts, and learns. A character may be brilliant or unusually intuitive without possessing the outline. Flag conclusions, terminology, or emotional recognition for which the text supplies no usable path.
- **Conversation and action:** Ask whether each speaker pursues a situated purpose or whether one asks reader-facing questions while another delivers the prepared account. Generic gestures can become punctuation for an argument without changing the encounter. Silence, non-answer, misunderstanding, or unequal knowledge may be more truthful, but none is mandatory.
- **Scene architecture:** Look past line polish for an inherited briefing, recruitment, interrogation, confession, mentor lesson, or other familiar scaffold that may be assigning roles, props, and revelations by default. A recognisable form can be intentional; change it only when it displaces the story's more particular relationship or causality.

## Rework the cause, not the tell

Start at the highest-leverage scale responsible for the effect.

- Redistribute information before replacing words. Let a document, object, bodily response, spatial relation, interruption, error, delayed consequence, or later echo carry what need not be stated now. Some design facts may remain clues or never become explicit story text.
- Restore the perceptual and epistemic path to an important inference. Let characters form partial or wrong models, ask from their actual knowledge, and revise only when something reaches them.
- Give the scene back to its participants. Their speech may evade, protect, test, impress, irritate, or seek company; it need not answer the reader's next question efficiently.
- If a generic dramatic scaffold controls the scene, reconsider the characters' prior relation to the problem, who owns the material, what each person brings, and what mundane concern competes with the apparent plot function.
- Edit cadence after information ownership and scene logic are sound. Vary a recurring form where its sameness is accidental; keep it where repetition, precision, awkwardness, rhetoric, or compression belongs to the character or scene.

Do not manufacture randomness, fragments, slang, vagueness, or casualness merely to appear less generated. Do not shorten a discursive character into generic contemporary dialogue. Do not preserve every planning fact by finding it a subtler synonym.

Searches, counts, or detector scores may help locate clusters, but they do not decide whether a passage works and should not become the revision target.

## Return a usable judgement

For a review, explain where the AI-shaped feeling comes from, at what scale it operates, what existing effect should survive, and which intervention would change the most. Distinguish a confident diagnosis from an aesthetic possibility.

For an authorised revision, make the focused structural or line changes the diagnosis warrants, then reread the unmarked passage for intelligibility, character continuity, and new monotony. Report any information deferred or removed and any semantic or relational consequence that changed. Do not claim the whole draft has been cleared when only an excerpt was examined.

Review does not by itself authorise edits. Preserve purposeful strangeness and unresolved choices rather than silently normalising them.
