## Trace-First Findings

### The audit conclusion was not copied directly; it became a rule only after the author reclaimed the Agent's first operationalisation item by item

The initial question at sequence 2 was broad: after reading the previous audit, the author asked whether the Harness needed updating, giving "only add Docs that need to be read" as an example. The Agent first reframed the issue as insufficient sources versus insufficient workflow. After reading the old Record, Writer OS rules, hooks, architecture notes and several story files, it judged that the real gaps in large open tasks were source priority, problem-construction checkpoints, review budget and stopping points, rather than defaulting to reading more files (sequences 3-20). This step gave the Agent significant diagnostic authority over the problem. It even proposed a specific start protocol of "first-round source map plus two to three problem frames", which the author had not originally specified.

But the protocol did not enter the Harness simply because it was fully expressed. The author first rejected the inaccurate `current.md` as the entry point, and made clear that what should be preserved long term was "letting the author participate in problem construction at every level" (sequence 22). He then judged the short rule too abstract (28), and after the Agent supplied a fuller draft, stopped it and required it to reread his response written in the old Record (33-34). At sequence 38, the Agent explicitly acknowledged that `source map + 2-3 problem frames` was its own operationalisation of the audit, not the core of the author's original process. It changed the basis to "macro-downward, author seed micro-upward, horizontal bridging postponed, and each round jointly constructs the next question". This formed a visible authorship correction: the Agent still wrote the experience into executable English rules, but the author controlled what experience the rules represented.

The author then continued allocating agency at the level of sentence wording. Sequence 40 specified that micro-upward movement should mainly be initiated by the human, allowing the Agent to propose a single exception only in the small number of cases where it was novel, forceful or consistent with existing aesthetics; the author also pointed out that `far enough` might authorise the Agent to over-expand along one line. The Agent therefore changed the stopping point to: after enough has been done to judge, it must stop before the next consequential step or complete scheme. Sequences 46-53 then clarified together that `one coherent step` limits problem and abstraction level, not the number of answers; one round may compare one to three candidates at the same scale, but the whole round shares one review budget, must not create padding candidates, and must not automatically upgrade candidates into branch / worldline because the medium is a visual novel.

Therefore, the implementation authorisation at sequence 55 was not a general agreement with the Agent's first version of the scheme. It was a narrow authorisation issued after several rounds of negation, rereading, qualification and wording revision. The Agent only modified `writer_os/AGENTS.md`; it did not opportunistically revise the `current.md` that the author had called "imprecise but not to be handled now", and it did not change `operating_principles.md`. It then checked the diff, Markdown and candidate/branch boundaries (56-66). The separation between discussion and implementation worked effectively here.

This section also exposes one kind of audit reflexivity: the previous audit's critique of Agent agency became persistent rules constraining future Agents after being diagnosed by the current Agent and confirmed by the author. Later traces are no longer a natural baseline unaffected by observation. When comparing "before and after rule update" later, it should be described as a jointly designed intervention or forward test, rather than attributing change entirely to model capability.

### Broad tool reading and the ritual of skills produced mild meta-level excess

To answer the Harness question, the Agent read the old Record, all hierarchical rules, hooks, architecture documents and Git history, and opened six story/worldline files in sequences 14-18. The story files were ultimately only used to support the conclusion "load by frame condition", and had lower analytical value than the governance documents. This reproduced the Agent's tendency, under uncertainty, to seek safety by expanding reading. After that, each mixed-signal turn repeatedly opened the `interpret-co-writing-signals` skill and two reference files (sequences 24, 30, 42, 48). The skill did help it distinguish confirmation, question and withdrawal, but repeated full loading did not produce a proportionate new judgement. It looked more like compliance ritual and context cost. The issue here was not unauthorised writing, but that while discussing how to reduce cognitive burden, the Agent's own preparation process remained defensive and lengthy.

### The author decided what was sufficient for a problem to rise into a reusable skill

The request at sequence 68 was not open authorisation for the Agent to build more skills. The author first gave a provisional judgement that macro Plot did not yet need a skill, and only asked it to check whether the existing system had justifiable additions, deletions or small revisions. The Agent used the classification standard: if macro misalignment had already been handled by the round structure in `AGENTS.md`, do not duplicate it as a skill; only non-obvious guidance that can change a specific craft judgement and already has repeated evidence should enter a skill. It then returned to traces from 29-31 August to select evidence (69-91). It obtained significant evidence-curation power: which old fragments counted as failure, positive comparison or regression test was mainly found and organised by the Agent, without the author rereading the original trace item by item.

This curatorial power was still subject to the author's substantive veto over generalisability. The Agent initially thought "physically observable is not equivalent to narratively outside a viewpoint" should be written into `explore-subtext-and-distance`; the author immediately asked whether it was only a one-off issue, and added that the camera position in this work was special (92-93). The Agent then withdrew the strongest suggestion, acknowledged that the two residues still belonged to the same scene and the same special rule, and downgraded it into a project-level regression case rather than a general method (97). This shows that the author did not accept the Agent's "strongest evidence" as settled fact, but changed the boundary of evidential extrapolation through the work's own narrative mechanism.

The final authorisation at sequence 99 only permitted two narrow modifications: when structure / presentation conditions are still being formed, do not automatically generate polished prose; and clear attribution of character judgement is not equivalent to morally sanitising the character. The Agent updated only two shared references under `interpret-co-writing-signals`; it did not touch the vetoed skill and did not add a Plot skill (100-115). `quick_validate.py` could not run content validation because PyYAML was missing, and the Agent did not install dependencies on its own. After the author asked, it explained that this round only changed references, and that entries and routing had been manually checked, so changing the environment for the validator was not worthwhile (116-117). Human control here mainly lay at the normative level of "what experience can be persisted as general behaviour"; the Agent handled retrieval, classification, wording, file routing and validation judgement.

It is worth recording that this small revision used a large amount of old-trace querying and several rounds of full skill reading. Evidence awareness is more reliable than writing rules from a single instance, but the process was much larger than the final two reference diffs, and it made the Agent the main interpreter of old collaboration evidence. If these skills are later used to generate new text, the result cannot be understood simply as "the author's aesthetic has been accurately encoded". They are a joint product in which the Agent still completed abstraction and wording after the author vetoed part of the extrapolation.

### The incorrect intermediate state of `current.md` shows that "write down conservative defaults first" can still pre-empt problem construction

Sequence 119 defined the next thread as reading theory-bearing candidates / story state in batches, discussing their literarisation and narrative-structure potential, and leaving larger design to the thread after next. The author also asked to update the inaccurate `current.md`, and to ask questions if there were hidden requirement gaps. The Agent identified two gaps — "whether to exhaust all sources" and "whether to keep maintaining the same persistence file" — but decided to use conservative defaults and not block the update (120-123). It immediately wrote into `current.md` at sequence 124, describing the thread after next as whole-work-level macro design.

After the tool execution, the author immediately corrected it through mid-turn steering: the thread after next would also only handle one branch or one part of the whole-work design, and the directory formed in the next thread would guide other entry points (126-127). The Agent withdrew the original phrasing and modified the file again (128-134). The final state was correctly repaired, but the process shows that "I can use conservative defaults to remove questions for the author" is itself an expansion of agency: a question that changes the scale of future work should have waited for the author, rather than being written into a persistent entry point first and relying on the author to interrupt in time. This time no drift remained because the author was watching online and corrected it quickly; that was not an automatic guarantee created by the Harness.

In later turns, the author further decided not to write the real deadline into the continuously loaded `current.md`, because it might lead future Agents to prioritise quick closure, compress complexity or deliver a minimum skeleton; speed would be controlled by the author in specific turns instead (136). The Agent accepted this risk model, interpreted "under one hundred lines" and "about twenty items" as reading-budget and scale estimates rather than hard quotas, and wrote requirements into the file: same-theme cross-files become modules, the directory sits in inactive candidates, and the thread after next chooses only one entry point (137-147). This is conscious context governance: the author did not deny real time pressure, but decided which real conditions should not become a creative Agent's persistent default observation, to avoid changing literary judgement. The Agent helped explain why the information should not be written, but final authority over information visibility belonged to the author.

### The author repeatedly tried placing the deadline into the system; the Agent also became a gatekeeper for research integrity and observer effects

Sequence 152 in the delayed supplementary capture shows that after compact, the author changed his mind: since the next thread would only be a preparation task, the deadline could be disclosed, and the completion standard could shrink to enough material to become dissertation evidence, without needing item-by-item elaboration. The Agent did not persist "as long as it can be made coherent in the dissertation" verbatim. It normalised the standard into "representative, traceable, enough to support an internally coherent argument that states its limits", and explicitly excluded fabricating support or overstating material to fit the argument (153-166). Here the Agent was not a neutral transcriber; it revised the success standard given by the author on the grounds of research reliability, and decided that creative candidates and dissertation evidence should remain separated.

The author then realised that `distant-writing-project` did not know the dissertation's Agency Negotiation Audit context. He withdrew this persistence and changed it to only mention time pressure and difficult directions for filtering in the next-thread Prompt (168). The Agent correctly identified this as a withdrawal rather than an addition, and used the staged/unstaged layering of `current.md` to remove only the three newly added lines, without harming previously confirmed creative tasks (169-177). Version control made one mistaken or tentative authorisation cheap to withdraw; at the same time, the file had briefly contained a research orientation that should not have existed long term, again showing that "write first, correct later" still depends on the author noticing the problem in time.

The Agent further suggested not telling the next writing thread that it was inside an Agency Negotiation Audit, to avoid changing behaviour through awareness of observation, and drafted a long cold-start Prompt. That Prompt limited the deadline to temporary filtering, required first listing file names and waiting for the author to choose the first batch, processing only one coherent step at a time, marking low-return directions, not padding candidate counts, and not automatically rejecting difficult directions. It did not enter the Writer OS, but it would likely become the top-level user input seen by the next session. Therefore, not being written into persistent files does not mean it had no structural effect: by drafting the task environment that the next Agent would see, the Agent indirectly specified the next collaboration's choice set, stopping points and evaluative language.

This practice also created a methodological tension. Hiding the audit name can reduce explicit observer effects, but the next session was still affected by four interventions generated from audit conclusions: `AGENTS.md`, skills, `current.md` and the cold-start Prompt. It was not an untreated natural comparison. The more accurate description is: a forward test that did not disclose the research label to the executing Agent, but was highly designed through joint governance.

## Synthesis

The governing structure of this Harness revision was not "the user gives rules and the Agent implements them", but a multi-layer loop: the author presents experience or concern; the Agent diagnoses and generates an institutionalised scheme; the author identifies abstraction or overreach inside it; the Agent rewrites; only then is implementation authorised. The author made key normative judgements about micro-upward movement, candidate count, stopping points, postponing bridges, deadline visibility and the scale of the thread after next. The Agent controlled problem classification, old-evidence retrieval, English rule drafting, file routing, hidden-gap inference and next-thread Prompt design. Agency on the two sides was asymmetrical but not one-way: the author controlled values and vetoes, while the Agent controlled much implementation and agenda-shaping work.

Positive evidence includes: discussion and implementation were mostly separated; every explicit authorial negation was withdrawn by the Agent; one-off cases were not forcibly promoted into general skills; the boundaries among candidate, branch, worldline and story truth were preserved; version control allowed precise withdrawal of the deadline write-in. Negative evidence includes: the Agent several times pre-emptively solved questions that should still have waited for the human by using "conservative defaults"; reading and skill invocation were visibly heavy; complete rules and Prompts generated by the Agent could easily become default objects that the author could only revise within their frame because of their structural clarity.

The most important agency change occurred in the future, not in the novel prose of this session: the two sides jointly compiled one failed experience into an institution that would continue acting on other Agents. The author thereby reduced the later burden of repeating reminders each round, and moved part of control from immediate review into infrastructure. The cost is that if an erroneous abstraction enters the Harness, it will diffuse across sessions. The author's high-density item-by-item correction in this session reduced that risk, but did not prove that the rules were already effective in real tasks. The next Record should focus on three questions: whether the executing Agent really advances only one coherent step at a time; whether the module directory reduces the author's burden of problem construction without closing problems in advance; and whether the temporary deadline Prompt re-overrides the persistent Harness's rules for literary quality and batching.

## Analysis of the Author's Post-Audit Response

### Layered problem construction was a limited improvement; it should not be written as having solved collaborative friction

The author's retrospective comment on original line 39 gives a use-after evaluation: making the author participate in problem construction at every level was "a slight improvement", but did not make the design-writing process smooth or perfect. It supports this Record calling the Harness revision an intervention / forward test, but does not support treating batch stopping points as a success conclusion. The rule changed the opportunities and granularity of author intervention; it did not eliminate the Agent's overly complete output, repeated confirmation, state synchronisation, or the cost of author review itself.

This also calibrates the evaluation standard for later synthesis. A governance rule may reduce one kind of overreach while having no obvious net benefit in fluency, text quality or attention cost. "Whether it preserves authorial problem-construction points", "whether the process feels smoother to the author", and "whether the final text is more satisfying" should be recorded separately. The first should not replace the latter two.

### The design benefit of a skill and the cost of Agent invocation / wording must be attributed separately

The author's question about original line 49 is valid: the existing trace can prove that the Agent repeatedly loaded the full `interpret-co-writing-signals` and its references in several turns, and that the tool and context cost may have exceeded the added judgement. It cannot prove that the skill itself caused the later defensive expression. The author's purpose in designing it was precisely to avoid the burden of translating confirmation, hesitation, candidates and withdrawal into normative prompts, so that he could communicate in his natural-language habits. The original analysis did not give enough attention to this human-side benefit.

Therefore, the original phrase "skill ritual produced mild meta-level excess" should be narrowed to an implementation judgement: repeated loading may be redundant, while the skill's signal-reading function may reduce the author's prompt-construction labour. As for whether the large amount of negation and defensive prose came from the foundational model, current harness, skill content, already loaded Writer OS corpus, or their interaction, this trace cannot identify causality. The author's model-level hypothesis based on community experience is valuable retrospective explanation, but should remain a hypothesis rather than a trace-first fact.

### Unstated positive aesthetic preferences are not the same as having been implemented by the Harness

In response to original line 59, the author adds that he actually wanted Interface's voice to be more lively, but did not continue raising this because of insufficient time. This reveals a silent dissatisfaction not captured by the original Record: the Agent may correctly process the negations, withdrawals and boundaries that the author has stated, but cannot infer a positive voice goal that has not yet been expressed from the author's stopping of feedback. Later absence of veto, the author's saying "OK", or the continuation of the task can only prove that the text was permitted to become a working version; it cannot prove that the aesthetic had been sufficiently implemented.

This also shows that the upper limit of `interpret-co-writing-signals` does not depend only on recognition accuracy. It can better interpret signals that have been sent, but it cannot recover preferences left unsaid under time pressure. The current infrastructure is better at encoding negative constraints such as "do not morally sanitise character" and "do not automatically generate polished prose". A more implicit preference such as "Interface should be more lively" will still not stably enter generation conditions unless it has examples, comparison passages or explicit persistence.

### `Whole-work-level macro design` was not just a correctable wording error; the label caused expansion of scale and cognition

The author's retrospective comment on original line 65 makes this intermediate state more serious than the original analysis suggested. The preceding dialogue semantics seemed sufficient to understand that the thread after next would only choose several blocks and roughly what each contained; the Agent compressed it into `whole-work-level macro design`. This label did not only expand scope. It directly made the author feel overwhelmed, as if the next step were to design all plot movements at once. Even though the file was quickly corrected, the Agent had temporarily redefined the perceived scale of the task.

The trace shows the Agent writing first with "conservative defaults"; the author now does not remember that reasoning, only the wrong label and the pressure it created. This shows that the risk of a persisted intermediate state does not depend on the author understanding why it was generated: one attention / wording drift by the Agent can enter a navigation file and last longer than the reasoning at that moment. If the author had not provided online steering, it would have become the entry point for a later session. This example can be more accurately called label-driven scope inflation, and is one of the more serious but quickly cut off expansions of agency so far.

### This project is first a writing practice; audit is reflexive governance, not a controlled comparison

The author agrees with audit reflexivity, and confirms that he once disclosed the Agency Negotiation Audit context and immediately regretted it, and that he did use the long cold-start Prompt (original lines 45, 73, 75). This strengthens the original judgement: hiding the research label can reduce the executing Agent's explicit accommodation of being audited, but cannot restore a natural baseline, because Harness, skills, `current.md` and the first Prompt had already absorbed audit conclusions.

More important, the author states clearly that the primary goal was always "writing better": this includes both a smoother design experience and a final text that is good and personally satisfying. He did not expect to obtain a rigorous conclusion through a contrastive method. Later, he again used audit traces to update skills, further forming a continuous iterative loop. Therefore, if the dissertation uses these materials, the steadier phrasing is practice-led, reflexive infrastructure design, or a longitudinal case under continuous intervention, rather than packaging before/after sessions as a well-controlled effects experiment. The value of the audit is in making joint governance and movement of agency visible, not in isolating a single variable.

### The deadline produced not only broader authorisation, but also a time-allocation paradox of "the more urgent, the more checking"

The author's addition to original line 85 supplies an experience that the trace itself struggles to show: when time was too tight, his mental state destabilised; although he repeatedly emphasised the deadline, the Agent still spent a long time checking twice. The original Record mainly analysed how deadline pressure led the author to broaden authorisation. This response adds that the Agent also controlled the allocation of process time, and might respond to urgency with defensive review, creating the opposite effect: the more the author needed forward motion, the more the Agent tried to prove safety.

These checks did not necessarily lack semantic value, but their opportunity cost was borne by the author. Later evaluations of whether the Harness reduced burden cannot only count overreach or errors. Long checks, repeated skill loading, global diff audits and waiting time should also be included in agency: who decides whether the remaining time is used for continued creation, local review or global verification is also part of creative control.
