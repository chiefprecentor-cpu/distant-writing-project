## The Corner

He was not running. He was walking fast enough that the distinction was mostly private, a slice of peanut-butter toast held between his teeth.

Someone came around the blind corner at the same moment he did. He moved left. She moved the same way. They both corrected and collided anyway.

The toast slipped from his teeth. He caught it by the crust.

“Sorry.”

“I’m fine.” Her eyes went to the toast in his hand. “I thought this only happened in shoujo manga.”

He looked at the toast.

“It has peanut butter,” he said.

She looked back at him. “I don’t think that changes the genre.”

She stepped past him and continued along the street.

He waited a few seconds before resuming the route he had already intended to take.

At the next turning, she went left. So did he.

At the entrance to a building, she stopped to check something on her phone. Several paces behind her, he checked the address on his. She noticed him. Neither spoke.

She went inside.

At the end of the corridor, an A4 notice beside a half-open door marked the room for the talk. She read it and entered. He checked it after her, although he already knew what it said.

Only a few of the chairs were occupied. She chose a seat in the front half of the room. He sat several rows behind her and across the aisle.

## The Talk

The lights dimmed. The speaker began.

On the screen, a page of text gave way to a title.

> PATCHWORK GIRL  
> SHELLEY JACKSON / AFTER MARY SHELLEY

*Frankenstein*. Still his favourite British science-fiction novel. The prose could be absurdly mannered. That was part of why he liked it.

The screen filled and cleared several times. A few people typed. Someone near the door coughed.

The next title he noticed was:

> CALVINO  
> THE BURNING OF THE ABOMINABLE HOUSE

*Calvino. Who?*

More slides passed.

> STORY SIFTING  
> SELECT THE UNEXPECTED

This time his attention left the screen.

The woman from the corner sat several rows ahead, her face turned towards the speaker.

The *bishoujo* in his head had never looked like her. Yet by his own private reading of each of the word’s three characters, she was one. Annoyingly, he could not argue himself out of it.

When he looked back, the slide had changed.

The talk continued. When the lights came up, the sparse audience gathered itself and moved towards the door. She left with the first few people.

By the time he reached the corridor, she was gone.

## Three Bad Mornings

For three weeks running, the one morning he took the Tube was the morning something went wrong.

An announcement broke apart in the noise above the platform. People looked up, then at one another, and began to move before he had caught more than an apology.

Across the crowd, he saw the woman from the corner. Her head turned. Before he could tell whether she had seen him, people crossed between them. When the gap opened, she was gone.

*Cut.*

Another announcement. Another crowd changing its mind.

This time she saw him first.

“You again.”

He opened his mouth. The crowd changed direction once more and took her one way and him another.

*Cut.*

By the third week, he had begun moving before he made out the words. Part of the line was suspended. Everyone was being sent up to the street and towards replacement buses that had not yet arrived.

He found her in the crowd. She saw him too.

They came out of the station a few steps apart. On the pavement, the crowd spread towards the replacement queue. When it thinned, they were still walking in the same direction.

## The Walk

The queue was already folding back along the pavement.

“I’m going to walk to the Strand,” he said.

“All the way?”

“I usually do.”

She looked back at the queue. “I’m going there too. I think I’ll walk.”

They left the queue behind.

*The station-side background gives way to a road: buses and cars form a steady lateral blur, while the pavement and the space around the characters remain clear.*

“I never got your name,” he said.

“Neither did I.”

“He Xuyuan.”

“What should I call you?”

“Xuyuan.”

“Xuyuan,” she repeated carefully. “I’m Lulu.”

“Lulu,” he repeated.

Lulu looked towards the next crossing. The signal changed against them before they reached it.

“Do you have a class?” he asked as they waited.

“A lecture. You?”

“A seminar.”

When they started again, she glanced at him.

“You walk this every day?”

“Most days. It’s cheaper.”

“Why did you take the Tube today?”

“I overslept.”

“All three times?”

He looked at her.

“Yes.”

They walked for a while before he spoke again.

“What did you think of the talk?”

“I liked it. I was actually looking for the room next door.”

“But you stayed.”

“It was interesting.” She looked at him. “What did you think?”

“I liked it.”

“Was it for your course?”

“Not officially.”

“What do you study?”

“Digital culture. English literature before that.”

“That’s quite a change.”

“It didn’t feel like one.”

“What about you?”

“War Studies.”

At the next crossing, they stopped with everyone else. Traffic passed. When the road cleared, they crossed and walked most of the next block without speaking.

At the edge of the campus, Lulu slowed.

“My lecture’s this way.”

“Right.”

“See you.”

“See you.”

She turned towards one entrance. He continued towards another.

## The Following Week

The following week, nothing went wrong.

No announcement rearranged the platform. The train arrived, the doors opened, and nobody was sent back towards the stairs.

He saw Lulu farther down the carriage. She saw him and came over.

“You overslept again.”

“Yes.”

The doors closed without incident.

“Nothing’s gone wrong,” he said.

“I know.”
