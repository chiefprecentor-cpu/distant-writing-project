## The Corner

He was not running. He was walking fast enough that the distinction was mostly private, a slice of peanut-butter toast held between his teeth.

Someone came around the blind corner at the same moment he did. He moved left. She moved the same way. They both corrected and collided anyway.

The toast slipped from his teeth. He caught it by the crust.

**He:** Sorry.

**Woman:** I’m fine.

Her eyes went to the toast in his hand.

**Woman:** I thought this only happened in shoujo manga.

He looked at the toast.

**He:** It has peanut butter.

She looked back at him.

**Woman:** I don’t think that changes the genre.

She stepped past him and continued along the street.

He waited a few seconds before resuming the route he had already intended to take.

At the next turning, she went left. So did he.

At the entrance to a building, she stopped to check something on her phone. Several paces behind her, he checked the address on his. She noticed him. Neither spoke.

She went inside.

At the end of the corridor, an A4 notice beside a half-open door marked the room for the talk. She read it and entered. He checked it after her, although he already knew what it said.

Only a few of the chairs were occupied. She chose a seat in the front half of the room. He sat several rows behind her and across the aisle.

## The Talk

The lights dimmed. The speaker began.

On the screen, a page of text gave way to a title.

*Visual: Slide—“PATCHWORK GIRL / SHELLEY JACKSON / AFTER MARY SHELLEY”.*

*Frankenstein*. Still his favourite British science-fiction novel. The prose could be absurdly mannered. That was part of why he liked it.

The screen filled and cleared several times. A few people typed. Someone near the door coughed.

The next title he noticed was:

*Visual: Slide—“CALVINO / THE BURNING OF THE ABOMINABLE HOUSE”.*

*Calvino. Who?*

More slides passed.

*Visual: Slide—“STORY SIFTING / SELECT THE UNEXPECTED”.*

This time his attention left the screen.

The woman from the corner sat several rows ahead, her face turned towards the speaker.

The *bishoujo* in his head had never looked like her. Yet by his own private reading of each of the word’s three characters, she was one. Annoyingly, he could not argue himself out of it.

When he looked back, the slide had changed.

The talk continued. When the lights came up, the sparse audience gathered itself and moved towards the door. She left with the first few people.

By the time he reached the corridor, she was gone.

## Three Bad Mornings

For three weeks running, the one morning he took the Tube was the morning something went wrong.

An announcement broke apart in the noise above the platform. People looked up, then at one another, and began to move before he had caught more than an apology.

Across the crowd, he saw the woman from the corner. Her head turned. Before he could tell whether she had seen him, people crossed between them. When the gap opened, she was gone.

*Cut: The next bad morning.*

Another announcement. Another crowd changing its mind.

This time she saw him first.

**Woman:** You again.

He opened his mouth. The crowd changed direction once more and took her one way and him another.

*Cut: The third bad morning.*

By the third week, he had begun moving before he made out the words. Part of the line was suspended. Everyone was being sent up to the street and towards replacement buses that had not yet arrived.

He found her in the crowd. She saw him too.

They came out of the station a few steps apart. On the pavement, the crowd spread towards the replacement queue. When it thinned, they were still walking in the same direction.

## The Walk

The queue was already folding back along the pavement.

**He:** I’m going to walk to the Strand.

**Woman:** All the way?

**He:** I usually do.

She looked back at the queue.

**Woman:** I’m going there too. I think I’ll walk.

They left the queue behind.

*Visual: The station-side background gives way to a road. Buses and cars form a steady lateral blur, while the pavement and the space around the characters remain clear.*

**He:** I never got your name.

**Woman:** Neither did I.

**He:** He Xuyuan.

**Woman:** What should I call you?

**He:** Xuyuan.

**Woman:** Xuyuan.

She repeated it carefully.

**Woman:** I’m Lulu.

**Xuyuan:** Lulu.

Lulu looked towards the next crossing. The signal changed against them before they reached it.

**Xuyuan:** Do you have a class?

**Lulu:** A lecture. You?

**Xuyuan:** A seminar.

When they started again, she glanced at him.

**Lulu:** You walk this every day?

**Xuyuan:** Most days. It’s cheaper.

**Lulu:** Why did you take the Tube today?

**Xuyuan:** I overslept.

**Lulu:** All three times?

He looked at her.

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

They walked for a while before he spoke again.

**Xuyuan:** What did you think of the talk?

**Lulu:** I liked it. I was actually looking for the room next door.

**Xuyuan:** But you stayed.

**Lulu:** It was interesting.

She looked at him.

**Lulu:** What did you think?

**Xuyuan:** I liked it.

**Lulu:** Was it for your course?

**Xuyuan:** Not officially.

**Lulu:** What do you study?

**Xuyuan:** Digital culture. English literature before that.

**Lulu:** That’s quite a change.

**Xuyuan:** It didn’t feel like one.

**Xuyuan:** What about you?

**Lulu:** War Studies.

At the next crossing, they stopped with everyone else. Traffic passed. When the road cleared, they crossed and walked most of the next block without speaking.

At the edge of the campus, Lulu slowed.

**Lulu:** My lecture’s this way.

**Xuyuan:** Right.

**Lulu:** See you.

**Xuyuan:** See you.

She turned towards one entrance. He continued towards another.

## The Following Week

The following week, nothing went wrong.

No announcement rearranged the platform. The train arrived, the doors opened, and nobody was sent back towards the stairs.

He saw Lulu farther down the carriage. She saw him and came over.

**Lulu:** You overslept again.

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

The doors closed without incident.

**Xuyuan:** Nothing’s gone wrong.

**Lulu:** I know.

## The Pattern

*Scene: Later that day—a café off Kingsway.*

Yuankai, Xuyuan’s friend, was halfway through lunch when Xuyuan began.

**Xuyuan:** Most people’s interests are activities. Films. Games. Sport. Mine is *bishoujo*. Each of its three characters is a separate dimension. How I read all three is entirely up to me.

Yuankai waited.

**Yuankai:** That part is new.

**Xuyuan:** It has always been implicit. But if all three readings are mine, then I should decide what I mean and afterwards decide whether someone fits. This time I noticed the second decision first. At the talk I looked at a woman and realised I had already put her in the category before I could have said why. I had to work backwards from there.

**Yuankai:** It was still your judgement.

**Xuyuan:** I know. The result was not unusual. The order was.

Yuankai took another bite.

**Yuankai:** Who was she?

**Xuyuan:** Her name is Lulu.

**Yuankai:** VTuber?

**Xuyuan:** War Studies.

Yuankai nodded as if this had answered the question.

**Xuyuan:** She was the woman I walked into outside that talk. She had meant to go to the room next door, but found ours interesting and stayed. Then, for three Thursdays in a row, I overslept, took the Tube and found a different part of the system failing each time. The first week I saw her across the crowd and lost sight of her. The second week she saw me first and spoke, but the crowd separated us. The third week the suspended line sent us both to the street, so we walked to campus together.

He paused. Yuankai did not fill the pause.

**Xuyuan:** This morning nothing failed. She was farther down the carriage. She saw me and came over anyway. By then it felt as though fate had been staging the same meeting again and again, each time letting it run a little longer.

**Yuankai:** Fate has an unusually detailed knowledge of service disruptions.

**Xuyuan:** There is no fate involved. Network failures and emergent crowd behaviour can explain how we met on each of the first three mornings separately. They do not bind four Thursdays into a single causal system. But ‘not arranged by fate’ does not mean ‘formless’. The causes changed. She was the refrain. Each time, one less external thing had to happen for the interaction to continue. Being certain it was coincidence would not turn what happened into noise. Probability is not an instruction to be unimpressed.

Yuankai set down his fork.

**Yuankai:** You have rehearsed that.

**Xuyuan:** I thought of it while I was speaking.

**Yuankai:** That is not better. You selected the sequence after it happened. London gives millions of people broken journeys and repeated strangers. Of course your selected set has a refrain. You selected it by the person who recurred.

**Xuyuan:** That means I cannot use the pattern as evidence that someone intended it. It does not follow that I should discard it.

**Yuankai:** Noise is relative to a question. And you are not counting service failures. You are counting appearances of Lulu.

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

**Yuankai:** And this morning Lulu walked down a carriage to speak to you when nothing was wrong. You’re not usually one of those ‘a woman spoke to me, so she must be interested’ people.

**Xuyuan:** I did not say she was interested. I said the structure changed. Until today, the system did the moving. It altered the route, moved the crowd and put us on the pavement. Today the system worked, and she moved.

**Yuankai:** She walked down a carriage.

**Xuyuan:** That is what I said.

**Yuankai:** You needed the whole transport network to say that.

Yuankai returned to his lunch.

**Yuankai:** So she’s cute.

**Xuyuan:** That is an aggressive compression.

**Yuankai:** Is it wrong?

Xuyuan did not answer at once.

**Xuyuan:** It is incomplete.

## Mallowbell Maid Café

*Scene: Saturday afternoon—a side street north of Holborn.*

*Visual: Exterior establishing shot. The painted sign above the door reads “MALLOWBELL MAID CAFÉ”.*

**Xuyuan:** You said it was nearby.

**Yuankai:** It is.

**Xuyuan:** You omitted several relevant properties.

Yuankai looked up at the sign.

**Yuankai:** They were on the sign.

He opened the door.

*Audio: A bell rings above them.*

**Maid:** Welcome home, Masters.

Xuyuan had known what sort of place it was. Knowledge had not prepared him to be welcomed home as a master before he had taken off his coat.

The maid at the door asked whether it was their first visit.

**Yuankai:** For both of us.

She explained the table time and minimum order in the clear voice of someone who had explained them many times. They could photograph themselves and their food, but not the maids without permission. Instant photographs with a maid were a separate item on the menu.

Yuankai answered the practical questions. The maid led them to a four-person table facing the counter, where a line of paper blossoms hung above the till. Xuyuan sat opposite Yuankai.

Yuankai opened the menu and stopped at the desserts.

**Yuankai:** They have the matcha parfait.

**Xuyuan:** You knew that.

**Yuankai:** Yes.

**Xuyuan:** Before we came.

**Yuankai:** Yes.

Yuankai kept one finger on the page and showed no further uncertainty about his order.

*Audio: The bell above the door rings again.*

**Maid:** Welcome home, Princesses.

Xuyuan looked up.

Lulu stood just inside the door with another woman. She looked past the maid and across the crowded room. Her gaze stopped at his table.

**Lulu:** You again.

**Xuyuan:** Hello.

Yuankai looked at Xuyuan once and then at the menu.

The café was nearly full. After speaking to Lulu and her friend, the maid came to their table.

**Maid:** We’re quite busy at the moment. Would both parties be comfortable sharing the large table?

**Yuankai:** Of course.

**Lulu’s friend:** That’s fine.

Before Xuyuan had moved anything, Yuankai brought his glass and menu around to the empty place beside him. Lulu’s friend sat opposite Yuankai. Lulu took the remaining chair, opposite Xuyuan.

**Lulu’s friend:** You two know each other?

**Lulu:** We keep meeting.

**Lulu’s friend:** I’m Anna, by the way.

**Yuankai:** Yuankai.

Lulu looked across the table.

**Lulu:** And this is Xuyuan.

He looked at her.

**Lulu:** You told me.

Anna glanced between the two men.

**Anna:** Are you both at King’s?

**Yuankai:** No, LSE.

**Lulu:** Xuyuan is at King’s. Digital Culture.

He looked at her again.

**Lulu:** You also told me that.

**Anna:** What do you do at LSE?

**Yuankai:** Philosophy of the social sciences.

**Anna:** Master’s?

**Yuankai:** PhD.

**Anna:** That sounds broad.

**Yuankai:** The thesis isn’t.

**Anna:** Lulu and I both study War Studies. We met through the society.

She picked up her menu and looked around the café.

**Anna:** For the record, this was my idea.

**Yuankai:** Mine too.

**Anna:** So they can blame us.

**Yuankai:** That seems fair.

When the maid returned, Anna gave her order first. Lulu chose the curry omelette rice. Xuyuan chose the classic omelette rice. Yuankai ordered the matcha parfait without reopening the menu.

Anna turned to the illustrated instructions beside the food photographs.

**Anna:** It says we have to cast a spell.

**Xuyuan:** The version everyone recognises—the omelette rice and *moe moe kyun*—comes from *K-ON!*.

**Lulu:** No. Maid cafés in Akihabara were already using the line. *K-ON!* made it famous.

Anna looked at her.

**Anna:** I thought this was your first time.

**Lulu:** It is. I still know things.

**Xuyuan:** So you know the spell, not the procedure.

**Lulu:** Do you know the procedure?

**Xuyuan:** I arrived five minutes before you.

Yuankai, whose experience was also five minutes long, left them to it.

The food arrived in stages. The maid set Xuyuan’s omelette rice in front of him with an unopened bottle of ketchup.

**Maid:** What would you like me to draw?

He had not prepared an artistic preference.

**Anna:** Can it really be anything?

**Maid:** Anything simple.

**Xuyuan:** A cat, please.

She drew a round face, pointed ears and whiskers across the yellow omelette. The result was recognisably a cat and happier than any cat Xuyuan had met.

The curry omelette rice went in front of Lulu. Yuankai’s parfait arrived in a tall glass, green and white beneath a crown of cream.

Before anyone picked up a spoon, the maid brought her hands together.

**Maid:** I need all our masters and princesses to help me cast the magic that makes everything delicious. Hands like this. Ready?

Xuyuan had been trying to find a socially neutral amount of participation. Lulu had already made the heart. Anna followed. Yuankai did the same with the untroubled precision of someone completing the next step in a procedure.

Xuyuan raised his hands.

**All:** Become delicious—*moe, moe, kyun!*

Four hearts moved towards the table. The maid thanked them in her ordinary voice and went to the next group.

Anna looked at Yuankai’s parfait.

**Anna:** Worth it?

Yuankai took a spoonful.

**Yuankai:** Yes.

For a while, the four of them ate.

**Xuyuan:** You chose the curry one.

**Lulu:** Yes.

**Xuyuan:** Do you prefer curry or rice?

The question became stupid as soon as he heard it outside his head. Curry was not an alternative to rice. It was currently resting on it.

**Xuyuan:** That was a stupid question.

**Lulu:** A little. But I know what you meant. I prefer curry.

**Xuyuan:** I prefer rice.

Lulu looked at him.

**Lulu:** Was that why you asked?

**Xuyuan:** Not entirely. I wanted to know which part made you choose it. Rice is usually treated as whatever happens underneath the answer. This rice is too wet. It hasn’t rested properly, and the grains have stopped being individual grains.

Lulu looked down at the rice beneath her curry.

**Lulu:** Mine tastes fine.

**Xuyuan:** It is the same rice.

**Lulu:** Then yours tastes fine too.

**Xuyuan:** I make better rice.

**Lulu:** I’m sure you do.

He used brown rice and long-grain rice together. The ratio depended on what had to go with it. The water mattered, and so did leaving the lid closed long enough after the heat went off. If Lulu tried it, she would understand that rice was not merely the substance beneath something more interesting.

The proposition arrived with a kitchen, an evening and Lulu seated at a table while he lifted the lid. It omitted the smaller problem that they had no arrangement to meet again.

**Lulu:** Your cat is getting cold.

Xuyuan looked down. One ketchup whisker had begun to run.

**Xuyuan:** Right.

The plates gradually emptied. Conversation loosened, then settled. Around them, greetings rose from the door and spoons struck glass.

Anna looked towards the paper blossoms above the counter.

**Anna:** Are those meant to be cherry blossoms?

**Lulu:** I think so.

Lulu followed her gaze for a moment, then turned back to Xuyuan.

**Lulu:** You walk to campus most days. Have you seen any real ones yet?

**Xuyuan:** Not properly. Some of them should be out by now.

**Lulu:** I haven’t seen them properly since I came here. I’d like to, before they’re gone.

**Xuyuan:** There are several places.

**Lulu:** If you find somewhere good, send it to me.

**Xuyuan:** I don’t have your number.

**Lulu:** Oh.

Lulu took out her phone.

**Lulu:** Do you use WhatsApp?

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

They exchanged numbers across the table. Her name appeared on his screen: Lulu.

When they had paid, the maid accompanied them to the door.

**Maid:** Safe travels, Masters and Princesses. Come home again soon.

Outside, the four of them stopped at the next corner.

**Anna:** We’re going this way.

Lulu lifted one hand.

**Lulu:** Send me what you find.

**Xuyuan:** I will.

She and Anna crossed the road. Yuankai began walking in the other direction. Xuyuan went with him.

## Location Research and Invitation

Over the next two evenings, Xuyuan reduced London to three browser tabs.

St James’s Park was the easiest to remove. It was small enough that a busy day might make the whole visit feel crowded. Regent’s Park remained possible. Kew survived every comparison, mostly because its different flowering areas made it less dependent on finding one particular row of trees at exactly the right time.

He sent Lulu the Kew page and the shortest version of his reasoning.

“I looked at St James’s Park and Regent’s Park as well. Kew looks like the most reliable option,” he wrote.

“Kew looks good,” Lulu replied.

That was an answer about the place. It was not yet an arrangement.

He typed the next sentence beneath it, read it twice, and sent it before a third reading could improve it into something else.

“Would you like to go there together?” he wrote.

Her reply arrived before he found a useful place to put the phone.

“Yes, I’d like to,” Lulu replied.

They compared schedules and agreed to meet at noon on Saturday. Lulu had something later that evening, but there was time to visit Kew first. They chose a central London station they both knew. The remaining messages concerned entrances and trains and caused no difficulty.

Once these were settled, Xuyuan returned to the Kew website to plan a route through the gardens. One of its suggested trails was called “First Date”.

He opened it. The page had not changed its name.

He closed it. There would be paper maps at Victoria Gate.

## Central London to Kew Gardens

Late on Saturday morning, the extra layer under Xuyuan’s trousers made no visible difference until he removed it. Without it, the difference was obvious. This was almost certainly not how comparisons worked. He left it off anyway.

He had allowed enough time for the journey. He had not allowed for changing twice and then checking whether warmth had a visible width.

By the time he left, being early required running.

*Scene: An unnamed central London station.*

He stopped running before the station entrance and walked the final stretch. His breathing did not recognise the distinction. The run had also left him too warm to regret anything he had removed.

He reached the station entrance four minutes before Lulu.

That was long enough for his breathing to slow and not long enough to decide how to begin.

She came up the pavement towards him.

**Lulu:** Did you run?

**Xuyuan:** Only the last part.

**Lulu:** Are we late?

**Xuyuan:** No. I left later than I meant to.

**Lulu:** Which platform?

**Xuyuan:** This way.

The train arrived when the board said it would. Nothing on the platform required them to change direction.

For the first few minutes, they confirmed the route, the stop and the walk from Kew Gardens station to Victoria Gate. Then the train continued, and none of those things required further discussion.

**Xuyuan:** Have you actually watched *K-ON!*?

**Lulu:** Yes. Have you?

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

There was a short pause.

**Xuyuan:** What else do you like?

**Lulu:** You first.

**Xuyuan:** *Steins;Gate* is my favourite.

**Lulu:** What’s second?

*Date A Live* arrived first and could not be allowed to remain there. One title would be enough to assemble the rest of the category around it: harem anime, male otaku, conclusions reached. *Neon Genesis Evangelion* was safer, except it was so common that saying it now looked like choosing a safe answer. The internet had also recently found a way to make liking it embarrassing. This should not have mattered. It did.

The pause grew long enough for Lulu to look back from the window.

**Xuyuan:** I have a problem with *Steins;Gate 0*.

Lulu waited.

**Xuyuan:** Future Luka appears as if the story has promoted him into a one-person special unit. He has a sword, he fights soldiers—it is ridiculous, and a very good entrance. Then he is shot almost immediately and dies. They made a whole version of the character and barely let him exist after introducing him.

**Lulu:** But you liked the entrance.

**Xuyuan:** Very much. That made wasting it worse.

Second place was now far enough behind him to be safe.

**Xuyuan:** If a story is going to use a future war, *Eighty-Six* has much more potential. It hardly looks high-tech at first, but the “unmanned warfare”, automated weapons and the boundary between a machine and what remains of a person give it somewhere to go.

**Lulu:** It’s hard to choose one overall, but *Porco Rosso* is my favourite war anime.

**Xuyuan:** I don’t know that one.

**Lulu:** Miyazaki.

Miyazaki did not narrow the field enough.

**Xuyuan:** Which one?

**Lulu:** The pilot is a pig.

**Xuyuan:** Oh. *Red Pig*.

**Lulu:** That’s the same title.

**Xuyuan:** Not to me until now. I only knew it in Chinese.

The train continued west. After it came above ground, brick walls gave way by degrees to roofs, back gardens and a larger share of sky. Neither of them resumed the list.

At Kew Gardens, both looked up at the announcement.

## Kew Gardens Station to Victoria Gate

*Scene: Kew Gardens station.*

Before they reached the stairs, Xuyuan saw a pale crown of blossom beyond the far end of the platform.

**Xuyuan:** I think that’s cherry blossom.

Lulu followed the direction of his hand.

**Lulu:** From the station?

**Xuyuan:** Apparently.

**Lulu:** That seems promising.

Outside the station, his phone gave them a short blue line to Victoria Gate. It led them through residential streets where the houses stood behind low walls and front gardens.

**Lulu:** The houses are nice too.

Xuyuan looked up from the route.

**Xuyuan:** They are.

On one of them, a vine had climbed past the ground floor and spread across the brick beneath the upper windows.

Its oldest stems were thick enough to look less attached to the wall than built into it.

Lulu slowed. Xuyuan took two more steps before noticing and came back. He lowered the phone.

They stayed there for a moment, then continued towards the gate.

*Scene: Victoria Gate.*

They passed through the entrance, and the view opened.

*Visual: Grass fills the bottom of the frame, with tiny white flowers barely visible across it. An asphalt path runs diagonally from the lower left towards the upper right. At the left edge, only a corner of the pond is visible. Beyond it, roughly five-sixths of the Palm House is in frame, seen from the side. A large old tree with many dividing branches carries strong visual weight in the middle ground on the right, its crown extending beyond the top of the frame.*

The Palm House stood beyond the path, its long glass body seen from the side.

*Visual: The image holds.*

## Palm House and Great Pagoda

A rack near the entrance held folded maps. Xuyuan took one and opened the first two panels.

The map marked Cherry Hotspots and Magnolia Hotspots across the gardens. The Palm House required no symbol. It was still in front of them.

For the moment, the paper and the garden agreed.

*Scene: Inside the Palm House.*

The second set of doors admitted them into heat and damp air. Lulu took off her coat and folded it over one arm.

**Xuyuan:** Do you want me to carry that?

**Lulu:** No, it’s all right.

They followed the path beneath overlapping leaves. Condensation gathered on the glass above them. Names on small labels accumulated faster than either of them could retain them, and after a while they stopped trying to read every one.

Outside again, Lulu put on her coat. Xuyuan opened the map.

The Great Pagoda stood near the far edge of the gardens, but its upper tiers still showed above the trees. The Temperate House lay between it and them, surrounded on the map by several of the coloured marks they had come to find.

*Visual: A straight path approaches the Temperate House between flowering trees. Pink branches crowd both sides of the frame and cross in front of the glasshouse beyond. Steps rise to its central entrance.*

They slowed before reaching the steps.

**Lulu:** There are more than I expected.

**Xuyuan:** This whole part is marked as a hotspot.

She looked from one side of the path to the other.

**Xuyuan:** The Pagoda is the furthest point. We could go there first and come back to this.

**Lulu:** Then we get to see these twice.

They continued towards the tower.

They kept the Great Pagoda in sight as they crossed the gardens. Close to the base, they had to lean back to see the painted animals along the roofs.

**Lulu:** That one’s head looks a little like a dog.

**Xuyuan:** The body looks like a smooth green caterpillar.

**Lulu:** With very long feathered wings.

Xuyuan looked at the creature’s thick forelegs and claws.

**Xuyuan:** And arms.

Lulu laughed. They walked around the base to inspect it from another angle. The description survived, and they continued around to compare the others.

When they finally moved away from the tower, three paths looked equally capable of being the one they had arrived by.

Xuyuan unfolded the map to its full size. The Pagoda was easy to find on paper. Their own position beside it was also easy. The direction in which they were facing was not.

He turned the sheet once, then again, trying to make the angles of the paths agree with the ground. When he flattened the last fold, a north arrow appeared in the lower-right corner. Until then, it had been hidden. The top of the sheet represented a direction roughly sixty degrees west of north.

A game map would have supplied a moving position and the direction of his face. The paper knew the first only in the abstract and regarded the second as his problem.

Lulu leaned closer to look.

Beyond the trees, a strip of glass caught the light.

**Lulu:** Is that the Temperate House?

Xuyuan compared it with the page.

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

She pointed to the broadest of the three paths.

**Lulu:** Then shouldn’t we go that way?

He turned the map to match the glass roof.

**Xuyuan:** You’re right.

They were not lost for long. The Pagoda had still been farther from the glasshouse than it looked, and circling its base had done nothing to shorten the return.

## Temperate House

They approached the flowering path from the other direction.

**Lulu:** We did get to see them twice.

The branches shifted above her in the breeze.

*Scene: Inside the Temperate House.*

The air was gentler than in the Palm House, but warmer than outside, with the smell of damp soil in it. Lulu slipped off her coat. Xuyuan was about to offer again; she had already folded it over one arm and gone to look at the first bank of plants.

The damp air made Xuyuan nostalgic, though not for home exactly. It belonged to flower markets, tropical glasshouses and a few paths through forest parks: places visited rarely enough that the memory had kept their climate before anything else.

Water had been running somewhere nearby. He had heard it for several minutes without separating it from the leaves, the soil or the air.

Lulu stopped. This time he stopped with her.

She looked between the leaves, and he followed her attention.

*Visual: The image from the cold open returns. Leaves conceal most of a little rockery; water slips from one stone ledge to the next.*

Until she stopped, somewhere in his mind the water had belonged to the air.

Lulu raised her phone.

*Audio: A phone camera clicks.*

When they left the Temperate House, she put her coat on again. The outdoor air cooled the damp at Xuyuan’s collar before they had reached the end of the steps.

The extra layer under his trousers would not have shown. This remained true and became less important.

## The Lake and the Return

The path brought the Treetop Walkway into view above them.

**Xuyuan:** Do you want to go up?

Lulu looked at the stairs and then at the walkway between the trees.

**Lulu:** No. I’m afraid of heights.

**Xuyuan:** We can leave it.

At the lake, a duck dipped its head under the water to feed. For several seconds, only its upright tail and orange feet remained above the surface, the feet paddling at the air. Xuyuan and Lulu stopped at almost the same moment.

**Lulu:** That’s very cute.

A second duck farther along tipped up in the same way. Lulu laughed.

The folded map stayed in Xuyuan’s hand. Neither of them reached for another subject. When the ducks drifted towards the reeds, they continued along the water.

On their way back, they came to a squat sculpture shaped like a mushroom with a wedge missing from its cap. A panel beside it explained what the shape had to do with carbon. They read the panel together.

Halfway through it, Xuyuan yawned. He covered his mouth late enough for the gesture to become retrospective.

Lulu smiled.

He had not intended the yawn to review the afternoon on his behalf.

**Xuyuan:** Sorry.

**Lulu:** It’s all right.

They walked on.

The map was no longer needed. The Palm House appeared again between the trees, and the fold containing Victoria Gate fitted beneath Xuyuan’s thumb. Sunlight reached them at a lower angle now, catching the upper branches while longer shadows crossed the grass.

Behind the Palm House, a path curved between clipped dark hedges and flowering trees. A bench stood on the grass beneath one of the trees.

When the bench came into view, they slowed together.

**Lulu:** Shall we sit down for a while?

**Xuyuan:** Yes.

## Beneath the Cherry Trees

*Visual: A pale path curves away between tall, rounded hedges. Cherry trees frame both sides and spread across the upper part of the image. Low sunlight enters from the left and throws branch shadows over the path. On the right, a wooden bench stands beneath a flowering tree.*

Lulu sat first and let out a small breath as she settled against the backrest. Xuyuan folded the map until the gardens became a rectangle again and sat beside her.

At first, sitting was only relief. The warmth of walking remained in his legs, then did not. The layer he had left at home ceased to be theoretical.

A petal landed on his sleeve. He left it there.

For a while they watched people pass between the trees.

**Xuyuan:** I wish I could stay another year.

Lulu looked up through the branches.

**Lulu:** Mm. London is nice.

**Xuyuan:** It is.

A large bee moved among the cherry blossoms above them, vanished behind one cluster and emerged from another.

**Lulu:** That’s a bumblebee.

**Xuyuan:** Are you sure?

**Lulu:** Yes.

They watched until it left the tree and disappeared into the flowers beside the path.

Lulu sat with her hands together in her lap and watched the light move through the branches.

That should have made it easier. It gave him time. Time kept producing more time.

He could say it before she looked back, but then he would be saying it to the side of her face. He could wait until she turned towards him.

She did.

The sentence went away.

A couple came along the path. Xuyuan waited until their voices had passed behind the hedges. The path was empty again.

*There’s something I wanted to tell you* would only postpone the thing itself by one sentence. The thing itself remained where it had been.

The petal was still on his sleeve.

Beyond the bench, the path returned to Victoria Gate. After the gate would come the train. Sunlight withdrew from another strip of the bench. Cold tightened across his shoulders, and he held them still.

He had already tried the version without her in it. Another year. London is nice.

The next version would have to contain her.

**Xuyuan:** Lulu.

She turned towards him.

The first shiver reached his shoulders before the next word.

He could wait for it to pass. Then he would be waiting again.

There were still safer ways to begin: how little time they had known each other, how soon his course would end, where he might be afterwards. Any of them could postpone her by another sentence.

**Xuyuan:** I like you.

The sentence was smaller than everything he had arranged behind it.

**Xuyuan:** I know we haven’t known each other very long. My course ends soon, and I might be leaving London after that.

Another shiver crossed his shoulders. He was visibly trembling through a confession. It looked ridiculous. The fact that he was actually cold did not make it look any better.

**Xuyuan:** But I still wanted to tell you.

A branch shadow moved across the bench.

**Lulu:** I’m sorry.

**Lulu:** I’m already seeing someone.

Xuyuan nodded once.

**Xuyuan:** Right.

He looked down at the folded map in his hands.

**Xuyuan:** I don’t know when it started. Every time we met again, I had this—*doki-doki* feeling.

Xuyuan gave a short laugh. Lulu laughed too, briefly.

**Lulu:** I didn’t know.

His shoulders shook again. Lulu looked at him.

**Xuyuan:** I should have worn another layer.

**Lulu:** You should have said.

Above them, the cherry branches shifted in the wind.

For a while, neither of them spoke.

People passed along the path. Footsteps approached, crossed the open stretch between the hedges and faded. A strip of sunlight moved off the end of the bench.

Xuyuan looked at the time on his phone.

**Xuyuan:** Shall we go?

**Lulu:** Yes.

They stood.

*Cut: The return train.*

The carriage was busier now. Conversations overlapped beneath the noise of the wheels. The daylight outside the windows had thinned.

Xuyuan said something as the train entered a tunnel. Lulu turned towards him.

**Lulu:** Sorry?

**Xuyuan:** I said thank you for coming today.

**Lulu:** I really enjoyed seeing the cherry blossoms today.

The windows reflected the carriage.
