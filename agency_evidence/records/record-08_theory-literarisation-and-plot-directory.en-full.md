## Forward Comparison with the Harness Revision

This session does not prove that the new Harness was "effective" or "ineffective". It shows that it changed the granularity of agency movement.

- Positive changes: the Agent first only listed filenames and stopped; the author actually corrected classification; each batch only read fixed scope, discussed it, and then waited for write authorisation; unselected directions remained inactive; it did not pad the count to two or three candidates; most batches did not continue sliding into Draft.
- Still present: inside each batch, the Agent controlled strongest-direction selection, conceptual connection, input-output ranking and module wording; complete and attractive explanations often became the author's main visible options. One batch could still contain multi-layer structure across characters, scenes, time and ethics.
- New risks: minimal loading can leave the Agent unaware of how much candidate material has already entered the primary Draft; the author must correct this from memory. Agent-designed Prompts and relevance maps shape later choices strongly even when they do not become canon.
- Exception mechanism: the author can explicitly authorise crossing the original session boundary. The whole-work compression link in this session was actively initiated by the author under deadline; it cannot simply be described as the Agent failing to obey the Harness, but it should be recorded as a case of human time pressure producing a larger delegation of agency.

## Synthesis

The overall pattern of this session was: the author sets boundaries and provides high-value seeds; the Agent reads large amounts of semantic material and generates structured syntheses; the author confirms, negates or redirects through short feedback; the Agent routes and persists. Compared with an earlier open long turn, this significantly increased visible stopping points, candidate status and opportunities for withdrawal. Compared with fine-grained line-by-line co-writing, the Agent still handled most problem construction and relation-explication.

The most creative results — theoretical letter, the impossibility of lossless spectatorship, rerouting consequences inside a singularity, the mirror between micro-level care and worldline curation, and the replacement of a too-late meeting by an earlier request — all depended on the Agent's ability to connect scattered materials. The author did not passively agree: he repeatedly refuted minimal theory explanation, withdrew the relationship module, protected the train as an aesthetic asset, removed the premise that the terminal AI was despairing, and proposed the orbital mission and A/B compression seed. But because the Agent's outputs were often already structurally complete, the author's frequent short confirmations also meant that many alternative connections were never proposed.

From the angle of agency negotiation, the most important conclusion is not "whether the Agent decided the story", but that the location of control moved. The author retained goals, values, key facts, vetoes and write authorisation; the Agent acquired strong control over source curation, invention of literary relations, difficulty assessment, file architecture, next questions and the cold-start environment. The new Harness cut this power into reviewable batches, but it did not, and should not pretend to, replace the author's actual review. The stronger the deadline, the more likely the author is to treat "excellent and sufficient" as the stopping standard, and to personally authorise crossing scale boundaries that were originally established to protect agency.

## Analysis of the Author's Post-Audit Response

### "Theoretical letter" needs to be split again into the author's latent claim, the Agent's metaphor and the author's unpreserved constraint

The original Record attributed "a theoretical letter sent to a reader who may not exist" to a new literary relation produced by the Agent. That attribution needs refinement. The author now explains that the theoretical submission, as a claim addressed to a special audience, was already his not-yet-fully-stated intention; the Agent's contribution was mainly to make it explicit and compress it into the strong metaphor of "letter / recipient". The author did not claim that the work was truly a letter, and did not want to use second person; the audience below the stage, the text's second-person position and the Grand Curator appearing in third person inside the work are different address layers, and the most important audience remains the third-person `the Grand Curator`.

A more accurate co-creative attribution is therefore: the author supplied the latent relation between theoretical text and special audience, and the third-person curator direction; the Agent made the relation explicit and gave it the emotional structure of a "letter"; the author limited second person on the spot. More important, the author reports that when actual drafting later began, the Agent forgot this limitation. This later result does not change the fact that sequence 37 in the fixed trace did include correction, but it shows that a boundary written into an inactive module did not stably migrate into the Draft stage. The Agent's striking "letter / recipient" metaphor survived across sessions more easily than the author's weaker-form "not necessarily second person". This formed a salience asymmetry: the strong metaphor was remembered, while the small constraint was lost.

### Candidate is not only a low-authority holding area; it is also active memory for thought provenance and local facts

Original line 53 already stated that candidate is not an "influence-free wastepaper basket". ### Overall calibration after the response

This author response makes the core conclusion of the Record more asymmetrical. The problem should not be summarised as "the Agent did too much".
