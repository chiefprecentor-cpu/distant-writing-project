# Workshop Dialogue and Comedy

Treat dialogue and comedy as character action inside a particular scene, not as a general naturalness clean-up.

For the current distant-writing project, read the shared [aesthetic profile](../interpret-co-writing-signals/references/aesthetic-profile.md).

This skill owns dialogue- and comedy-local problems. When an explicit AI-fingerprint concern extends beyond the conversation into narration, epistemic shortcuts, or the scene's dramatic scaffold, use [rework-ai-shaped-fiction](../rework-ai-shaped-fiction/SKILL.md) rather than stretching a dialogue clean-up across the whole draft.

When the request spans a long scene or draft, read that scope and identify patterns before selecting examples. Look for repeated sentence shapes, turn-taking habits, compressed or missing social transitions, implied history created by pronouns or reactions, clusters of jokes, and shifts in who is allowed to occupy language. Work from a preselected line when the user's request is local.

Questions that may open useful options:

- Why does this person say this now, and what response are they seeking?
- Does a question arise from the speaker's own knowledge, desire, or uncertainty, or mainly cue the next piece of exposition for the reader?
- Does the next line answer the conversational move or only deliver information to the reader?
- What unintended character history, threshold, intimacy, or competence does the wording imply?
- Would longer ownership of a turn reveal thought in motion, crystallise a persuasive self-account, or sound more settled than the character's thinking at this moment?
- Is an awkward, technical, translated, or overly precise phrase a defect, a character strategy, or both?
- Is the joke produced by situation, wording, reference recognition, group membership, delayed understanding, or a collision of character logics?
- How often have recent jokes asked the reader to possess outside knowledge, and what happens emotionally if they do not?

A reference need not work for every reader. Compare possible degrees of openness without ranking them automatically: self-contained, context-supported, informed-reader only, deliberately private, or delayed until later recognition. Consider local frequency and atmosphere rather than applying an accessibility gate to every joke.

Return the high-value observations the material supports, alternative phrasings or beat shapes when useful, and a recommendation whose trade-off is explicit. If authorised to edit, preserve voice asymmetry and avoid normalising every line to concise contemporary speech.
