# Draft a VN Scene from Design

Turn selected planning material into an experienced scene rather than a performed summary of the planning. Preserve what the design constrains while letting prose, dialogue, action, and presentation discover their own local movement.

For the current distant-writing project, read the shared [aesthetic profile](../interpret-co-writing-signals/references/aesthetic-profile.md). It supplies provisional calibration, not a prose template.

## Fix the authorised scene

Read the current draft junction, the sources the author identifies, and only the directly relevant confirmed commitments. Distinguish what must remain true from active design, open candidates, and unresolved details. Permission to draft a selected direction does not silently confirm every source idea or authorise unrelated state changes.

Establish the scene's actual entry, stopping point, and protected prior text. Do not continue into the next consequential scene merely because its setup is available. If mixed signals in the request affect what has been selected or authorised, consult [interpret-co-writing-signals](../interpret-co-writing-signals/SKILL.md) before drafting.

## Convert design into movement

Before writing, form a working account of the participants' situated purposes, what changes by the stopping point, what each can perceive or know, and what the player needs in order to follow the present action. Keep this account internal unless showing it would help the author decide an unresolved direction.

Treat the design as a field of constraints and possibilities, not a delivery checklist:

- Decide which facts must become legible now, which may first appear as pressure or clue, which belong later, and which need never be stated.
- Give important information a carrier suited to the scene: a person's chosen words, an object or document, bodily attention, spatial relation, interruption, mistake, silence, visual change, sound, or a later consequence.
- Preserve a usable path from perception and prior knowledge to inference. Intelligence or intuition may compress this path; it should not give a character access to the outline.
- Let dialogue arise from what each person wants to obtain, avoid, protect, test, or share. A character need not ask the reader's next question or supply the complete answer.
- Check whether a familiar briefing, recruitment, confession, lesson, or confrontation scaffold is assigning roles by default. Keep it when chosen; otherwise recover the more particular prior relation each participant has to the problem.
- Leave a bridge unresolved when the available scene does not yet earn it. A clean transition is not worth flattening motivation or inventing ordinary connective material.

High-concept, technical, or philosophical material may remain dense. Its literary form can be a character thinking in motion, two incompatible uses of the same term, a practical consequence, an incomplete model, or pressure distributed across later scenes; it need not be simplified into generic short dialogue.

## Use VN channels proportionally

Keep spoken text, player-visible narration or thought, and production-facing visual or audio directions distinguishable to the degree the project requires. Speaker labels, sprites, compositions, and cuts can reveal knowledge before the prose does; use them deliberately.

Respect the production scope or asset budget stated for the scene. A stable background or composition can carry substantial movement through text, sound, timing, and the player's changing understanding. Do not invent screen-on／screen-off background variants, small-action CGs, or continuous sprite churn merely to prove that the scene is visual. Conversely, do not suppress a presentation change when the intended effect genuinely depends on it and the author has left room for it.

When a stronger existing passage would materially help, compare how it assigns work across channels and how its characters acquire meaning. Do not copy its sentence length, quietness, number of assets, or surface mannerisms into a scene with different pressure.

## Consult craft guidance as needed

Load specialist guidance whenever it has a clear connection to a live drafting problem, not only when it is indispensable or certain to change the draft. Several lenses may be useful at once when dialogue, inference, rhythm, and AI-shaped regularity interact. If two closely relevant lenses both bear on the scene, read both and reconcile their guidance rather than forcing an early single-skill choice:

- [explore-subtext-and-distance](../explore-subtext-and-distance/SKILL.md) when interpretation timing, viewpoint ownership, or intentional ambiguity controls the scene;
- [workshop-dialogue-and-comedy](../workshop-dialogue-and-comedy/SKILL.md) when conversational action, voice, comedy, or reference openness is central;
- [shape-vn-rhythm-and-montage](../shape-vn-rhythm-and-montage/SKILL.md) when silence, repetition, cuts, or channel density determines the movement;
- [rework-ai-shaped-fiction](../rework-ai-shaped-fiction/SKILL.md) when the emerging draft starts to perform the design too regularly, gives characters outline knowledge, or settles into a generic scaffold.

Do not load every sibling skill automatically. Load generously within the live scene problem, stop when further guidance would only restate what is already available, and return from each specialist lens to the concrete scene being written.

## Draft and reread

Write in the project's required language and format, and modify the primary draft only when implementation is authorised. Preserve protected wording and do not create a second prose source.

Reread the drafted scope continuously rather than only checking individual sentences. Check that something actually moves, the information belongs to its carriers, the characters have not become explanation interfaces, the stopping point remains intact, and presentation demands stay within the stated production scope.

Report the completed span, unresolved choices or placeholders, any new semantic consequence, and any designed information deliberately deferred or left unstated. A first pass may remain a first pass; do not declare it final merely because every source constraint has been represented.
